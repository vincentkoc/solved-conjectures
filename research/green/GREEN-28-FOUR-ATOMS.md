# Green 28 with at most four atoms per marginal

Let $P,Q$ be independent integer-valued laws, each taking at most four values. If $P+Q$ is uniform on its support, both $P$ and $Q$ are uniform on their supports. The positions may be arbitrarily far apart, and their probabilities may be arbitrary positive reals. Any counterexample to Green 28 must therefore have at least five atoms in one marginal. [Lean theorem](Green28FourAtomLaws.lean) · [Pinned conjecture](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/28.lean)

The precise theorem is `Green28FourAtomLaws.uniform_of_at_most_four_atoms`. It takes the original hypothesis `Green28.IsUniformOnSupport (Green28.indepSum P Q)` and the two bounds `P.support.ncard ≤ 4`, `Q.support.ncard ≤ 4`. Uniformity of the sum already gives finiteness of both supports. The conclusion is exactly the conjunction of marginal uniformities in the pinned declaration. [Support finiteness and rational-ratio theorem](Green28UniformSums.lean)

## Why singleton sums force flat weights

Order the left and right sites as $a_i,b_j$ with respective positive masses $f_i,g_j$. Write $C$ for the mass of any occupied sum. Each cell $(i,j)$ contributes $f_i g_j\le C$ at $a_i+b_j$. Equality holds exactly when the sum has a unique representation, because every other representation contributes a positive mass. At the smallest sum, $f_0g_0=C$. Comparing all cells in its row and column gives $g_j\le g_0$ and $f_i\le f_0$. Hence a cell is a singleton precisely when both its row and column weights are maximal. The largest sum is also a singleton, so the two end sites on each side have maximal mass. [Positive-weight argument](Green28FourAtomLaws.lean)

The singleton cells thus form the rectangle of maximal rows and maximal columns. For ordered tables of size at most four by four, one entire side must be maximal. In a four-by-four table with a deficient weight on each side, each pair of interior positions has three possible nonempty sets of deficient positions. This gives nine cases. A second representation of a given cell must move to a smaller row and a larger column, or to a larger row and a smaller column. A deficient cell needs a second representation from outside the singleton rectangle. For example, when each side has just one deficient site, their intersection has no possible partner, since another deficient cell would share its row or its column. The other cases give finite disjunctions of integer linear equations between the site positions, which Lean discharges using strict order. Shorter tables use the same argument, with one- and two-site sides covered by their end sites. [Grid classification](Green28FourAtoms.lean)

A flat marginal has only zero or one as ratios of masses to a positive mass. The previously checked rational-ratio theorem then gives uniformity of the other marginal. This uses monic division and integrality of coefficients, so the final step also covers irrational input masses. [Arithmetic of a uniform sum](GREEN-28-ARITHMETIC.md)

## Prior work and verification

Hare's coefficient-pattern method uses the classes zero, one, and strictly between zero and one, including the rule that a unit contribution saturates its sum. The condition on the numbers of atoms here permits arbitrarily large polynomial degrees after translation. Earlier computational bounds use degree as a parameter. A November 2026 manuscript directed by Alessandro Baravelli gives a separate result when one factor has three nonzero terms and the other is unrestricted. The four-by-four case here allows four nonzero terms on both sides. [Hare, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07363) · [MathOverflow problem and computations](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/339137/) · [Trinomial manuscript, Theorem 1](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00674v2)

The DeepMind issue and statement PR, Left to Solve, and Open Math Agents' degree and reflection reductions were checked. The smaller-degree bound of four in the Green 28 reduction concerns the span of the exponents. Searches found no matching four-atoms-per-side theorem. The claim here is the checked restricted result. The unrestricted question remains open.
