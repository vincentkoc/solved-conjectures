# Green 28 with a fixed budget on one marginal

For a fixed $m$, consider the original integer question for a left marginal with at most $m$ atoms and **no size or diameter restriction on the right marginal**. This is equivalent to the following prime cyclic tests. For each prime $p$, take a left law with at most $m$ atoms and a right law with $n$ atoms satisfying $(2n)^{m-1}<p$. Whenever their independent sum is uniform on its support, both laws must be uniform. Thus the integer four-site question is equivalent to testing right supports of growing size $2n<\sqrt[3]{p}$ on prime cycles. This is a reduction. The integer four-site question with an unrestricted opposite law remains open. [Lean theorem `oneSided_iff_primes`](Green28AsymmetricSupports.lean) · [Pinned Green 28](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/28.lean)

Two conclusions hold unconditionally. In the asymmetric range $(2n)^{|A|-1}<p$, rational ratios between all positive masses on **either** side force both cyclic marginals to be uniform whenever the sum is uniform. In particular, a counterexample with such ratios and at most four atoms on the left would need $(2n)^3\ge p$. With at most four atoms on **each** side, arbitrary real probabilities are allowed for every prime $p>512$. The earlier sufficient cutoff was $15{,}625$. The least sufficient cutoff is undetermined. ## Where the cubic bound comes from

Let $A,B$ be the supports on a prime cycle, $n=|B|$, $L=2n$, and $h=\lfloor(p-1)/L\rfloor$. Choose $a_0\in A$. Partition the representatives $0,\ldots,p-1$ into at most $L$ boxes of width $h+1$. If $L^{|A|-1}<p$, two multipliers give the same box for each of the $|A|-1$ nonzero differences from $a_0$. Their difference is a nonzero multiplier $q$. Every $q(a-a_0)$ now has a signed representative in $[-h,h]$. [`narrow`](Green28AsymmetricSupports.lean)

A cut at $t$ is forbidden if $qb+t$ has least representative below $2h$ for some $b\in B$. At most $2hn\le p-1$ cuts are forbidden, so a valid cut exists. Translate the signed left representatives by $h$ into $[0,2h]$. On the right take the least representatives of $qb+t$ and subtract $2h$. Then every occupied pair has integer sum in $[0,p-1]$. This sum represents $q(a+b-a_0)+t-h$. Since this affine transformation is injective on the entire cycle, the two relabelled pairs have equal integer sums exactly when their original cyclic sums agree. All three atom masses are preserved. This proof requires neither a bound on the gaps of $B$ nor a bound on the total number of sum outcomes. [`avoid` and `lift`](Green28AsymmetricSupports.lean) · [Mass-preserving transport](Green28CyclicLaws.lean)

For the reverse implication, start with any integer laws having a uniform sum. Uniformity gives finite support for both marginals, as required in the pinned proposition. Compress the supports and their sums into the known bounded integer interval, then choose a prime larger than both its upper endpoint and $(2n)^{m-1}$. Reduction is injective on all occupied sites. The proof keeps the original marginal cardinalities through both transports. [Integer compression](GREEN-28-FINITE-TEST.md)

## Sources and verification

Candela, González-Sánchez and Grynkiewicz describe rectifiability of a pair of sets via containment in two progressions of the same difference whose lengths sum to at most $p+1$. This proof obtains that containment by compressing one side and counting forbidden cuts on the other. Small-set rectification itself is classical. Searches of the DeepMind issue and PRs, Left to Solve and Open Math Agents found the integer polynomial reformulations and degree reductions. The contribution here is the checked asymmetric range, equivalence and cyclic consequences for the original probability laws.
