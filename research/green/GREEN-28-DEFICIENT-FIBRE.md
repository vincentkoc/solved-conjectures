# Green 28 with one atom below maximum mass

Let the independent integer laws $P,Q$ have a sum uniform on its support. Suppose at most one occupied site of $Q$ has mass below its largest atom probability. **If the below-maximum positive masses of $P$ are pairwise rational multiples of one another, both laws are uniform.** In particular $P$ may have at most two distinct positive mass values, with any number of atoms at either value. There are no bounds on the support sizes, diameters or gaps. ## A subset and an integer span

In a hypothetical nonuniform decomposition, normalize each marginal by its largest point probability, attained at both endpoints. Write $d$ for the unique deficient site of $Q$, $u$ for its normalized weight, and $D$ for the set of distinct normalized deficient weights of $P$. Two necessary conditions hold.

- There is a nonempty set of **distinct sites** of $P$, all deficient, whose normalized weights sum to one. Different sites can carry the same weight. The theorem finds a single occupied sum for which every representation uses one of these rows and a maximum-weight column.
- The additive group of integer linear combinations of $D$ equals $\mathbb Z[u]$ as a subset of the reals. Here $\mathbb Z[u]$ consists of all integer polynomials evaluated at $u$. The normalized coefficient $u$ is an algebraic integer. The statement concerns this order specifically. [`subset_mass_of_one_deficit` and `deficient_integer_span`](Green28DeficientFibre.lean) · [Integrality of normalized coefficients](Green28PolynomialReduction.lean)

For the first criterion in the introduction, choose one participating mass $w$. Under commensurability, the subset sum says $1=cw$ for some positive rational $c$. Every occupied normalized mass strictly between zero and one is irrational, so this is impossible. [Endpoint arithmetic](Green28UniformSums.lean)

## Locating the fibre

Write $A,B$ for the supports, $L$ for the deficient rows in $A$, and $C=\{j-d:j\in B,\ j\ne d\}$. A cell gives a unique sum precisely when it pairs two maximum-weight atoms. Thus each $x\in L$ has a predecessor $z\in L$ with $x=z+t$, $t\in C$, by considering the second representation of $x+d$. For a maximum-weight row $x$, each $x+t$ lies outside $A$, since otherwise $x+j=(x+t)+d$ would violate uniqueness. [Singleton rectangle](Green28UnpairedSites.lean)

Some deficient row also has a step leaving $A$. Otherwise take a positive $c\in C$, which exists because the right endpoint of $B$ exceeds $d$. If $z,z+t\in L$ for an allowed $t$, then $z+c$ must be deficient. Indeed $z+c\in A$ by the assumption on deficient rows, and $(z+c)+t=(z+t)+c\in A$ rules out a maximum-weight row. Pulling a $c$-progression in $L$ back through a predecessor extends its length by one. Finiteness of $L$ rules this out. For an escaping step $x+(j-d)\notin A$, the sum $x+j$ has no representation using column $d$, and all its representations have deficient rows by the singleton rectangle. Summing the probabilities on this fibre gives the asserted subset. [Finite-set lemmas](Green28DeficientFibre.lean)

## Recovering the order

Let $T$ be the additive span of $D$. The subset gives $1\in T$. If row $y$ has normalized weight $w_y$, the fibre $y+d$ contributes $uw_y$ at row $y$. Every other occupied term uses a maximum-weight column. Each such row is deficient, since the term at $y$ is positive and the total is one. Subtraction gives $uw_y\in T$. Multiplication by $u$ therefore preserves $T$, and $1\in T$ gives $\mathbb Z[u]\subseteq T$. Conversely the normalized polynomial for $Q$ is monic with every coefficient in $\{0,1,u\}$. Monic division of the binary product over $\mathbb Z[u]$ recovers every normalized coefficient of $P$ in this ring. ## Scope and verification

The earlier one-deficient-atom bound compares support widths and bounds the algebraic degree by the total number of opposite mass levels. The two-mass-level result has separate cardinality or support hypotheses. Ghidelli studies a fixed trinomial, Hare examines coefficient patterns of bounded degree, Hasanbegović treats reflected supports and gaps, and the trinomial manuscript treats three nonzero terms with arbitrary exponents. The present criterion permits arbitrarily many maximal atoms on the one-deficit side. The unrestricted Green 28 assertion remains open.
