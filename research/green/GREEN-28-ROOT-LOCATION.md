# Green 28 and the roots of the output mask

Let $H=\sum_{s\in S}X^{s-\min S}$ be the mask of the support of a uniform independent integer sum. Count complex roots **with multiplicity** in the closed upper half-plane, including the real axis. If $H$ has at most one such root strictly inside the unit circle, or at most one strictly outside, both original marginal laws are uniform. Roots on the circle are unrestricted. A double real root contributes two to its region. Each nonreal conjugate pair contributes its upper member. [`uniform_of_root_count`](Green28RootLocation.lean) · [Pinned Green 28](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/28.lean)

## An integer certificate

It suffices to exhibit $m>0$, $e\ge0$ and a nonzero integer polynomial $R$ of degree at most three such that
\[
H\mid R(X)(X^m-1)^e\quad\text{in }\mathbb Z[X].
\]
Only the roots of $R$ can contribute to the two strict radial counts. Their counts sum to at most three, so one is at most one. The certificate allows cyclotomic factors of arbitrary degree and repeated factors. Neither the mask nor either marginal support needs to be palindromic. [`uniform_of_cubic_residual`](Green28RootLocation.lean)

For example, take $S=\{0,1,3,k,k+1,k+3\}$ for any $k\ge4$. Its mask is $(1+X+X^3)(1+X^k)$, which divides $(1+X+X^3)(X^{2k}-1)$. This support is nonpalindromic and its uniform law is the sum of the independent uniforms on $\{0,1,3\}$ and $\{0,k\}$. The theorem forces fairness for **every** independent decomposition of that uniform law. The same two-block argument applies to every nonzero common mask of degree at most three when the product is an output mask. [`uniform_of_two_cubic_blocks`](Green28RootLocation.lean)

## Why a counterexample needs four roots

The normalized real marginal polynomials $f,g$ are monic with constant term one, have nonnegative coefficients, and satisfy $fg=H$. For either factor the absolute values of the complex roots multiply to one. If all roots of $f$ have modulus one, inversion on roots agrees with complex conjugation. Reversing the linear factorization gives $f^*=f$. Thus a nonreciprocal real factor has a root on each side of the circle, and conjugating if necessary gives witnesses in the closed upper half-plane. [Root-product and reversal lemmas](Green28RootLocation.lean) · [Normalization of the pinned probability laws](Green28PrimitiveSupport.lean)

If one normalized factor is reciprocal, put $d=\deg f$. The coefficient of $X^d$ in $fg$ already receives $f_dg_0=1$. For $i>0$, positive $f_i$ and $g_i$ would contribute a second positive term $f_{d-i}g_i$, since $f_{d-i}=f_i$. So the two factors have disjoint positive-degree supports. Induction on the coefficient equation, followed by the rational-factor criterion, makes both factors binary. The argument uses the palindromic-support obstruction of Hasanbegović, Theorem 3. The new formal criteria concern root counts of the **output** and apply to asymmetric masks. The palindromic-output result is classical and has an existing Lean formalization by TheSil.
