# Green 28 and the number of sum outcomes

Suppose an independent integer sum is uniform on its $M$ possible outcomes. Let $Q$ have $q$ occupied sites and at most one site below maximum probability, and let $P$ be its partner. If either marginal fails to be uniform and $P$ takes at most three distinct positive probability values, then some integer $k$ satisfies
\[
  2\le k,\qquad 2k+1\le q,\qquad q(q-k-1)+1\mid M.
\]
For $q=5$ this requires $11\mid M$, and for $q=6$ it requires $19\mid M$. If $M$ divides a power of $q$, both marginals must be uniform. This includes a power-of-two output size whenever $q$ is even. For fixed $q>0$, among the first $N$ positive integers **at most $\lfloor N/q\rfloor$** can occur as the output size of any counterexample in this class. This bound ranges over all supports, widths, probabilities and allowed $k$ at that $q$. ## The unrestricted partner

Normalize each marginal by its largest probability. Write $u$ for the unique deficient weight of $Q$ in a nonuniform example, and set $x=1-u$. With any partner $P$, the monic integral minimal polynomial $m_x$ satisfies $m_x(q)\mid M$. To see this, let $v$ be the sum of the normalized weights of $P$. These weights belong to $\mathbb Z[u]=\mathbb Z[x]$ by the deficient-fibre theorem. The sum of the normalized weights of $Q$ is $q-x$. At the largest output site only the two rightmost atoms contribute, so the product of their probabilities is $1/M$. Since each law has total probability one, this gives $(q-x)v=M$. Express $v=p(x)$ with $p\in\mathbb Z[X]$. Then $m_x(X)$ divides $(q-X)p(X)-M$. Evaluation at $q$ gives the integer divisibility assertion. [`specialize_linear` and `outcome_count`](Green28NormOutcomes.lean) · [Deficient mass span](GREEN-28-DEFICIENT-FIBRE.md)

With at most three positive partner weights, the previously checked classification gives $x^2-(k+1)x+1=0$, $0<x<1$, and at least $k$ occupied sites on each side of the exceptional site. The monic quadratic is the minimal polynomial since $x$ is an algebraic integer strictly between zero and one. Its value at $q$ is the displayed divisor. This integer exceeds one and is coprime to $q$, proving the exclusion for divisors of powers of $q$. [Three-level weight classification](GREEN-28-THREE-LEVEL-PARTNER.md) · [`three_level_count` and `uniform_of_card_dvd_power`](Green28NormOutcomes.lean)

For the finite count put $L=\lfloor(q-1)/2\rfloor$. Every admissible $M$ is recovered from $k\le L$ and a positive quotient $t$ by $M=(q(q-k-1)+1)t$. The divisor is at least $qL+1$, so $t\le\lfloor N/(qL+1)\rfloor$. These pairs account for at most $N/q$ sizes. The theorem makes no assertion of existence for any of the allowed sizes. ## A divisor of the full output mask

Let $A,B\in\mathbb Q[X]$ be coprime, with $A$ monic and $\deg B<\deg A$ (the Lean statement uses natural degrees). Let $u$ be real algebraic with monic minimal polynomial $m(T)=\sum_{i=0}^r m_iT^i$. Define
\[
  D(X)=\sum_{i=0}^r m_i A(X)^i(-B(X))^{r-i}.
\]
A rational polynomial $F$ is divisible by $A+uB$ over the reals if and only if $D$ divides $F$ over the rationals. For integral $A,B,F$ and an algebraic integer $u$, the divisibility holds in $\mathbb Z[X]$ using the integral minimal polynomial. Thus a normalized marginal polynomial of this form forces its norm to divide the entire shifted binary support mask of a uniform independent sum. The PMF theorem assumes $A(0)=1$, $B(0)=0$ and the specified marginal model. The opposite PMF has unrestricted masses and support. Divisibility tests the presence of the factor; a probability decomposition also requires a nonnegative cofactor. [`pencil_dvd_iff_norm`, `integral_norm_iff` and `norm_dvd_output`](Green28PencilNorm.lean)

The norm is the classical product of conjugate factors. The formal proof uses only the rational polynomial and its real specialization. Modulo $A+uB$, replace $A$ by $-uB$ in the displayed sum, obtaining $(-B)^rm(u)=0$. The leading term of $D$ is $A^r$. The coefficient-projection bound forces every nonzero rational multiple of the pencil to have degree at least $r\deg A$. The remainder of any rational multiple upon division by $D$ is another such multiple of smaller degree, hence zero. Monic division descends to the integers.
