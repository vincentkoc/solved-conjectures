# Green 28 with one atom below maximal mass

Let independent integer laws $P,Q$ have a sum uniform on its support. Write $w(P)$ for the largest occupied integer minus the smallest, and $L(Q)$ for the number of distinct positive masses in $Q$. If at most one occupied site of $P$ has less than maximal mass and $w(Q)\le w(P)$, then **both laws are uniform**. The number and spacing of sites and the weights of $Q$ are arbitrary. Uniformity of the sum implies finite support for both marginals. The theorem uses the upstream definitions of the sum and uniformity. [Lean proof](Green28AlgebraicWidth.lean) · [Pinned Green 28](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/28.lean)

More precisely, if the laws fail to be uniform and $P$ has at most one deficient atom, let $u$ be its mass divided by the mass of the rightmost occupied site. Then $0<u<1$, and its algebraic degree $d$ over $\mathbb Q$ satisfies
\[
  2\le d\le L(Q),\qquad w(P)<w(Q),\qquad (d-1)w(P)\le w(Q).
\]
The value bound also holds with several deficient atoms. It suffices that $u<1$ occurs among the occupied ratios and every occupied ratio on the left is $c+eu$ for rational $c,e$. All ratios use the same rightmost mass as denominator. Thus an affine family of left weights of degree $d$ requires at least $d$ distinct right weights. This statement allows either law to be wider. [`one_deficit_alternative` and `degree_of_affine_ratios`](Green28AlgebraicWidth.lean)

## Why the cofactor has to grow

Translate each support to start at zero and scale each polynomial to be monic. The binary product has constant term one. Each factor's constant term is at most one by pairing it with the opposite leading term. Hence both constant terms equal one. If both widths equal the same positive integer, the corresponding product coefficient receives two unit contributions. This excludes equal positive widths. Earlier arithmetic of the normalized factors gives integrality and rules out any rational coefficient strictly between zero and one. [Polynomial model and arithmetic](GREEN-28-ARITHMETIC.md)

Consider a rational product $(A+uB)g=F$ with $A,B,F\in\mathbb Q[X]$ and $g$ real and monic. For $k<d$, choose a rational linear functional $\phi$ on the reals that kills $1,u,\ldots,u^{k-1}$ and sends $u^k$ to one. Apply it coefficientwise to $u^i g$ to form $G_i$. For $i<k$ the product identity gives $AG_i=-BG_{i+1}$. If $A,B$ are coprime, iteration implies $A^k\mid G_k$. The coefficient of $G_k$ at $\deg g$ is one, so $k\deg A\le\deg g$. For the single deficient site at exponent $r$, the monic left polynomial is $A+uX^r$. The rational mask $A$ has constant term one and degree $w(P)$, so it is coprime to $X^r$. [`cofactor_degree`](Green28AlgebraicWidth.lean)

For the value bound, take any rational vector space $V\subseteq\mathbb R$ containing the coefficients of $g$. If some $u^k$ were outside $V$, choose $\phi$ vanishing on $V$ with $\phi(u^k)=1$. Then $G_0=0$. Whenever $G_i=0$, its leading-position coefficient gives $\phi(u^i)=0$, so the same recurrence forces $G_{i+1}=0$ because $B\ne0$. At $i=k$ this contradicts $\phi(u^k)=1$. The first $d$ powers of $u$ are linearly independent. The coefficient span of $g$ is generated by at most $L(Q)$ values, since scaling all positive masses by one common factor preserves their number. [`cofactor_span`](Green28AlgebraicWidth.lean)

## Scope and sources

Norm methods for factoring rational polynomials over an algebraic field are classical. The projection proof here works directly with a real cofactor. Hare studies coefficient constraints for unfair factors, and Hasanbegović studies the first nonbinary coefficients and large gaps. The trinomial manuscript treats one factor with exactly three terms at arbitrary widths. The width criterion above permits arbitrarily many terms with a single deficient coefficient and requires the indicated width comparison. The general conjecture remains open.
