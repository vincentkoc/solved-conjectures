# A size gap for cyclic bases containing a prime Singer line

Let $p$ be prime, $v=p^2+p+1$, and $D\subset\mathbb Z/v\mathbb Z$ the projective labels of the kernel of any nonzero linear functional on a cubic extension of $\mathbb F_p$. Assume the classical Deligne rank-four hyper-Kloosterman bound in the normalization of the earlier Singer calculation. For $p\ge100000$, every ordinary basis $T\subseteq\mathbb Z/(2v)\mathbb Z$ containing the even copy $2D$ satisfies
\[
        20000|T|\ge40001p,\qquad
        \frac{|T|}{\sqrt{2v}}\ge\sqrt2+\frac1{100000}.
\]
There is no size or shape restriction on the odd layer or on extra even points. More generally, for any full basis, adjoining its missing Singer points gives the same lower bounds with $|T|+|2D\setminus T|$ on the left in place of $|T|$. In particular, omitting $o(p)$ Singer points still precludes an asymptotically optimal family. The constants are explicit and unoptimized. ## Why the other parity cannot repair the line cheaply

Take any $B\subseteq\mathbb Z/v\mathbb Z$ with $|B|=p+1$. For $p\ge4096$, the ordinary sumset of $2D\cup(1+2B)$ misses at least $p^2/20$ residues. Write $h=v-|D+B|$, the number of lines disjoint from $B$. If $20h\ge p^2$, the odd layer alone gives the bound. Otherwise prime-plane stability supplies a line containing all but at most $3p/40$ points of $B$. A reflected pair of lines misses at least $7p^2/32$ residues under the rank-four estimate. Each displaced point can gain at most $2(p+1)$ sums. These estimates give the balanced defect for every $B$. [Finite reflection estimate](Green33SingerAllCharacteristics.lean) · [Earlier incidence counts and repair inequalities](SINGER-BLOCKING-LAYERS.md)

The geometric step adapts Szőnyi and Weiner’s prime-plane stability argument to these cyclic coordinates. At a point with few secants, choose a tangent as the line at infinity. An auxiliary polynomial of degree $d$ vanishes on the missed affine lines with nonzero slope. The resulting Rédei polynomial at slope zero has the form $X^p f+g$. Its Wronskian gives either many distinct intercepts or a large repeated intercept. The secant bound rules out the first case. The group labels in the proof come from the scalar quotient of the given cubic field. This part uses only prime-field algebra and finite counting. [Szőnyi and Weiner, Theorem 2.4](https://real.mtak.hu/13831/1/stabprimfinal.pdf) · [Blokhuis et al., §3.1](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/3536392/696271935668854.pdf) · [Lacunary polynomial](Green33PrimeSingerStability.lean) · [Affine slope count](Green33PrimeAffineSlopes.lean) · [Cyclic incidence](Green33PrimeSingerIncidence.lean)

For a full basis with even and odd layer sizes $x,y$, the odd residues require $xy\ge v$. Trim or extend the odd layer to $p+1$ points, and keep $D$ in the even layer. Each point of $T$ outside this balanced set can cover at most $|T|$ holes. If $x+y$ is close to $2p$, the product bound forces both layers close to $p$. The quadratic defect then exceeds what those outside points could cover. The theorem `anchored_gap` makes this deduction for any prescribed even layer with the stated defect.
