# Testing Green 28 on a prime cycle

Fix an upper bound $k$ for the number of values taken by an independent sum. For **any one prime** $p>5^{k+1}$, Green 28 for such sums over the integers is equivalent to the same assertion over $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$. Both factors may have arbitrary real atom probabilities. Taking one such prime for each $k$ gives an equivalence with the exact pinned proposition, including its finite-support hypotheses. The validity of these tests for every $k$ remains open. **Four atoms per marginal.** On every prime cycle of order $p>15{,}625$, two independent laws with at most four atoms each can have a uniform sum only if both laws are uniform on their respective supports. Atom positions are unrestricted. The numerical bound is sufficient. The proof uses the six differences from the two anchor sites and the integer four-atom theorem. ## Which modular sums lift

For a list of $m$ residues with $5^m<p$, simultaneous approximation gives a single multiplier $0<q<p$ and integer representatives $r_i\equiv qx_i\pmod p$ with $5|r_i|<p$. If $x_i+x_j=x_u+x_v$ in the cycle, the difference $r_i+r_j-r_u-r_v$ is divisible by $p$ and has absolute value less than $p$. It vanishes. Conversely, an integer equality gives the modular equality by cancellation of $q$. This treats every pair, including repeated sites and sums whose least nonnegative representatives differ by $p$. [`pair_iff` and `rectify`](Green28PrimeCyclic.lean)

Let $A,B$ be the marginal supports and $S=A+B$ the occupied output sites. For $5^{|S|}<p$, rectify the sites of $S$ using a map $F$. Choose $a_0\in A$, $b_0\in B$, and write $m=a_0+b_0$. Send the original atoms $a,b$ to $F(a+b_0)-F(m)$ and $F(a_0+b)$, respectively. The identity $(a+b_0)+(a_0+b)=(a+b)+m$ lifts on $S$, so each pair lands at $F(a+b)$. Injectivity on $S$ ensures that distinct occupied sums stay distinct. The two marginals and the independent sum are pushforwards under injections on their supports. Each atom retains its original mass, and equality of independent sums holds as an equality of probability measures. The reverse implication uses the integer compression into $[0,5^{k+1}]$, which already contains the occupied sums as well as the two marginal supports. Reduction modulo $p$ therefore preserves all three laws. [`lift_measures`, `Transport.map_sum` and `Transport.masses`](Green28CyclicLaws.lean) · [Integer compression](GREEN-28-FINITE-TEST.md)

A second criterion needs only $5^{|A|+|B|-2}<p$. Rectify the nonzero differences in $(A-a_0)\cup(B-b_0)$, with zero represented by zero. For every two occupied pairs, equality of their sums in the cycle is equivalent to equality of their new integer sums. Thus any representative pair defines the same output label, and distinct output sites receive distinct labels. This argument works for arbitrary probabilities, even when the sum is nonuniform. [`lift_by_marginals`](Green28CyclicLaws.lean)

## Sources and verification

Rectification of small sets in prime cyclic groups is classical. Bilu, Lev and Ruzsa's Theorem 3.1 gives a stronger order-two cardinality bound, also stated as Lemma 1 in *On Unique Sums in Abelian Groups*. The elementary fifths argument here supplies explicit representatives for the probability-law transport. The DeepMind issue and statement PR, Left to Solve and Open Math Agents were checked for the prime-cycle reduction and its four-atom application. The earlier polynomial reduction concerns the integer problem.
