# Twofold sums of cubic graphs

Let $f:U\to V$ be a map of additive degree at most three between finite binary vector spaces. Its graph has an additive complement of size $|V|$. The twofold graph sum contains a **horizontal affine subspace** of codimension bounded only in $|V|$ and independent of $\dim U$. More explicitly, there are $a\in U$ and a subspace $P\le U$ such that for every $h\in P$, some $x$ satisfies $f(x)=f(x+a+h)$. Adding the two corresponding graph points gives $(a+h,0)$. [Horizontal theorem and Green 52 specialization](Green52CubicTransversals.lean) · [Pinned Green 52](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/52.lean)

The hypothesis requires each mixed fourth forward difference to vanish. The proof also applies to the graph of $g\circ f$ for **any** map $g$ on the values of $f$, since equality of two $f$-values survives postcomposition. The horizontal codimension depends on the number of possible intermediate values. Embedding the flat in the graph’s ambient space adds the dimension of the final output space. The same flat works for any set containing this graph. Arbitrary function graphs, which suffice for the full conjecture, remain open. [Function-graph reduction](EXACT-COMPLEMENTS.md)

## Restricting the third differences

For a scalar component $\lambda f$, the third difference is a symmetric trilinear form $T_\lambda$. At a fixed input direction $a$, the first difference is a quadratic function with polar form $T_\lambda(a,\cdot,\cdot)$. We use a finite-field rank argument to restrict the input space. The connection between bias, derivative rank and the structure of cubic polynomials is classical. Haramaty and Shpilka give general structure theorems for biased cubics. [Haramaty and Shpilka](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2853) · [Polar form in Lean](Green52CubicTransversals.lean)

If rank-at-most-$R$ directions have density at least $2^{-D}$, their span has codimension at most $D$ and consists of sums of at most $2\cdot4^D$ such directions. To see this, add the set to itself four times at each step. Its cardinality cannot double more than $D$ times. Once $|4B|<2|B|$, every translate $v+B$ for $v\in3B$ meets $B$, giving $3B=2B$. Then $2B$ is already a subspace. Subadditivity of rank gives a uniform bound $L=2\cdot4^D R$ on the resulting span. [Dense directions and trilinear restrictions](Green52CubicRegularity.lean)

Inside that span, choose a contraction of maximum rank $r\le L$. Impose its radical condition and freeze the values at $r$ pivot vectors. Each pivot costs at most $r$ linear equations by symmetry. The image of the contraction on the resulting parameter translate stays fixed by maximality. The trilinear form vanishes on a subspace of additional codimension at most $r(r+1)$. Repeat on the **current** input space until every scalar form either vanishes there or has rare low-rank directions there. Each restriction makes at least one more scalar form vanish. [Simultaneous restriction](Green52CubicRegularity.lean)

## Finding a flat of zero derivatives

Write $N=|V|$, set $D=2N$ and $R=2(N+1)$. First restrict directions to a subspace of codimension at most $N-1$ where every scalar derivative takes zero somewhere. The rare sets above leave a direction $a$ in this subspace. For each surviving high-rank form, fixing an $R$ by $R$ minor on a translate through $a$ costs at most $R^2$ further linear equations. These equations restrict the directions; the quadratic character sums still average over the entire input space obtained in the preceding stage. [Scalar projections](Green52AffineDerivatives.lean) · [Finite union and rank bounds](Green52CubicParameters.lean)

For a vanished trilinear form, the first derivative of its scalar component is affine. It takes zero, so its binary character sum is nonnegative. For the other components the quadratic character bound makes each absolute sum smaller than the size of the input space divided by $N$. The zero functional contributes the whole input space. Orthogonality then forces a simultaneous vector-valued zero on each of the selected directions. [Character estimates](Green52CubicFibres.lean)

In the notation above the proof bounds the horizontal codimension by $N(D+L(L+1))+N+NR^2$. The earlier $d^2$ bound for quadratic graphs with $d$ output coordinates is substantially smaller. [Quadratic case](EXACT-COMPLEMENTS.md) · [Replay instructions](../README.md#replay)
