# Polynomial models in twofold sumsets have density threshold one half

At density at least $1/2$, every binary set $A$ has a linear functional whose kernel lies in $A+A$. Below $1/2$, the answer changes even if a model may use any finite system of polynomial equations. For each $\rho<1/2$, degree budget $D$, and minimum dimension $N$, a Hamming ball of density greater than $\rho$ in some dimension $n\ge N$ has the following property. Every nonempty common zero set contained in $A+A$ requires equations whose total degrees sum to more than $D$. The declaration `bounded_degree_iff` gives the exact equivalence. [Threshold theorem](Green50PolynomialModels.lean) · [Half-density endpoint](Green50HalfDensity.lean)

For a full polynomial graph in the $r$-fold sum of a radius-$k$ Hamming ball, write $P_j$ for its output coordinates in arbitrary input and output bases. The ambient space can be decomposed into any pair of complementary subspaces. Then

$$n-rk\;\le\;\sum_j \max(1,\deg P_j).$$

In particular a quadratic graph in $A+A$ needs at least $(n-2k)/2$ output dimensions. For every $C\ge0$ and every $\rho<1/2$, some balls of density $\alpha>\rho$ in arbitrarily large dimensions force that dimension above $C\log_2(1/\alpha)$. The proof uses the same polar identity and graph predicate as the five-sum theorem. It rules out obtaining the unrestricted Green 50 conjecture by extracting a quadratic graph of this size from every twofold sum. Green’s question about affine subspaces in $10A$ remains open. ## Proof

Over any finite field, choose a common zero with the fewest zero coordinates. Freeze the nonzero coordinates and let the remaining $h$ coordinates vary. The restricted system has exactly one solution, since any other one has fewer zeros. Affine substitution preserves the total-degree upper bound. Chevalley–Warning therefore gives $h\le\sum_i\deg P_i$. In the binary cube this also bounds the Hamming weight of some zero from below. [Mathlib’s Chevalley–Warning theorem](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/FieldTheory/ChevalleyWarning.lean) · [Polynomial and graph proofs](Green50PolynomialModels.lean)

Take $n=2m+1$ and $k=m-s$. The twofold sum has maximum weight at most $2k$, leaving a gap of $2s+1$. Symmetry of the binomial coefficients and the bound $(m+1)\binom{2m}{m}^2\le16^m$ give density at least $1/2-1/q$ whenever $m\ge(qs)^2$ and $q\ge1$. Thus the gap can grow at any density threshold below one half. At the endpoint, each missing sum translates $A$ onto its complement. Two missing sums differ by an element of the translation stabilizer, so a linear functional separating one missing sum from that stabilizer supplies the kernel. Sanders records the Green–Ruzsa construction for subspaces in twofold sums and proves a stronger near-half result for subspaces.
