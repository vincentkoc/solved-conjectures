# Green 33 · a higher-moment obstruction

Every two-basis in the cyclic group of prime order **173,203,273** has at least **18,613** elements. The finite order bound permits 18,612 and the [first power-sum test](PRIME-MOMENTS.md) also permits that size. Indeed, writing `m = 9306` gives `p = 2m² + 1` and `4⁵⁵² ≡ −m (mod p)`. Both this congruence and the improved bound are checked in `obstruction_mod_173203273`. [Lean proof](Green33NewtonObstruction.lean)

**General criterion.** Let `A + A = Z/p` have size `k`, with `2p = k² + 2` and `k < p − 1`. Call a positive degree `r ≤ k` resonant if `2ʳ = −2k` in the prime field. The theorem `card_mem_of_resonant_degrees` proves that `k` belongs to every additive submonoid of the natural numbers containing these degrees. In particular it belongs to the submonoid they generate.

For the [extremal reflection](REFLECTION-RIGIDITY.md) with center `s`, set `v(a) = 2a − s` and `Pᵣ = ∑ v(a)ʳ` over `a ∈ A`. Suppose a submonoid `D` contains every resonant degree. If `r ∉ D`, every splitting `r = j + (r − j)` has at least one part outside `D`. Induction kills all interior products in the binomial expansion of the pair moment. The weighted representation identity then reduces to `(2k + 2ʳ)Pᵣ = 0`. Since `r` lies outside `D`, this forces `Pᵣ = 0`. Newton’s identities now give the same vanishing for elementary symmetric functions of degrees outside `D`. The top one is the product of all the nonzero entries `v(a)`, so its degree `k` must lie in `D`. This adapts the two inductions in Dongryul Kim’s argument for perfect Lee codes to the [two-basis representation profile](Green33PrimeMoments.lean). [Kim, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.08412#page=3)

**The modulus.** The element 2 has order 4,418 modulo `p = 173203273`, and `2¹¹⁰⁶ = −37224` there. At size `k = 18612` every resonant degree therefore has the form `1106 + 4418b`. The submonoid generated by 1,106 and 4,418 contains every such degree but omits 18,612. In a putative expression `18612 = 1106a + 4418b`, one would have `0 ≤ b ≤ 4`, and none of those five remainders is divisible by 1,106.
