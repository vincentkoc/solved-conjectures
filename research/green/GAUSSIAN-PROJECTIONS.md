# Green 54 · Gaussian convexity in a countable product

For every balanced compact set of standard Gaussian mass at least 0.99 in ℝ^ℕ, its tenfold Minkowski sum contains a compact convex set of mass at least 0.01. `Green54Solved.green_54` proves this directly at the type of the pinned DeepMind declaration. The mathematics follows Hua, Song and Tudose’s solution of Talagrand’s problem. The separate two-summand counterexample was formalized in open-math-agents. [Lean theorem](Green54Solved.lean) · [DeepMind statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/54.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10908) · [Two-summand proof](https://github.com/GuillaumeBAUD-coder/open-math-agents/pull/9697)

The proof also gives six summands and a convex witness of mass at least one half for any compact input of mass greater than five sixths. The three-Gaussian theorem also holds for target laws with unbounded support in every finite dimension. All the proofs use the notebook’s pinned replay. [Three-Gaussian theorem](Green54GeneralLaws.lean) · [Replay](../README.md#replay)

## Finite projections

Let μ be any finite Borel measure on ℝ^ℕ, D a compact set, and β a mass threshold. Then D contains a compact convex set of μ-mass at least β if and only if every projection of D onto a nonempty finite coordinate set contains a compact convex set of marginal mass at least β. Each projection can choose its own witness.

For a finite coordinate set I, lift its witness back to D by taking the inverse image and intersecting with D. In the compact space of compact subsets of D, require the I-projection to be convex and its marginal mass to be at least β. Both conditions are closed. For the mass condition, outer regularity gives an open neighborhood of any compact set whose mass is below β, with neighborhood mass still below β. A witness for the union of finitely many coordinate sets meets all their conditions. Compactness gives one compact C meeting every condition. Its projection cylinders decrease to C, so continuity of the measure gives μ(C) ≥ β. Convexity follows by intersecting these convex cylinders. [`compact_convex_iff_projections`](Green54GaussianProjections.lean)

## Gaussian constants

Write γ for standard Gaussian measure in the dimension under discussion. Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 5.2 of Hua, Song and Tudose, with `b = c = 1/3`, give the finite input. For compact K with γ(K) > 5/6, the real dilation `3 · (K + K + K)` contains a compact convex C with γ(C) ≥ 1/3. Their body can also be chosen symmetric. The Lean hypothesis uses only convexity and compactness. [Hua–Song–Tudose, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S5)

`finite_iff_countable` proves that this compact-input estimate in all finite dimensions is equivalent to the same estimate on ℝ^ℕ. Coordinate projections commute with sums and real dilations. For the converse, lift a finite input to its cylinder in ℝ^ℕ. Its mass exceeds 5/6, so inner regularity gives a compact subset still above the threshold. Project the resulting countable witness back onto the original coordinates.

Every point of `3 · (K + K + K)` is a sum of nine elements of K. This gives the countable nine-summand conclusion for arbitrary compact K. In DeepMind’s balanced case, positive mass makes K nonempty and balancedness gives 0 ∈ K. Adding zero gives a tenth summand. The final theorem `green_54_of_finite` has exactly the positive DeepMind conclusion under the finite hypothesis. The proof of the positive declaration uses the six-summand estimate below. ## Three summands

Mazhar subsequently proved that if a finite-dimensional Gaussian set A has mass greater than 2/3, then A + A + A contains a compact convex set of mass at least 1/2 containing the origin. The notebook proves the countable compact-input mass conclusion from the finite theorem. More generally, `gaussian_sum_of_finite` preserves any fixed number of Minkowski summands and both mass thresholds. The specialization `gaussian_three_of_finite` uses the finite mass estimate as a hypothesis and gives mass at least 1/2 inside K + K + K, without a balance assumption. ## Deriving the convex witness

`green_54_of_analytic_inputs` derives the exact positive DeepMind conclusion from two finite analytic statements. The first is the compact-support case of the three-Gaussian theorem of Hua, Song and Tudose. A law below the standard Gaussian in convex order can be realized as the sum of three vectors with standard Gaussian marginals. The second is Kwapień’s comparison. For a continuous integrable convex function f and standard Gaussian G, the event `f(G) ≤ E f(G)` has probability at least 1/2. The compact-support decomposition is proved below. Kwapień’s comparison remains a hypothesis of this three-summand argument. [Lean proof](Green54ConvexTests.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S1) · [Mazhar’s use of Kwapień’s inequality, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14832v2#S2)

The argument follows Mazhar’s three-sum proof. Let F be compact and disjoint from A + A + A. Under any coupling with the prescribed marginals, all three coordinates belong to A with positive probability when γ(A) > 2/3. Their sum therefore cannot have a law supported on F. Triple decomposition implies that no probability law on F is dominated in convex order by γ.

Lean then produces **one** continuous convex function f with `f(x) > E f(G)` at every x in F. The separation lemma works on any compact Hausdorff space. If each test in a convex family containing zero is nonpositive somewhere, Hahn–Banach separates the family from the strictly positive functions. The Riesz representation theorem turns the separating positive functional into one probability measure with nonpositive integral for every test. Applying this alternative to convex functions minus their Gaussian means gives the required f.

Kwapień’s estimate gives mass at least 1/2 to the convex sublevel `f ≤ E f(G)`. Intersect it with a large closed ball and take F to be the part of the ball outside a small neighborhood of A + A + A. The intersection lies in that neighborhood, and the lost mass is bounded by the mass outside the ball. Compactness of the space of compact convex witnesses recovers the exact triple sum and the full mass threshold simultaneously. The finite proof also applies to any reference law with the stated decomposition and sublevel bounds. [Finite-dimensional derivation](Green54ConvexTests.lean) · [Compactness and countable transfer](Green54GaussianProjections.lean)

## Six summands from Gaussian decomposition alone

`green_54_of_triple` derives the exact positive DeepMind declaration with a single analytic hypothesis, the compact-support case of Hua–Song–Tudose’s three-Gaussian theorem. For any compact K of mass greater than 5/6, six Minkowski summands contain a compact convex set of mass at least 1/2. For symmetric compact K the input threshold falls to 2/3, and the witness lies inside the real dilation `2 · (K + K + K)`. Both bounds hold in the countable Gaussian product. [Lean proof](Green54SymmetricBarrier.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S1)

For a symmetric input, separate each forbidden point and its reflection from the triple sum using one convex test f. The function `g(x) = f(x) + f(−x) − 2f(0)` is convex, nonnegative, and zero at the origin. Reflection invariance preserves the strict gap above its expectation on the forbidden points. Markov’s inequality gives mass at least 1/2 to the sublevel at twice the expectation. Convexity gives `g(x/2) ≤ g(x)/2`, so this sublevel avoids the doubled forbidden set. Compact approximation gives the claimed witness. For arbitrary K of mass greater than 5/6, its symmetric subset `K ∩ (−K)` has mass greater than 2/3. Mazhar discusses even barriers and the cost of using Markov’s inequality in the finite-dimensional construction. [Mazhar, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14832v2#S3)

## Reduction to finite support

It suffices to construct a coupling for a finite-support target law dominated in convex order by the law of `rG`, for some `0 < r < 1`. Each of the three summands must have the original standard Gaussian law. Under this hypothesis in every finite dimension, `green_54_of_finite_laws` gives the positive DeepMind conclusion with its original thresholds. The conditional-density construction below supplies the next part of this coupling. [Lean proof](Green54FiniteLaws.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, proof of Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S3)

The reduction works for any reference probability law on a separable Banach space. Partition a compact support into finitely many measurable cells of diameter less than ε. Replace each positive-mass cell by its barycentre. Jensen’s inequality decreases every continuous convex test integral, and the replacement moves each point by less than ε. The resulting finite laws converge weakly to the original law. The joint laws with three fixed marginals are tight and weakly closed, so Prokhorov’s theorem makes them compact. Addition is continuous, hence the set of their sum laws is compact as well. This proves the equivalence of finite and compact support. Finally, contract the finite atoms by factors tending to one. Their laws are dominated by the corresponding contractions of the reference law, and weak closure gives the original target. [Hua–Song–Tudose, Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S2) · [Lemma 3.1 and the contraction argument](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S3)

## Constructing the finite conditional laws

For a finite target with positive atom masses `pᵢ` at `xᵢ`, the notebook now constructs its conditional Gaussian densities. They are positive, smooth and log-concave. Each density `fᵢ` has Gaussian integral one and barycentre `xᵢ`, and `Σ pᵢ fᵢ(y) = 1` at every point. The construction applies when the target is dominated in convex order by a strict contraction of the Gaussian. It handles zero-mass labels by selecting exactly the positive atoms and recovering the original measure from their weighted point masses. This formalizes the existence direction of Hua–Song–Tudose’s softmax result for the reference law that Green 54 needs. [Lean construction](Green54Softmax.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, Proposition 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S3)

The proof uses the paper’s log-partition objective. Fix one affine score to zero. If two scores have different slopes, their affine graphs cross, and the full support of the reference law makes the expected upper envelope strictly exceed its value at the origin. Strict contraction gives a gap between that expectation and the score assigned to the target atoms. If the slopes agree, unequal intercepts give the gap because every listed atom has positive mass. Compactness makes the gap uniform on the normalized parameter sphere. By homogeneity the objective decreases at least linearly at infinity, so it attains a maximum. Differentiating under the integral at the maximum gives both moment constraints. The variational proof works for any centered, full-support probability measure with finite first moment in a finite-dimensional real normed space. [`exists_softmax_densities`](Green54Softmax.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S4.SS1)

For each softmax component the translated two-Gaussian representation expresses its law as that of `xᵢ + U + V`, where both marginals are standard Gaussian. Mixing the triples `(xᵢ + U + V, −U, −V)` with probabilities `pᵢ` gives three standard Gaussian marginals whose sum has the original target law. The first marginal follows from the pointwise density identity, and the other two follow from Gaussian reflection symmetry. [Component representation](Green54SoftmaxPair.lean) · [Finite mixture](Green54GaussianTilts.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, Corollary 2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S2.SS4)

## Adapted Gaussian contractions

Let $G_1,\ldots,G_n$ be independent standard Gaussian vectors in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. Before observing $G_j$, choose a measurable linear contraction $A_j$ using the earlier vectors. The normalized sum $n^{-1/2}\sum_j A_jG_j$ has the law of the midpoint of two standard Gaussian vectors. This also holds for every weak limit of such sums. [Lean proof](Green54GaussianContractions.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, proof of Lemma 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S2.SS4)

For each step, write $B_j=(I-A_jA_j^*)^{1/2}$ and take a fresh independent standard Gaussian vector $H_j$. Both $A_jG_j+B_jH_j$ and $A_jG_j-B_jH_j$ have standard Gaussian law conditional on the past. The continuous dependence of the positive square root on the contraction makes these corrections measurable. Each sign gives a sequence of independent standard Gaussians. Their normalized sums have the required midpoint. Tightness of the two fixed marginals gives weak compactness of all midpoint laws.

## Centered Lipschitz images

For every 2-Lipschitz map $T$ on a finite-dimensional Euclidean space and every standard Gaussian $G$, the law of $T(G)-\mathbb E T(G)$ equals the law of $X+Y$ for a pair with standard Gaussian marginals. The joint law of the pair depends on $T$. This is the normalized form of Song’s Lipschitz image lemma, restated as Lemma 2.3 by Hua, Song and Tudose. [Lean theorem](Green54LipschitzImages.lean) · [Song, Lemma 2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22342) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S2.SS4)

The proof uses $n+1$ independent Gaussian increments. At each step the sum of the other $n$ increments, divided by $\sqrt n$, is exactly standard Gaussian and independent of the current increment. Rademacher’s theorem and dominated convergence give a Gaussian mean-square bound on the directional remainder. Averaging the derivative over the unrevealed increments gives a linear map of norm at most two. After centering, the errors at different steps are orthogonal. Their total second moment tends to zero, which gives the required weak approximation by finite adapted contractions. [Directional estimate](Green54GaussianLinearization.lean) · [Resampling and orthogonality](Green54GaussianResampling.lean) · [Finite approximation](Green54GaussianApproximation.lean)

One route from this lemma to Green 54 assumes Caffarelli’s contraction theorem for smooth positive log-concave Gaussian tilts. The proof below constructs the couplings needed for softmax components using Langevin approximation. ## Langevin approximation

Let $\pi(dx)=c\,e^{-U(x)}\,dx$ be a probability measure in a finite Euclidean space. Suppose $G$ is an $L$-Lipschitz subgradient of $U$ and $\langle x-y,G(x)-G(y)\rangle\geq\|x-y\|^2$. For independent $Y\sim\pi$ and standard Gaussian $Z$, set $M=\mathbb E(\|G(Y)\|+2\|Z\|)^3$. Assume $M$ and $\mathbb E\|Y-x_0\|$ are finite. The unadjusted Langevin step sends $x$ to $x-hG(x)+\sqrt{2h}\,Z$. For $0<2h\leq1$ and $hL^2\leq1$, its expectation operator $P_h$ satisfies
$$
\left|P_h^n f(x_0)-\int f\,d\pi\right|
\leq \operatorname{Lip}(f)\left[(1-h)^{n/2}\mathbb E\|Y-x_0\|+
2\left(\frac L2+\frac L4(2+L)\right)M\sqrt h\right].
$$
One choice of $h,n$ therefore works for all 1-Lipschitz tests to any prescribed precision. Linear tests are included. Quantitative convergence of this algorithm for strongly convex potentials is classical. [Lean estimate and approximation theorem](Green54LangevinStationarity.lean) · [Durmus and Moulines](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05021)

The proof compares the density of $(Y,Y-hG(Y)+\sqrt{2h}\,Z)$ with its transpose. Their minimum is symmetric, so it integrates $f(y)-f(x)$ to zero. The remaining term costs at most $\operatorname{Lip}(f)(L/2+L(2+L)/4)Mh^{3/2}$ per step. Contraction sums those errors geometrically. Independently, the centered finite Euler endpoint, viewed as a function of all its Gaussian innovations, has an exact representation as a sum of two standard Gaussian marginals. [Gaussian kernel](Green54GaussianKernel.lean) · [Log-density bound](Green54GaussianBalance.lean) · [Error propagation](Green54LangevinConvergence.lean) · [Finite-iterate Gaussian representation](Green54GaussianIterations.lean) · [Rectangular Lipschitz images](Green54RectangularImages.lean)

## Passing to an exact coupling

Under the preceding Lipschitz subgradient and moment hypotheses, the centered law of $Y\sim\pi$ is exactly the law of $X_1+X_2$, with each $X_i$ standard Gaussian. To take the limit, first group the coordinates of a single Gaussian into independent innovation vectors. The resulting path integral equals $P_h^n f(x_0)$ for every Lipschitz $f$. The uniform test bound controls the norm of the difference of the means by testing the inner product in that direction. It follows that the centered endpoint laws converge weakly to the centered target. The laws of sums with two prescribed marginals form a compact set, so the target has the required coupling. [Path integral](Green54LangevinPaths.lean) · [Exact representation](Green54LangevinLimits.lean)

## Applying the representation to Green 54

For a softmax component the potential is half the squared norm plus the log-sum-exp of the affine scores, minus the selected score. Its drift is the point plus the weighted mean slope, minus the selected slope. Jensen’s inequality gives the supporting hyperplane. The quadratic term gives strong monotonicity with modulus one. Finitely many slopes and softmax weights bounded by one give a global Lipschitz constant. Each conditional law has a bounded density relative to the standard Gaussian, so its first moment and the required cubic moment are finite. The limiting two-Gaussian coupling therefore applies to each component. Mixing the couplings, taking compact limits and applying the six-summand estimate yields the stated ten-summand result. ## Target laws with unbounded support

Let a probability law on a finite-dimensional real space be dominated by the standard Gaussian in convex order. Then three random vectors, each with standard Gaussian law, have a sum with the given distribution. Their dependence is part of the construction. This is Theorem 1.1 of Hua, Song and Tudose. [Lean theorem](Green54GeneralLaws.lean) · [Original theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S1)

The reduction from compact targets works in any separable Banach space with a reference law of finite first moment. Tightness gives compact sets whose omitted probabilities are summable. On each complement, replace the target vector by its conditional mean. Jensen’s inequality preserves convex domination. Almost surely these replacements eventually leave each sample fixed, so the laws converge weakly. The set of laws of sums with the three prescribed marginals is weakly closed.
