# Green 54 · Gaussian convexity in a countable product

DeepMind asks whether ten sums of a balanced compact set of Gaussian mass at least 0.99 in ℝ^ℕ contain a compact convex set of mass at least 0.01. Hua, Song and Tudose proved a quantitative theorem for finite-dimensional Gaussian measure. This notebook proves the transfer to ℝ^ℕ in Lean. The finite analytic theorem is an explicit hypothesis. [Pinned DeepMind declaration](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/54.lean) · [Lean proof](Green54GaussianProjections.lean)

## Finite projections

Let μ be any finite Borel measure on ℝ^ℕ, D a compact set, and β a mass threshold. Then D contains a compact convex set of μ-mass at least β if and only if every projection of D onto a nonempty finite coordinate set contains a compact convex set of marginal mass at least β. Each projection can choose its own witness.

For a finite coordinate set I, lift its witness back to D by taking the inverse image and intersecting with D. In the compact space of compact subsets of D, require the I-projection to be convex and its marginal mass to be at least β. Both conditions are closed. For the mass condition, outer regularity gives an open neighborhood of any compact set whose mass is below β, with neighborhood mass still below β. A witness for the union of finitely many coordinate sets meets all their conditions. Compactness gives one compact C meeting every condition. Its projection cylinders decrease to C, so continuity of the measure gives μ(C) ≥ β. Convexity follows by intersecting these convex cylinders. [`compact_convex_iff_projections`](Green54GaussianProjections.lean)

## Gaussian constants

Write γ for standard Gaussian measure in the dimension under discussion. Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 5.2 of Hua, Song and Tudose, with `b = c = 1/3`, give the finite input. For compact K with γ(K) > 5/6, the real dilation `3 · (K + K + K)` contains a compact convex C with γ(C) ≥ 1/3. Their body can also be chosen symmetric. The Lean hypothesis uses only convexity and compactness. [Hua–Song–Tudose, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S5)

`finite_iff_countable` proves that this compact-input estimate in all finite dimensions is equivalent to the same estimate on ℝ^ℕ. Coordinate projections commute with sums and real dilations. For the converse, lift a finite input to its cylinder in ℝ^ℕ. Its mass exceeds 5/6, so inner regularity gives a compact subset still above the threshold. Project the resulting countable witness back onto the original coordinates.

Every point of `3 · (K + K + K)` is a sum of nine elements of K. This gives the countable nine-summand conclusion for arbitrary compact K. In DeepMind’s balanced case, positive mass makes K nonempty and balancedness gives 0 ∈ K. Adding zero gives a tenth summand. The final theorem `green_54_of_finite` has exactly the positive DeepMind conclusion under the finite hypothesis. An unconditional Lean proof of that finite hypothesis remains to be supplied. ## Three summands

Mazhar subsequently proved that if a finite-dimensional Gaussian set A has mass greater than 2/3, then A + A + A contains a compact convex set of mass at least 1/2 containing the origin. The notebook proves the countable compact-input mass conclusion from the finite theorem. More generally, `gaussian_sum_of_finite` preserves any fixed number of Minkowski summands and both mass thresholds. The specialization `gaussian_three_of_finite` uses the finite mass estimate as a hypothesis and gives mass at least 1/2 inside K + K + K, without a balance assumption. ## Deriving the convex witness

`green_54_of_analytic_inputs` derives the exact positive DeepMind conclusion from two finite analytic statements. The first is the compact-support case of the three-Gaussian theorem of Hua, Song and Tudose. A law below the standard Gaussian in convex order can be realized as the sum of three vectors with standard Gaussian marginals. The second is Kwapień’s comparison. For a continuous integrable convex function f and standard Gaussian G, the event `f(G) ≤ E f(G)` has probability at least 1/2. Both statements remain explicit hypotheses. [Lean proof](Green54ConvexTests.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S1) · [Mazhar’s use of Kwapień’s inequality, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14832v2#S2)

The argument follows Mazhar’s three-sum proof. Let F be compact and disjoint from A + A + A. Under any coupling with the prescribed marginals, all three coordinates belong to A with positive probability when γ(A) > 2/3. Their sum therefore cannot have a law supported on F. Triple decomposition implies that no probability law on F is dominated in convex order by γ.

Lean then produces **one** continuous convex function f with `f(x) > E f(G)` at every x in F. The separation lemma works on any compact Hausdorff space. If each test in a convex family containing zero is nonpositive somewhere, Hahn–Banach separates the family from the strictly positive functions. The Riesz representation theorem turns the separating positive functional into one probability measure with nonpositive integral for every test. Applying this alternative to convex functions minus their Gaussian means gives the required f.

Kwapień’s estimate gives mass at least 1/2 to the convex sublevel `f ≤ E f(G)`. Intersect it with a large closed ball and take F to be the part of the ball outside a small neighborhood of A + A + A. The intersection lies in that neighborhood, and the lost mass is bounded by the mass outside the ball. Compactness of the space of compact convex witnesses recovers the exact triple sum and the full mass threshold simultaneously. The finite proof also applies to any reference law with the stated decomposition and sublevel bounds. [Finite-dimensional derivation](Green54ConvexTests.lean) · [Compactness and countable transfer](Green54GaussianProjections.lean)

## Six summands from Gaussian decomposition alone

`green_54_of_triple` derives the exact positive DeepMind declaration with a single analytic hypothesis, the compact-support case of Hua–Song–Tudose’s three-Gaussian theorem. For any compact K of mass greater than 5/6, six Minkowski summands contain a compact convex set of mass at least 1/2. For symmetric compact K the input threshold falls to 2/3, and the witness lies inside the real dilation `2 · (K + K + K)`. Both bounds hold in the countable Gaussian product. [Lean proof](Green54SymmetricBarrier.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S1)

For a symmetric input, separate each forbidden point and its reflection from the triple sum using one convex test f. The function `g(x) = f(x) + f(−x) − 2f(0)` is convex, nonnegative, and zero at the origin. Reflection invariance preserves the strict gap above its expectation on the forbidden points. Markov’s inequality gives mass at least 1/2 to the sublevel at twice the expectation. Convexity gives `g(x/2) ≤ g(x)/2`, so this sublevel avoids the doubled forbidden set. Compact approximation gives the claimed witness. For arbitrary K of mass greater than 5/6, its symmetric subset `K ∩ (−K)` has mass greater than 2/3. Mazhar discusses even barriers and the cost of using Markov’s inequality in the finite-dimensional construction. [Mazhar, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14832v2#S3)

## Reduction to finite support

It suffices to construct a coupling for a finite-support target law dominated in convex order by the law of `rG`, for some `0 < r < 1`. Each of the three summands must have the original standard Gaussian law. Under this hypothesis in every finite dimension, `green_54_of_finite_laws` gives the positive DeepMind conclusion with its original thresholds. The finite-atomic coupling is the remaining analytic input. [Lean proof](Green54FiniteLaws.lean) · [Hua–Song–Tudose, proof of Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S3)

The reduction works for any reference probability law on a separable Banach space. Partition a compact support into finitely many measurable cells of diameter less than ε. Replace each positive-mass cell by its barycentre. Jensen’s inequality decreases every continuous convex test integral, and the replacement moves each point by less than ε. The resulting finite laws converge weakly to the original law. The joint laws with three fixed marginals are tight and weakly closed, so Prokhorov’s theorem makes them compact. Addition is continuous, hence the set of their sum laws is compact as well. This proves the equivalence of finite and compact support. Finally, contract the finite atoms by factors tending to one. Their laws are dominated by the corresponding contractions of the reference law, and weak closure gives the original target. [Hua–Song–Tudose, Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S2) · [Lemma 3.1 and the contraction argument](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10908v2#S3)
