# Colourings by threshold tests

Let a colour on the binary cube depend on the outcomes of $r$ real affine threshold tests. Choose one linear basis for all the tests and allow arbitrary real coefficients, offsets and regrouping of their outcomes. Some colour class $A$ has a linear subspace in $A+A$ of codimension at most the smaller of $(2r+1)(2^r-1)$ and $8r^3(r+1)^2$. In the chosen basis it is a coordinate subspace. The bound is independent of the dimension of the cube. Equality at a threshold counts on its weakly positive side. [Lean theorem](Green53ThresholdColours.lean) · [Pinned Green 53](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/53.lean)

Write the coordinates as signs. For each row in turn, fix its largest remaining coefficients in absolute value. Choose the signs so their contributions reinforce the current offset. The first row gets a block of $(2r+1)2^{r-1}$ coordinates, and the block size halves at each step. If fewer coordinates remain, fix them all. Every row with a nonzero free coefficient then has margin at least $(2r+1)M$, where $M$ is the largest absolute free coefficient in that row.

There is a second way to choose the fixed signs. Take $c=2r+1$, $u=r(c+1)$ and the union of the $2u^2$ largest coordinates from each row, or all coordinates if fewer exist. Let $M$ be the largest absolute free coefficient of a row. When $M>0$, each coefficient in that row’s selected block has absolute value at least $M$. Conditional on the other signs, the chance that the absolute offset falls below or equals $cM$ is at most $(c+1)\binom{2u^2}{u^2}/2^{2u^2}$. To see this, orient the large coefficients positively. Sign choices in each half-open interval of width $2M$ form an antichain, so Sperner’s theorem bounds their number. The elementary inequality $(v+1)\binom{2v}{v}^2\le16^v$ makes the sum of the failure probabilities over the rows strictly less than one. At most $2ru^2=8r^3(r+1)^2$ coordinates are fixed. This is the Erdős–Littlewood–Offord argument. [Lean interval count](Green53ThresholdConcentration.lean) · [Erdős, *On a lemma of Littlewood and Offord*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1945-04.pdf)

For any displacement vanishing on the fixed coordinates, split the free coordinates into those it flips and those it preserves. On each part, take an extreme point of the cube section given by zero row sums. At most $r$ coordinates lie strictly between the endpoints. Rounding those to their nearest signs alters each row by at most $rM$. Both ends of the displacement therefore give the same prescribed threshold outcomes, including ties in rows with only zero free coefficients. This is the floating-variables method of discrepancy theory. [Rounding proof](Green53ThresholdRounding.lean) · [Beck and Fiala](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(81)90022-6)

The $r$ coordinate thresholds in dimension $r$ have singleton joint cells. Their doubles contain only zero, so a uniform codimension bound must be at least $r$. Both bounds also apply to arbitrary linear changes of the common basis. [Coordinate lower bound](Green53ThresholdColours.lean)

## Dependent parities

A threshold can also take a weighted sum of parity characters. Write all the parity bits used by the tests as an affine map $x\mapsto Ex+h$ into $\mathbb F_2^N$, and put $s=N-\operatorname{rank}(E)$. The number of bits and the ambient dimension can be arbitrarily large. For any readout of $r$ such thresholds, some colour $A$ has a linear subspace in $A+A$ of codimension at most
$$
2r\bigl(2^s r(2r+2s+2)\bigr)^2.
$$
In particular, the partition conclusion holds when both $r$ and $s$ are bounded in terms of the number of colours. For example, two coordinate bases differing in at most $s$ coordinates after reordering use at most $n+s$ distinct parities and span the whole dual space. Their tests therefore satisfy the bound. [Lean proofs of the slice and partition theorems](Green53ParityThresholds.lean)

The affine image occupies a fraction $2^{-s}$ of the $N$-dimensional cube. In the interval-counting argument above, make the fraction of bad sign assignments smaller than $2^{-s}$. This gives an assignment in the image whose fixed coordinates provide a margin of $(2r+2s+1)M$ in each row. Rounding the remaining signs can take the provisional point outside the image. Keep the fixed bits and select a basis for the image of the free coordinates under the parity checks. Flipping at most $s$ of those bits returns the point to the required affine slice. The same flips repair both endpoints of any displacement in the linear image. The extra error in a row is at most $2sM$. Pulling the fixed-coordinate constraints back through $E$ gives the claimed subspace in the original cube.

## A polynomial bound in the number of relations

For $r$ thresholds and $s=N-\operatorname{rank}(E)$ parity relations, the codimension is also bounded by
$$512(2s+1)r^3(r+s+1)^2.$$
For fixed $r$, this gives a monochromatic sumset containing a subspace of codimension $o(n)$ whenever $s=o(n^{1/3})$. The same proof works on one affine subspace of codimension $d$ where the colour has the stated description, at an additional codimension cost of $d$. In particular the common-basis weights, offsets and final colouring may all be arbitrary functions of $d$ input parities. [Polynomial bound and affine restriction](Green53ParitySliceConcentration.lean)

The counting estimate concerns a real weighted sum of signs on a nonempty binary affine slice. Take $M>0$ real and integers $r\ge1$, $c\ge0$. If at least $(2s+1)\,8(4r(c+1))^2$ coordinates have weight of magnitude at least $M$, at most a fraction $1/(2r)$ of the slice lies in any closed interval of radius $cM$. Here $s$ bounds the rank of the parity checks. With exactly $2s$ heavy coordinates, $s$ independent checks can force the signs to agree in pairs. Using weights $+1,-1$ within each pair makes the whole row zero. [Slice interval bound](Green53ParitySliceConcentration.lean) · [Paired example](Green53ParityBlockDirections.lean)

Divide the heavy coordinates into groups of $2s+1$. In each group choose at most $s$ coordinates spanning the check columns, remove them, and choose a spanning set among the remaining columns. There is still a coordinate $j$ outside both sets. Its parity-check column can be represented on either spanning set. Adding the bit at $j$ to each representation gives two flips that preserve every check, with $j$ as their only common coordinate. Flip each pair independently and average over the original slice. [Crossing-flip construction](Green53ParityBlockDirections.lean)

Conditional on the first signs $x_i$, the second signs see weights $B_i+C_i x_i$ with $|C_i|\ge M$. At least one choice of $x_i$ makes each weight heavy. With $m$ blocks the fraction matching fewer than $m/4$ preferred signs is at most $4/m$, by the second moment of the match count. The ordinary Erdős–Littlewood–Offord interval estimate applies on the remaining signings. A union bound supplies the simultaneous margins, and the parity repair above realizes each required displacement. Meka, Nguyen and Vu give a broader theory for interval probabilities of polynomials of independent variables. The two flips per block permit this elementary calculation with explicit constants. [Two-sign estimate](Green53ParityBlocks.lean) · [Meka, Nguyen and Vu](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v012a011/)

Green asks the question for arbitrary finite partitions. Jaber and Kumar prove cases for Hamming balls in different bases under hypotheses on their radius or density. The bound above depends on the number of relations among the parities used by the tests. [Green, problem 53](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf) · [Jaber and Kumar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07918)
