# Colourings by threshold tests

Let a colour on the binary cube depend on the outcomes of $r$ real affine threshold tests. Choose one linear basis for all the tests and allow arbitrary real coefficients, offsets and regrouping of their outcomes. Some colour class $A$ has a linear subspace in $A+A$ of codimension at most the smaller of $(2r+1)(2^r-1)$ and $8r^3(r+1)^2$. In the chosen basis it is a coordinate subspace. The bound is independent of the dimension of the cube. Equality at a threshold counts on its weakly positive side. [Lean theorem](Green53ThresholdColours.lean) · [Pinned Green 53](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/53.lean)

Write the coordinates as signs. For each row in turn, fix its largest remaining coefficients in absolute value. Choose the signs so their contributions reinforce the current offset. The first row gets a block of $(2r+1)2^{r-1}$ coordinates, and the block size halves at each step. If fewer coordinates remain, fix them all. Every row with a nonzero free coefficient then has margin at least $(2r+1)M$, where $M$ is the largest absolute free coefficient in that row.

There is a second way to choose the fixed signs. Take $c=2r+1$, $u=r(c+1)$ and the union of the $2u^2$ largest coordinates from each row, or all coordinates if fewer exist. Let $M$ be the largest absolute free coefficient of a row. When $M>0$, each coefficient in that row’s selected block has absolute value at least $M$. Conditional on the other signs, the chance that the absolute offset falls below or equals $cM$ is at most $(c+1)\binom{2u^2}{u^2}/2^{2u^2}$. To see this, orient the large coefficients positively. Sign choices in each half-open interval of width $2M$ form an antichain, so Sperner’s theorem bounds their number. The elementary inequality $(v+1)\binom{2v}{v}^2\le16^v$ makes the sum of the failure probabilities over the rows strictly less than one. At most $2ru^2=8r^3(r+1)^2$ coordinates are fixed. This is the Erdős–Littlewood–Offord argument. [Lean interval count](Green53ThresholdConcentration.lean) · [Erdős, *On a lemma of Littlewood and Offord*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1945-04.pdf)

For any displacement vanishing on the fixed coordinates, split the free coordinates into those it flips and those it preserves. On each part, take an extreme point of the cube section given by zero row sums. At most $r$ coordinates lie strictly between the endpoints. Rounding those to their nearest signs alters each row by at most $rM$. Both ends of the displacement therefore give the same prescribed threshold outcomes, including ties in rows with only zero free coefficients. This is the floating-variables method of discrepancy theory. [Rounding proof](Green53ThresholdRounding.lean) · [Beck and Fiala](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(81)90022-6)

The $r$ coordinate thresholds in dimension $r$ have singleton joint cells. Their doubles contain only zero, so a uniform codimension bound must be at least $r$. Both bounds also apply to arbitrary linear changes of the common basis. [Coordinate lower bound](Green53ThresholdColours.lean)

Green asks the question for arbitrary finite partitions. Jaber and Kumar study Hamming-ball cases which allow different bases. The common basis is a hypothesis here. [Green, problem 53](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf) · [Jaber and Kumar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07918)
