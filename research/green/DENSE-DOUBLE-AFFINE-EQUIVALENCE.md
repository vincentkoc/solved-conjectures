# Affine equivalence of dense doubles with four-point fibres

Six row counts determine the affine equivalence class of a dense binary sumset in the following family. Set $s\ge5$, $n=2(s+1)+2$, $g=2s+3$, and $\alpha=\tfrac12-\binom{2s}{s}/2^{2s+1}$. Take $S=A+A$ in $\mathbb F_2^n$ with generator density greater than $\alpha$, largest affine dimension $n-2$, and $k$ missing points satisfying $4\min(5g+12,4g+20)<8k$. Equivalence allows every invertible affine map on the ambient cube. [Classification](Green51DenseAffineClassification.lean)

Fix a spanning odd circuit of $g$ points in the quotient of dimension $n-2$. In suitable linear coordinates the missing points form $g$ nonempty rows, each a subset of a binary plane. Record the numbers of rows of sizes one, three and four. In a row of size two, the sum of its two points is one of the plane's three nonzero directions. Record the number of pairs in each direction and sort these three numbers. Two sumsets in the family are affinely equivalent exactly when the two triples agree. Every occupied pattern of total size $k$ gives an actual sumset in the family. [Presentations and realization](Green51DenseFramePresentations.lean) · [Frequency criterion](Green51RowFrequencies.lean)

To recover the common plane from the missing set $D$, count the points of $D$ that remain in $D$ after a shift. A shift along the plane keeps both copies in a carrier of size $4g$, giving overlap at least $2k-4g>8$. A shift across the plane can match at most two circuit rows, giving overlap at most eight. An affine map thus acts by the same linear transformation in every row. Conversely, any permutation of the circuit extends linearly on its span. Independent row translations extend to one affine map because the odd circuit's sole linear dependence determines the constant part of the translation. [Recognition](Green51FourFibreEquivalence.lean) · [Extension](Green51FrameOffsetInterpolation.lean)

For example, in dimension 16 the carrier has 15 rows and 60 places. With 58 missing points, the two unused places lie in one row or in two different rows. This gives two classes. With 41 missing points, direct enumeration gives 136 signatures. To reproduce these finite counts, enumerate nonnegative integers $a,c,d,x,y,z$ with $x\le y\le z$, $a+c+d+x+y+z=15$ and $a+3c+4d+2(x+y+z)=k$. Here $a,c,d$ are the counts of rows of sizes one, three and four. The Lean results above prove the classification and realization. These numerical evaluations use the displayed integer equations.

The odd-circuit input builds on Geelen's binary matroid bound. Alon and Zamir give asymptotic counts for unrestricted binary sumsets. Green's question about the guaranteed affine dimension at arbitrary density remains open.
