# Two trace tests on the binary BCH curve

Let \(K=\mathbb F_{2^{2k}}\), \(k\ge2\). For a binary linear map \(L:K\to\mathbb F_2^2\) set \(A_L=\{(x,x^3,x^5,L(x^7)):x\in K\}\). Every \(A_L+A_L\) contains an affine \(k\)-space. There is a surjective \(L\) for which \(k\) is the largest dimension. Thus the minimum of the largest flat dimension over all pairs of tests is exactly \(k\). [Exact minimum](Green51TwoGold.lean)

Explicitly, take \(L(z)=(\operatorname{Tr}(z),\operatorname{Tr}(cz))\) for any noncube \(c\in K\). If the double contained a flat of dimension \(d>k\), the normalized flat would give one shift \(t\ne0\) and one \(d\)-dimensional subspace isotropic for both Gold polar forms with coefficients \(t^7\) and \(ct^7\). Each radical has dimension at most two. The orthogonal dimension formula now forces each radical to have dimension two and to lie in that subspace. Nonzero points \(r,s\) of the two radicals satisfy \(t^7r^3=ct^7s^3=1\), giving \(c=(r/s)^3\). The same argument shows that the ratio of any two nonzero trace coefficients of an arbitrary projection is a cube whenever the flat has dimension at least three and \(2d\ge[K:\mathbb F_2]+2\). [Cubic ratio obstruction](Green51GoldPencils.lean) · [Gold polar form](Green51GoldForms.lean) · [Coşgun, §2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ffa.2017.11.005)

For the lower bound, set \(t=1\) and restrict the two alternating forms to a \((2k-1)\)-dimensional complement of the direction \(1\). Their common totally isotropic \(k\)-space lifts to an affine \(k\)-space in the double. This generalizes the earlier six-dimensional construction and applies to every linear map of rank at most two. The odd-power labels come from the classical BCH construction. [Lower bound](Green51BCHFourForces.lean) · [Simultaneous isotropy](Green51TwoAlternating.lean) · [Gow and Quinlan, §1](https://maths.ucd.ie/~rodgow/alternatingnew.pdf) · [Bose and Ray-Chaudhuri](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(60)90287-4)

The witness has \(2^{2k}\) points in \(6k+2\) binary coordinates. Hence \(\operatorname{guaranteedMaxCosetDim}(6k+2,\alpha)\le k\) whenever \(\alpha\le 2^{2k}/2^{6k+2}\). At \(k=4\) this gives 256 points in 26 coordinates whose double contains no affine five-space. The optimum above ranges over the specified BCH projections. The full Green 51 problem remains open.
