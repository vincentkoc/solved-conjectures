# Affine flats in sums of product coordinates

Let Γ be the graph of a map from an $n$-dimensional binary space to $d$ output bits. Suppose each bit is a product of two linear forms plus an affine function. For every $k\geq0$, the sumset $(2k+1)\Gamma$ contains an affine subspace of dimension at least $n-\lfloor d/4^k\rfloor$. In particular, five sums contain a flat of codimension at most $d+\lfloor d/16\rfloor$ in the full $n+d$ dimensions. If the products have the form $x_{u_i}x_{v_i}$, three sums already contain a flat of dimension at least $n$. The monomials may share variables and the affine terms may depend on every input. [Lean theorems](Green50ProductFlats.lean)

Green asks whether ten sums of any binary set of density $\alpha>0$ contain a coset of codimension $O(\log_2(1/\alpha))$. If $A+A$ contains the image of Γ under a linear isomorphism to the ambient space, its fivefold flat maps into $10A$ with the same dimension. In the [quotient construction](FUNCTION-GRAPH-REDUCTION.md), the output dimension is at most $\log_2(1/\alpha)$. When the selected graph has product coordinates, this gives coefficient $17/16$. The graph of the zero map shows that a uniform coefficient below one cannot hold for the graph bound. The quotient construction for general $A$ leaves the coordinate functions unrestricted. The [general quadratic theorem](QUADRATIC-BOGOLYUBOV.md) improves the fivefold codimension to $d$ for every binary quadratic map. The threefold bounds above use the product structure.

## How one direction reduces the loss

Write $C_i(x)=(\ell_i(x),m_i(x))$. The polarization in coordinate $i$ is the alternating form on this plane, evaluated at $C_i(x),C_i(y)$. A plane of rank below two has identically zero polarization. For a full-rank plane, exactly a quarter of the inputs have $C_i(a)=0$. Averaging over $a$ gives a direction which leaves at most a quarter of the remaining full-rank planes untouched.

Keep an input subspace $H$, a vertical output space $W$ attainable in the polar products already used, and the set of untouched coordinates. Write $P$ for projection to those coordinates and $D=P B(a,\cdot)$. Restrict $H$ to $H\cap\ker D$ and enlarge $W$ by $\operatorname{im}B(a,\cdot)$. The input loses at most $\operatorname{rank}D$ dimensions. The output gains at least that many because $P$ kills the old $W$. At every newly reached plane the restricted inputs are orthogonal to the same nonzero vector in a two-dimensional alternating space. They are therefore pairwise orthogonal there.

After $k$ choices, at most $\lfloor d/4^k\rfloor$ full-rank planes remain. Kill the first linear form on each of these planes. Now the restriction of the quadratic graph modulo $W$ is affine, and the free vertical values over it give a flat of dimension $\dim H+\dim W$. A coordinate-monomial family allows the all-ones input as the first direction, which meets every full-rank plane at once.
