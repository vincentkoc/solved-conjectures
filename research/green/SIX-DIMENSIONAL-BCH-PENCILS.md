# Two-bit BCH projections over the field of order 64

Let \(K=\mathbb F_{64}\) and \(A_L=\{(x,x^3,x^5,L(x^7)):x\in K\}\) for a surjective binary linear map \(L:K\to\mathbb F_2^2\). Exactly **54** maps give a largest affine space of dimension four in \(A_L+A_L\). The other **3,852** give largest dimension three. These are counts of maps with two ordered output coordinates. Up to a change of output basis, nine of the 651 coefficient planes give dimension four. [Dimension counts](Green51SixPencils.lean) · [Nine planes](Green51SixPencilPlanes.lean)

Every map has unique trace coefficients \(a,b\), so \(L(z)=(\operatorname{Tr}(az),\operatorname{Tr}(bz))\). Surjectivity means that \(a,b\) are nonzero and distinct. Put \(C=\{u:u^4+u^2+u=0\}\). The maximum is four exactly when both \(a^{36}\) and \(ba^{35}\) belong to \(C\). For a fixed \(a\), there are two valid choices of \(b\) when \((a^9)^3+a^9+1=0\), and zero otherwise. Exactly 27 values of \(a\) satisfy the cubic condition. For instance the scalar trace with \(a=1\) admits a four-space on its own, but every independent second trace eliminates it. [Coefficient test](Green51SixPencilGeometry.lean) · [Scalar classification](Green51GoldSix.lean)

## Why only nine planes occur

A four-space yields a nonzero \(t\) and a four-dimensional subspace of the absolute trace kernel on which both forms \((x,y)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(ct^7xy(x+y))\), for \(c=a,b\), vanish. Each form has a two-dimensional radical inside that subspace. The two radicals intersect trivially, and both lie in the kernel of the relative trace to \(\mathbb F_4\). Their sum is therefore the entire relative-trace kernel \(T=\{x:x^{16}+x^4+x=0\}\). The trace coefficients annihilating every \(xy(x+y)\) on \(T\) are exactly \(C\). The converse lifts \(T\) back to an affine four-space. [Trace calculation](Green51SixTraceSpaces.lean) · [Lifting a flat](Green51BCHFlatDimensions.lean)

The coefficient plane of any successful pair is consequently \(h^{-1}C\) for a seventh power \(h\in K^\times\). The seventh powers form a group of order nine. Its nine planes have pairwise trivial intersections, and each has six ordered bases. This gives the same count \(9\cdot6=54\). [Plane criterion and disjointness](Green51SixPencilPlanes.lean)

The odd-power curve comes from the binary BCH construction. The polar forms use the classical trace description of Gold quadratic forms. The earlier two-test bound gives the minimum largest dimension over all projections in every even field dimension. The counts here classify the surjective projections in field dimension six. Green 51 asks for the guarantee over all subsets at a prescribed density and remains open.
