# Green 28 with distinct prime-degree factors

Both marginal laws are uniform if the shifted output mask has the following factorization over $\mathbb Q$. Its nonlinear irreducible factors occur with multiplicity one, and their degrees are pairwise distinct primes. Linear factors can have arbitrary multiplicity. The result applies to the original independent integer laws with arbitrary real masses. It allows composite widths and nonconstant summands on both sides. [Probability theorem](Green28PrimeFactors.lean) · [Pinned Green 28](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/28.lean)

For example, the support $\{0,1,2,3,7,9\}$ has mask $(1+X^2)(1+X+X^7)$. The quadratic is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. To check the septic, reduce it modulo two. At either root of the irreducible binary quadratic it takes the value one, since the root has order three. Also $X(X^7+X+1)-(X^8-X)=X^2$ modulo two, so it has no root in $\mathbb F_8$. This excludes irreducible factors of degrees one, two and three, which suffices for a septic. The two uniform laws on $\{0,2\}$ and $\{0,1,7\}$ realize the given output with one representation at each occupied integer. These explicit example calculations are by hand.

## The algebraic conclusion

Let $L$ list monic rational factors in descending nonlinear degree. Each nonlinear factor is irreducible of prime degree with constant coefficient $1$ or $-1$. Every monic **complex** divisor of $\prod L$ with a nonzero rational constant coefficient is exactly the product of a sublist of $L$. The proof allows intersections among the splitting fields. When $\prod L$ is the integer output mask, its constant coefficient is one. The unit-constant condition for its rational factors follows from integrality of the coefficients of monic divisors. [Subproduct theorem and integrality argument](Green28PrimeFactors.lean)

For a largest remaining nonlinear component of degree $p$, lift a Galois $p$-cycle to an automorphism $\tau$ of the algebraic numbers. Raising it to $(p-1)!$ preserves the full cycle and fixes every root of every smaller component. Split the given divisor into a monic factor of that component and a monic divisor of the remaining product. The latter has fixed roots, so its constant term is fixed. Their product is a nonzero rational, hence the first constant term is also fixed.

Suppose the first factor selects a nonempty proper subset $I$ of the $p$ roots. Write $u_j=\log |a_j|$ in cyclic order. The fixed product makes $\sum_{i\in I}u_{n+i}$ independent of $n$. Its first difference vanishes. The prime cyclic shift lemma forces $u_{j+1}-u_j=0$ for every $j$. The component's unit constant term then gives $|a_j|=1$. An odd-degree component has a real root, which would be rational. For $p=2$ a proper subset selects a single root, which cannot be fixed by the transposition. Thus the divisor includes the entire component or none of it, and induction recovers the sublist. Drmota and Skałba's conjugate-product work is a classical antecedent for the prime-degree step. [Cyclic shift lemma](Green28PrimeDegree.lean) · [Drmota and Skałba, Theorem 1, stated in Virbalas, Lemma 8](https://doi.org/10.3390/math11061485)

The polynomial conclusion distinguishes multiplicity in the nonlinear components. For instance $(X-i)^4$ divides $(X^2+1)^4$ over $\mathbb C$ and has constant coefficient one. Its cubic coefficient is $-4i$. This example concerns the complex divisor theorem; its factors do not give probability laws.
