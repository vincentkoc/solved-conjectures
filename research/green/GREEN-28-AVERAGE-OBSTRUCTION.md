# Which atoms can have less than maximal mass?

Let $P,Q$ be integer laws whose independent sum is uniform on its support. Write $A,B$ for their supports and $n=|B|$. If $a\in A$ has strictly less mass than another atom of $P$, then
\[
t a = a_1+\cdots+a_t,\qquad 2\le t\le n,\qquad a_i\in A\setminus\{a\}.
\]
The summands may repeat. Thus if $A$ has no such averages, both original laws are uniform. The theorem applies at arbitrary support diameters and with arbitrary real probabilities. Uniformity of the sum implies the finite-support hypotheses in Green 28. ## The exact obstruction on supports

If $a+b$ has only one representation, its mass equals $P(a)Q(b)$. Every other occupied sum $x+b$ has the same mass and receives at least $P(x)Q(b)$. Cancelling $Q(b)>0$ shows $P(x)\le P(a)$. A row with less than maximal mass therefore needs a competing representation at **every** $b\in B$. Choose one, $a+b=x+c$ with $x\ne a$, and move from $b$ to $c$. A walk in the $n$ columns repeats a column within $n$ steps. Summing around the resulting cycle gives the displayed average. A single summand would equal $a$, so every cycle has length at least two. Once every row is maximal, its positive masses have ratio one, and the checked rational-ratio theorem gives uniformity on both sides. [Cycle and probability proofs](Green28AverageObstruction.lean) · [Rational-ratio theorem](GREEN-28-ARITHMETIC.md)

Conversely, any average of length $t$ gives a nonempty opposite support with at most $t$ sites covering every cell in row $a$ by another row. Take the partial sums of the steps $a-a_i$. Their total is zero, and moving to the next partial sum supplies the competing representation, including the last step back to zero. The least possible number of opposite sites for this covering problem therefore equals the shortest available average length whenever either exists. This equivalence is purely about representations. Constructing probabilities for a uniform convolution imposes further conditions. [`exists_covered_iff`](Green28AverageObstruction.lean)

## Separated sites and unbounded opposite supports

Translate the left support so all sites are nonnegative. If every pair $a<x$ of occupied sites satisfies $n a<x$, no average of at most $n$ other sites can equal $a$. Including a larger site already makes the sum too large, while using only smaller sites makes it too small. This gives an unconditional Green 28 result even as both support sizes and both diameters grow. [`uniform_of_gaps`](Green28AverageObstruction.lean)

The same proof works with one nonempty subset $E\subseteq B$ in place of all $n$ columns if every competing representation of a sum in $A+E$ also uses a column in $E$. Equivalently, $A+E$ is disjoint from $A+(B\setminus E)$. Every walk started in $E$ stays there, giving the bound $t\le |E|$. For example, take any translated subset of $\{0,1,N,N^2\}$ on the left. If $|E|<N$, then both laws are uniform. This allows arbitrarily many opposite sites outside $E$ and arbitrarily large gaps on either side. Taking $E=B$ gives the result for every right law with fewer than $N$ sites. The isolation hypothesis is part of the theorem when only a piece is small. The general case of an arbitrary four-site marginal and arbitrary opposite law remains open. [`uniform_of_piece` and `four_site_piece`](Green28AverageObstruction.lean)

## Prior work and verification

The condition excluding averages through a prescribed length is usually called $k$-average-freeness. Kaveh and Khovanskii describe the unbounded relation between equality after adding a finite set and equality of convex hulls. The cycle equivalence here counts the least possible size of the opposite support when deleting a specified site. The probability argument uses the singleton-cell observation from the four-atom proof. Hasanbegović's Theorem 3 covers a palindromic marginal support. The separated-sites result here includes nonsymmetric supports.
