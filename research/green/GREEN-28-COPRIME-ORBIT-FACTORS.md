# Green 28 · Recovering the factors from coprime root orders

The uniform law on the sixteen distinct sums of $A=\{0,1,2,14\}$ and $B=\{0,3,7,11\}$ has a unique decomposition into two independent nonconstant integer laws, up to exchanging the factors and translating them in opposite directions. The factors are the uniform laws on $A$ and $B$. The theorem allows arbitrary real marginal probabilities and unrestricted integer supports. A point mass and a translated copy of the output law give the remaining decompositions. [Explicit classification and realization](Green28CoprimeOrbitExample.lean)

## A criterion for simultaneous factor recovery

Let $H=\prod_i H_i$ be a finite product of monic integer polynomials. Fix a prime $p$. In an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_p$, suppose $H_i$ is the product of the $d_i$ linear factors with constants $\beta_i^{p^j}$ for $0\le j<d_i$. Write $n_i$ for the multiplicative order of $\beta_i$. Assume that the $n_i$ are pairwise coprime and
$$
n_i c_i=1+p+\cdots+p^{d_i-1},\qquad c_i\le p.
$$
Over every characteristic-zero field covered by the Lean theorem, each monic divisor $f$ of $H$ with $f(0)=1$ is exactly a product of whole components from this family. This includes complex divisors. Positivity and a binary output mask are needed only for the probability application. [`subproduct_of_coprime_orbits`](Green28CoprimeOrbitFactors.lean)

Split $f$ into monic divisors $u_i$ of the $H_i$. Their coefficients are algebraic integers, so all the $u_i$ can be reduced by the same homomorphism to one algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$. Monicity preserves their degrees. Each reduced constant $a_i$ is a power of $\beta_i$, and $\prod_i a_i=1$. Raising this equation to the product of the other orders shows that the order of $a_i$ divides both that product and $n_i$. Coprimality gives $a_i=1$. The small-index digit lemma now excludes every nonempty proper selection of Frobenius factors. Degree preservation and monic divisibility lift this conclusion separately for each $u_i$. The two resulting polynomials model the original PMFs with explicit shifts and positive scales. The marginal coefficients are integers. Nonnegativity and binary convolution then force both marginals to be uniform on their supports. ## The sixteen-site certificate

For $p=3$, the signed roots of $1+X+X^2+X^{14}$ and $1+X^3+X^7+X^{11}$ have orders $2\,391\,484=(3^{14}-1)/2$ and $88\,573=(3^{11}-1)/2$. Their gcd is one. The proof verifies ternary repeated-squaring and inverse certificates for each prime divisor of each order. It checks the complete Frobenius factorizations from the two roots. The four possible component subsets give the two point-mass cases and the two orders of the four-site factors. Both four-site supports have minimum zero. Their translations must add to zero because the minimum of the observed output is zero. Each output has one representation in $A+B$, and `realized` proves the exact convolution identity for the two fair four-site laws.
