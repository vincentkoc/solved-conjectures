# Two affine types of sixteen-label witnesses

Let \(A\) have sixteen elements in a finite vector space over \(\mathbb F_2\). Suppose \(A+A\) contains an affine three-space and contains no linear three-space. Up to translation and injective linear change of coordinates, there are exactly **two** such sets. Both occur. Their affine dimensions are eleven and ten.

| Affine dimension | Construction |
| --- | --- |
| 11 | For each \(z\in\mathbb F_2^3\), take the two points \((e_z,0,0)\) and \((e_z,1,z)\), where the eight \(e_z\) are independent coordinate vectors. Translate by \((e_0,0,0)\) to put the labels in the even-parity hyperplane. |
| 10 | Take the image of the eleven-dimensional configuration modulo the line generated by \((\mathbf 1,0,0)\), where \(\mathbf 1\) has all eight horizontal coordinates equal to one. |

The Lean theorem `witness_iff` tests this property directly on \(A+A\). Theorems `large_witness` and `small_witness` verify the two constructions. The direction space of a nonempty binary set is the linear span of its pair sums, so its dimension is invariant under the allowed changes of coordinates. Theorem `distinct_types` uses this invariant to separate the two models. [Classification and models](Green51PairedTypes.lean)

## Why there are only two

Fix a represented affine three-space \(p+W\). Choose one representing pair at each of its eight points. The resulting labels are a linear image of the universal pairs in the table. They still represent the whole flat, so the sixteen-label lower bound forces them to be distinct and to exhaust \(A\). [Affine matching bound](AFFINE-FLAT-MATCHINGS.md) · [Universal paired labels](Green51LocalFlats.lean)

Consider a nonzero vector in the even-parity part of the kernel of that linear map. Write it as a sum of universal labels. Every such expression uses at least eight labels. Two distinct images cannot sum to zero, and a relation of weight four or six would put a linear three-space in \(A+A\). Adding the same affine function of \(z\) to both slots of each pair leaves the represented vector unchanged. Complementation is one of these shifts, so the shifted word and its complement must each have weight eight.

Fourier inversion on \(\mathbb F_2^3\) now forces every pair to have opposite bits. In particular, every nonzero even kernel vector has all eight horizontal coordinates equal to one. Two different such vectors would sum to a nonzero kernel vector with zero horizontal part. Thus the kernel on the hyperplane is either zero or a line. Swapping the two labels within selected pairs removes the vertical coordinates of its generator, giving exactly the diagonal quotient. [Even relations](Green51PairedRelations.lean) · [Binary character calculation](Green51Hadamard.lean)

The coding-theoretic background includes the uniqueness of the binary linear first-order Reed–Muller code with parameters \([16,5,8]\). Chen and Vinck prove the general parameter characterization in Theorem 2.1. The argument here keeps track of the eight original pairs and the affine images of the labels. [Chen and Vinck, *Notes on Reed–Muller Codes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.2062)
