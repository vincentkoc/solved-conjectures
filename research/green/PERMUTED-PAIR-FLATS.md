# One or thirty-one affine four-spaces at the first separation dimension

Two 32-point subsets of \(\mathbb F_2^{15}\) can have different numbers of largest affine flats in their doubles. One double contains exactly one affine four-space. The other contains exactly 31. Both avoid linear three-spaces. The earlier dimension and label bounds show that both examples attain the minimum ambient dimension and the minimum cardinality. The number of flats distinguishes the sets up to affine equivalence. [Dimension bound](FOUR-SPACE-DIMENSION.md) · [Counting theorem](Green51PermutedCounts.lean)

For every \(d\ge4\), the same construction gives two sets in \(\mathbb F_2^{\binom{d+2}{2}}\). Each has \(2^{d+1}\) labels and each double has maximum coset dimension \(d\). Their doubles contain exactly one and \(2^{d+1}-1\) affine \(d\)-spaces, respectively. The general Green 51 density question remains open. ## How a permutation changes the largest flats

Let \(q(x)=(x_ix_j)_{i\le j}\) on \(\mathbb F_2^d\), and let \(f\) permute that space. At each \(x\), put two labels over \(q(x)\) whose difference is \(\delta_x=(1,f(x))\). The differences fill one affine \(d\)-space. The lower label may have an arbitrary vertical offset. A relation on four or six labels would project to a short relation on distinct quadratic points, or would consist entirely of whole pairs. Quadratic moments exclude the first case. In the second, two distinct pairs have different differences and three differences have odd first-coordinate sum. This rules out linear three-spaces in the double. [Paired labels](Green51PermutedPairs.lean) · [Compact coordinates](Green51PermutedModels.lean)

Over a nonzero quadratic chord \(q(x)+q(y)\), the four choices of endpoints give a translate of the plane spanned by \(\delta_x,\delta_y\). Its two points with odd first coordinate recover the unordered pair. Any additional affine \(d\)-space therefore projects with a one-dimensional kernel onto a full quadratic chord family \(\{q(x)+q(x+t):x\in\mathbb F_2^d\}\), for some \(t\ne0\). Its common vertical direction forces \(f(x+t)+f(x)\) to be constant. With zero lower offsets, every such constant derivative also produces an additional \(d\)-space. This gives an exact criterion for uniqueness. [Chord planes](Green51PermutedChords.lean) · [Projection](Green51PermutedProjection.lean) · [Both directions](Green51PermutedTopFlats.lean)

For the single-flat example, take inversion on \(\mathbb F_{2^d}\), fixing zero. A constant derivative in direction \(t\ne0\) would force every \(x\notin\{0,t\}\) to satisfy \(x^2+tx+t^2=0\). Nyberg gives the stronger classical differential-uniformity bounds for inversion. The Lean proof here uses the quadratic root count directly. The example with \(2^{d+1}-1\) flats comes from the ordinary quadratic graph in \(d+1\) variables and its existing classification by common input difference.
