# Structure at the sharp missing count

Write $h_{2s}=\tfrac12-\binom{2s}{s}/(2\cdot4^s)$. Fix $s\ge1$, $r\ge0$ and $n=2s+2+r$. Suppose $A\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n$ has density above $h_{2s}$, its double contains no affine hyperplane, and its double misses the maximum $(2s+3)2^r$ directions. Then the largest affine subspace contained in this particular $A+A$ has dimension exactly $n-2$, for every $r$. [Exact dimension](Green51ExtremalPeriods.lean) · [Sharp count](SHARP-MISSING-COUNT.md) · [Pinned Green 51](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/GreensOpenProblems/51.lean)

The missing set $D$ is a union of $2s+3$ cosets of an $r$-dimensional subspace $P$. In a complement to $P$, the coset representatives form one minimally dependent binary circuit of odd size $2s+3$. The translations preserving $D$ are exactly the elements of $P$. Filling the cosets of $P$ that meet $A$ gives a set $B=A+P$ with the same double and density at most $h_{2s+2}$. At that upper density $A=B$, and $P$ is also the full group of periods of $A$. In the other direction, every subset of $A$ still above $h_{2s}$ has exactly the same double. [Periods and density](Green51ExtremalPeriods.lean)

For instance, if $n=16$ and $s=6$, reaching the sharp count of 60 missing directions means they are fifteen cosets of one plane of order four. An input with 25,904 elements then has exactly four periods. An input with fewer elements can have fewer periods even when its double is the same. The general guaranteed dimension in Green 51 remains open.

## Equality in the binary odd-girth bound

Geelen proves the sharp cardinal bound for nonaffine binary matroids in Theorem 1.1 and constructs extremal examples by iteratively doubling an odd circuit in Theorem 4.2. He credits Govaerts and Storme for the classification at odd girth five. The Lean argument derives the equality structure directly in a finite binary vector space, then applies it to the directions of a double. [Geelen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.5769) · [Equality theorem](Green51GeelenEquality.lean)

Choose a shortest odd circuit $C$ in $D$. The cardinality equalities force each slice parallel to its span to be a translate of $C$. To see how the centres fit together, take three slices whose labels sum to zero in the quotient. Represent the sum of their centres by an even subset $T\subset C$. Changing any three circuit coordinates leaves a word of length at most three, by minimality of the odd circuit. If $|T|\ge4$, change one outside coordinate three times. If $|T|=2$, change three distinct outside coordinates. Both bounds fail, so $T$ is empty and the three centres sum to zero. The centres therefore make up a complementary linear subspace. [Compatibility of centres](Green51CircuitCentres.lean)

After projection along the centres, two linear binary coordinates with distinct exceptional circuit elements separate the entire missing set from zero. Their common kernel gives the required flat of codimension two. At the upper density a proper period-coset enlargement would violate the odd-girth bound, which forces the original input to be periodic.
