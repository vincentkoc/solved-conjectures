# Green 28 and multiplicities of equal atom probabilities

Suppose independent integer laws $P,Q$ have a sum uniform on $M$ outcomes. Group the occupied sites of each law by equal probability. If every class on the left has size divisible by $a$ and every class on the right has size divisible by $b$, then **$ab\mid M$**. Both laws can have arbitrarily many mass values, support sites and representations of a sum. If $M$ is prime and the opposite support has at least two sites, the class sizes on the given side have gcd one. [Formal statements](Green28PrimitiveOutcomeNorm.lean)

Divide the weights of each law by its rightmost probability. All resulting weights are algebraic integers. The sum of the normalized weights on the left is $av$ for an algebraic integer $v$, by grouping equal values, and on the right it is $bw$ with $w$ integral. The rightmost output has a unique representation, so the product of the two sums is $M$. Thus the rational number $M/(ab)=vw$ is an algebraic integer and belongs to $\mathbb Z$. For the prime case, translating the whole left support by the smaller of two opposite sites misses the sum of the rightmost left site and the larger opposite site. Therefore the left support, and each of its mass classes, has size strictly below $M$. [Normalized integrality](GREEN-28-ARITHMETIC.md) · [Endpoint identity](Green28NormOutcomes.lean)

## A stronger divisor with two mass values

Suppose one marginal has $h$ heavy sites of normalized weight one and $t>0$ light sites of normalized weight $u$. Write $g=\gcd(h,t)$, $H=h/g$, $T=t/g$, and let $m$ be the monic integral minimal polynomial of $u$, of degree $d$. Then
\[
       g\,\big|T^d m(-H/T)\big| \quad\text{divides } M.
\]
For instance, six heavy sites and four light sites with $u=(3-\sqrt5)/2$ require $62\mid M$. Here $g=2$, $(H,T)=(3,2)$ and $4m(-3/2)=31$. This calculation gives a necessary condition for such a pair of laws. The theorem places no restriction on the opposite probabilities or the number of representations of any outcome. [Two-level outcome theorem](Green28PrimitiveOutcomeNorm.lean)

For a finite set of two-level sites the earlier full-mask norm theorem can give a larger divisor when the heavy and light site polynomials are coprime over $\mathbb Q$. The count formula above requires only their two cardinalities. At $t=1$ it recovers the cardinality divisor from the single-deficit case after changing from $u$ to $1-u$. [Full-mask norm and single deficient atom](GREEN-28-NORM-OUTCOMES.md)

## Exact criterion in the coefficient order

For any real algebraic integer $u$ and coprime $H,T\in\mathbb Z$ with $T\ne0$, set $\Delta=T^d m(-H/T)$. An integer $N$ belongs to $(H+Tu)\mathbb Z[u]$ if and only if $\Delta\mid N$. Multiplying the generator by a nonzero $g\in\mathbb Z$ gives the corresponding criterion $g\Delta\mid N$. The PMF application uses the fact that all opposite normalized weights belong to $\mathbb Z[u]$, by monic division. Their sum $v$ satisfies $(h+tu)v=M$. [Exact integer criterion](Green28PrimitiveOutcomeNorm.lean) · [Subring containment](Green28DoubleFibres.lean)

For necessity, write $v=p(u)$ and let $f(X)=(H+TX)p(X)-N$. Then $m\mid f$. The scaling $p(X)\mapsto T^{\deg p}p(X/T)$ preserves divisibility, so evaluating both scaled polynomials at $-H$ gives $\Delta\mid -NT^{\deg f}$. Monicity gives $\Delta\equiv(-H)^d\pmod T$. Coprimality with $T$ yields $\Delta\mid N$. Conversely, the scaled minimal polynomial vanishes at $Tu$, whereas at $-H$ it equals $\Delta$. The polynomial difference of these values is divisible by $H+Tu$ inside $\mathbb Z[u]$. The additional factor $g$ follows because a rational algebraic integer is an integer. Homogeneous evaluation of a minimal polynomial is the classical binary norm form. [Polynomial scaling](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/ScaleRoots.html) · [Murphy, *Polynomial selection for the number field sieve*, §2.1](https://maths-people.anu.edu.au/brent/pd/Murphy-thesis.pdf)
