# Common differences in quartic level sets

Choose $m$ Boolean polynomials of degree at most four on $\mathbb F_2^n$ and one nonempty level of each. There is one **nonempty affine flat** $W$, of codimension bounded only by $m$, on which every chosen level has many representations. For every $z\in W$ and every chosen set $A$, at least $2^n/128$ inputs $x$ satisfy $x,x+z\in A$. The sets may overlap or be disjoint. In Green 53 it already suffices that one colour class contains any nonempty quartic level, with the other colour classes arbitrary. ## When a missing pair determines the polynomial

Let $f$ have additive degree at most $e+1$. Suppose $f$ is constant on the nonempty support of a Boolean function $g$ of degree at most $e$. There is a set $S$ of at most $2^e$ shifts meeting the support of $g$ from every input. For each $s\in S$, record $g(x+s)$ and $f(x+s)-f(x)$. These have degree at most $e$. The first coordinates identify a shift where $f(x+s)$ is known, and the corresponding difference recovers $f(x)$. Thus $f$ is a function of at most $2^{e+1}$ lower-degree coordinates. [Hitting-shift lemma](Green53PolynomialColours.lean)

Call $z$ an antiperiod if $f(x+z)=1+f(x)$ for every $x$. If $z$ is not an antiperiod but one of the equal-value pairs $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ never occurs, $f$ is constant wherever $1+f(x+z)-f(x)$ is nonzero. The preceding argument applies. Consequently, above the stated rank bound, **both** equal-value pairs occur at every non-antiperiod. A linear subspace of codimension at most one avoids all antiperiods. Functional rank here means representation by a function of lower-degree polynomials, as in work on polynomial bias. The proof uses the missing pair directly. [Linear projection lemma](Green52CubicFibres.lean) · [Kaufman and Lovett](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.4535) · [Haramaty and Shpilka](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2853)

## A flat for a family

For a quartic, the rank threshold gives 16 cubic coordinates. When a level is controlled by those coordinates, translate one of its points to the origin and retain the translated coordinates. In the high-rank case retain a linear coordinate whose kernel avoids all antiperiods. Concatenating the data costs at most $16m$ cubic coordinates. Differences in the fibre through the origin then work for every original level. In the notation of the cubic horizontal theorem the proof gives codimension at most $H((2^{16m})^{2^{64m}})$. [Common cubic fibres](CUBIC-COLOUR-CLASSES.md) · [Definition of $H$](CUBIC-TRANSVERSALS.md)

For a fixed displacement, a specified pair of quartic values is tested by one quartic equation and one cubic difference equation. Its Boolean indicator has degree at most seven. The Reed–Muller support bound gives the factor $1/128$ for every realized pair. The bound applies to each original level regardless of the size of the auxiliary cubic description.
