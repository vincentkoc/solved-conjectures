# Green 33 · prime-order obstructions from power sums

Let `p = 2m² + 1` be prime and `m > 1`. A two-basis of size `2m` for `Z/p` would force `4ⁱ ≡ −m (mod p)` for some `0 ≤ i < m`. If `m ≡ 1 (mod 4)`, quadratic reciprocity rules this out. In particular every two-basis modulo 163 has at least 19 elements. The general [finite order bound](REFLECTION-RIGIDITY.md) gives 18 there. [Lean proof](Green33PrimeMoments.lean)

**The moment argument.** The [extremal profile](Green33ReflectionRigidity.lean) supplies a reflection center `s` such that `s−A = A`, no diagonal sum equals `s`, and the ordered representation count plus the diagonal count is 2 at every other point. Set `v(a) = 2a − s` and let `Pᵣ = ∑ v(a)ʳ` over the basis, with values in the prime field. All the entries `v(a)` are nonzero.

There is a first nonzero positive moment of degree `r ≤ 2m`. Otherwise, summing the evaluations of the degree-`2m` polynomial `F(X) = ∏(X − v(a))` over its roots would give `0 = 2m F(0)`, with both factors nonzero. Reflection pairs each entry with its negative, so `r` is even.

Weight the representation profile at `t` by `(2(t − s))ʳ`. The weight vanishes at the center, and its sum over the whole field is zero since `0 < r < p − 1`. In the binomial expansion of the ordered-pair sum, every interior term vanishes by minimality of `r`. The two endpoint terms give `4m Pᵣ` and the diagonal sum contributes `2ʳ Pᵣ`. Thus `4m + 2ʳ = 0` in the prime field. Write `r = 2(i+1)` to obtain the claimed congruence.

**The residue-class exclusion.** For `m ≡ 1 (mod 4)`, the Jacobi symbols satisfy `(m/p) = (p/m) = 1` because `p ≡ 1 (mod m)`. Also `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, so `(−m/p) = −1`. Every power of four is a square. This argument allows composite `m`, including `m = 9`.

**Attribution and scope.** The finite-field power-sum method is adapted from Dongryul Kim’s proof for perfect radius-two Lee codes. Kim’s Theorem 1.4 treats a different group order, `2n² + 2n + 1`. Here the input is the extremal two-basis profile in the notebook. The proof gives a necessary condition for exact finite equality, with no claim about the occurrence of primes of the indicated form or the exact minimum at 163. Green’s question allows a vanishing relative error along arbitrary positive moduli and remains open.
