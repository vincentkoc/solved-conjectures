# Rational power relations in Hosoya windows

Write U(n) = Fₙ + Lₙ₊₁ and D(n) = Fₙ + Lₙ₋₁. Fix a window width H and a finite set S of primes. For every sufficiently large starting counter n, take any finite selection of sites from **both** diagonals at physical indices n+2 through n+H+2. Give the sites any integer exponents eᵢ. For every positive integer k, their product equals a rational kth power times a rational S-unit **if and only if k divides every eᵢ**. The same starting threshold works for every choice of sites, signs and exponents, and for all k at once. An S-unit here is a nonzero rational whose numerator and denominator have no prime divisors outside S. [Window theorem](HosoyaPowerWindows.lean)

The Lean declaration `eventual_window_power_iff` takes two rational-integer specializations of published superelliptic bounds as the explicit hypothesis `QuadraticUnitBounds`. The underlying number-theoretic results are proved in Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 2.2 of Bérczes, Bugeaud, Győry, Mello, Ostafe and Sha. Their proofs are external to the kernel replay. The Lean equivalence also includes k = 0, in which case a product is an S-unit exactly when every exponent vanishes. ## Removing the dependence on the exponent

The two diagonal sequences have companion integers x satisfying x²−5y²=±44. Given a fixed finite set of primes S, suppose all valuations of y outside S are multiples of k ≥ 2. The prime factorization splits y as d·zᵏ, where d is supported on S and z is positive. Hence one of x²+44 and x²−44 equals 5d²·z²ᵏ. Enlarge S by the prime 5. The coefficient 5d² is now an S-unit, so its S-norm is one regardless of its size. [Pell characterization](LucasFibonacciDiagonalUnion.lean) · [Factorization and twist proofs](HosoyaSmoothTwists.lean)

Apply the published results with number field ℚ, the infinite place and the primes in this enlarged set, the two simple-root quadratics X²±44, and exponent m=2k ≥ 4. For each fixed m, Proposition 4.3 bounds the height of x using the S-norm of the coefficient. Theorem 2.2 bounds m when z has a prime divisor outside S. Finitely many fixed-exponent bounds then give a common bound for x. If z itself is an S-unit, absorb its power into the coefficient and use the fixed-exponent bound at m=4 with base 1. Thus sufficiently large y has, for **every** k ≥ 2, a prime outside S whose valuation is not divisible by k. Equivalently the gcd of all these outside valuations is one. The primes witnessing this can differ with k. [Uniform bound](HosoyaSmoothTwists.lean) · [Published hypotheses and bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09704)

## Recovering the exponent at an individual site

A prime shared by distinct sites within H steps divides the fixed nonzero product of their shift determinants. Add its prime divisors to S before applying the uniform bound. The remaining prime valuations of each sufficiently late site have gcd one and vanish at every other site in the window. Bézout’s identity gives an integer linear combination of these rational prime valuations that reads one at this site, zero at the other sites, and zero on all S-units. Applied to a product with signed exponents, this coordinate reads precisely its chosen exponent. Applied to a kth power it gives a multiple of k. The converse follows by dividing each exponent by k. [Shift determinants](LucasFibonacciIsolatedDivisors.lean) · [Valuation coordinates and power criterion](HosoyaPowerWindows.lean)

There is also a nondecreasing function R(n) tending to infinity for which, at every sufficiently late n, the theorem holds with width R(n) and with all primes up to R(n) added to S. This diagonal argument gives no numerical growth rate. The earlier unconditional bound gives an explicit width for square classes with a quantified exceptional set.
