# Simultaneous Lucas–Fibonacci divisors at prime indices

Let $U(n)=2F_n+F_{n+2}=F_n+L_{n+1}$. Fix a finite set $S$ of factor primes and an affine index $r_p+d_p n$ for each $p\in S$, with $r_p\ge 0$ and $d_p>0$. The same prime parameter $\ell$ must serve all the indices. The following finite test decides whether, for every choice of exponents $b_p$, there are arbitrarily large primes $\ell$ satisfying $p^{b_p}\mid U(r_p+d_p\ell)$ for every $p\in S$. The affine indices can coincide. [Lean proof](HosoyaJointPrimeIndexValuations.lean) · [Canonical sequence and prime-value question](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/2211.10788/LucasFibonacciSumPrimes.lean)

For each $p$, let $e_p$ be the least positive $t$ such that $p^2\mid F_{d_pt}$. Put $E=\prod_{p\in S}p e_p$ and $a_p=v_p(F_{d_p E})$. Simultaneous lifting at arbitrarily large prime parameters holds **if and only if** there is a single $s<E$ with $\gcd(s,E)=1$ and $p^{a_p}\mid U(r_p+d_p s)$ for every $p\in S$. The theorem includes $p=2$ and imposes no restriction on how $p$ divides its first Fibonacci return. [Finite test](HosoyaJointPrimeIndexValuations.lean) · [Single-factor test on unrestricted parameters](PrimePowerLifts.md)

## What a successful residue determines

Fix any $s$ passing the test. For any extra exponents $b_p\ge0$, put $T=\prod_{p\in S}p^{b_p}$. Among the parameters $n\equiv s\pmod E$, the simultaneous conditions $p^{a_p+b_p}\mid U(r_p+d_p n)$ select **exactly one reduced residue class modulo $ET$**. In fact any positive $E$ with $p\mid E$ and $p^2\mid F_{d_p E}$ for all $p$ gives the same class conclusion when $s$ passes its valuation test. [Class theorem](HosoyaJointPrimeIndexValuations.lean)

To see necessity, take a prime parameter larger than $E$ at the prescribed base exponents. Reducing it modulo $E$ preserves divisibility because each $p^{a_p}$ divides the corresponding Fibonacci step. For sufficiency, write $n=s+Et$. After a successful base hit, the first Fibonacci return modulo $p^{a_p+b_p}$ in steps of $d_pE$ has rank exactly $p^{b_p}$. Thus each factor specifies a single residue of $t$ modulo $p^{b_p}$. The Chinese remainder theorem combines them. Every prime dividing $T$ already divides $E$, so the resulting class of $n$ is reduced. Dirichlet gives arbitrarily large prime parameters in that class. [Sharp rank and lift](LucasFibonacciPrimePowerLifts.lean) · [CRT and prime-parameter proof](HosoyaJointPrimeIndexValuations.lean)

## Why separate tests do not suffice

For $r=0,d=1$, both $5$ and $31$ lift to every power at arbitrarily large prime indices. For $5$, the certificate uses $s=17,E=25$. For $31$, it uses $s=833,E=930$. Yet $155$ divides **no** $U(n)$ at any natural index. The reduction uses $155\mid F_{30}$ and checks the thirty residues modulo 30. The two successful single-prime tests cannot be combined at a common parameter even at exponent one. [Kernel-checked example](HosoyaJointPrimeIndexValuations.lean)

The pinned upstream declaration asks whether the set of **prime values** of $U$ is infinite. That question remains open. The [earlier prime-index result](PrimeIndexDivisors.md) chooses factor primes to force composite values. The criterion here takes the factor primes as input. Moree and Stevenhagen studied the density of divisor primes of the associated Lagarias sequence. Ching states the original prime-value question.
