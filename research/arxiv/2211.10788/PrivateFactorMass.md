# Private prime factors account for almost the whole logarithm

Let `Uₙ = 2Fₙ + Fₙ₊₂`, the exact sequence in DeepMind’s prime-values question. At sample size `N`, choose any starting index `r_N`, positive step `d_N`, and set `S_N` of `o(N)` primes. For the first `N` terms on that progression the **mean fraction of the logarithm contributed by primes in `S_N` tends to zero**. The set may depend on the entire sample. Multiplicities count in full, and the fraction at the initial value 1 is defined as zero. The theorem `FactorMass.canonical_sparse_mean_share` uses the upstream function directly. Infinitude of the prime values remains open. [Pinned declaration](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/2211.10788/LucasFibonacciSumPrimes.lean) · [Ching et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10788) · [Lean statement](HosoyaPrivateCofactors.lean)

On the two neighboring diagonals, parameter `m` denotes the upper value `Uₘ₊₂` and the lower value `2Fₘ₊₂ + Fₘ`. Let the radius and cutoff satisfy `H_N² = o(N log N)` and `π(Y_N) = o(N)`. For every fixed `ε > 0`, the fraction of sampled parameters `m = r_N + d_N i`, `0 ≤ i < N`, where either diagonal fails the following condition tends to zero. **Each diagonal value `u` has a divisor `c ≥ u^(1−ε)` whose primes all exceed `Y_N` and divide no other site on either diagonal within `H_N` parameter steps.** The comparison includes sites outside the progression and the other side at distance zero. For example, when `m = 9`, `H = 2` and `Y = 11`, the two values 411 and 212 have such divisors 137 and 53, respectively, with `ε = 1/2`. The earlier density theorem supplied one isolated prime on each side. [Private cofactor theorem](HosoyaPrivateCofactors.lean) · [Earlier isolation theorem](IsolatedPrimeDivisors.md)

For a fixed prime and exponent level, choose the first term divisible by that prime power. Each further occurrence gives a distinct positive index difference from the first occurrence. The prime power divides the Fibonacci number at each difference. Counting by levels shows that for arbitrary sites in an interval of length `L`, the product of their values divides their lcm times `∏_{1≤j<L} F_{d j}`. This removes the square on the Fibonacci product in the earlier interval bound. [Prime-power count](HosoyaPrimePowerLayers.lean) · [Earlier interval theorem](LucasFibonacciLargeFactors.lean)

Apply the level bound to the primes in `S`, weight each occurrence by `log p`, and divide each term’s contribution by its own logarithm. On `K²` blocks one can discard the initial `K` blocks using the bound 1 on the share. The remaining blocks give a uniform mean bound of `6/K + 10K² |S|/N` whenever `1 ≤ K` and `K² ≤ N`. For the private divisors take the excluded set to be all primes up to the cutoff together with all primes in the shift determinants. This set has sublinear cardinality at the stated radii. Markov’s inequality applied to the sum of the two shares gives the simultaneous conclusion. Bugeaud and Evertse bound the part supported on a **fixed** finite prime set pointwise for sufficiently large terms under recurrence hypotheses. The mean here allows the set, offset and step to change with every sample. Stewart’s work treats the greatest prime factor of recurrence terms. The private divisor above can have several prime factors.
