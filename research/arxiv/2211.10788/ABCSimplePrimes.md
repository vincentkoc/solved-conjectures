# Exponent-one prime divisors on the two Hosoya diagonals

Assume the rational abc conjecture. At each sufficiently large index on **each** diagonal, there is a prime greater than ⌊n/64⌋ that occurs to exponent one and divides no other value on either diagonal within ⌊√n/16⌋ steps. Here n counts from physical index 2. At counter n the upper term is `lucasFibonacciSum (n+2)` and the lower term is `lowerNeighborAtAddTwo n`. The window includes the other diagonal at its center. [Isolation proof](HosoyaABCSimpleIsolation.lean) · [Diagonal definitions](LucasFibonacciToralGaps.lean)

One consequence concerns products of neighboring values. Choose any nonempty subset of the sites on both diagonals between n and n+⌊√n/16⌋ inclusive. For every sufficiently large n, the product of the selected values is nonsquare. Choose one selected site. Its prime occurs once in the product because every other selected index lies within the isolation radius of that site. Thus all the square classes in the window are independent. [`nonsquare_products_in_windows`](HosoyaABCSimpleIsolation.lean)

## From the Pell equation to the prime divisors

A value y on either diagonal has a companion x satisfying x²−5y²=±44. The smaller of x² and 5y², together with 44 and the larger term, gives a triple a+44=c. Dividing by gcd(a,44) gives a positive coprime triple for y≥1. The gcd is at most 44. The finite-exception formulation of abc gives a constant K for each ε>0, uniformly over the normalized triples. Before division the product is 220x²y², whose radical is at most 1540y rad(y) since x≤7y. Together these estimates give y²≤C(y rad(y))^(1+ε). Taking ε small shows that rad(y)>y^(1−δ) eventually for every δ>0. [Radical proof](HosoyaABCRadicals.lean) · [Pell characterization](LucasFibonacciDiagonalUnion.lean) · [Pinned abc statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Wikipedia/ABC.lean)

Let S(y) be the product of the prime divisors occurring to exponent one. Comparing exponents gives rad(y)²≤y S(y), so S(y)>y^(1−δ) eventually as well. Discard primes up to Y and primes shared with another site within radius H. Their product divides the primorial of Y times the product of the nonzero shift determinants. This costs at most 2^(2Y+4(H+1)(H+10)). Since 2ⁿ≤y², the remaining product is greater than y^(1−δ) whenever 4(2Y+4(H+1)(H+10))≤δn and n is sufficiently large. The threshold is uniform in H and Y. Taking δ=1/2, Y=⌊n/64⌋ and H=⌊√n/16⌋ gives the stated divisor. [Product estimate](HosoyaABCSimpleIsolation.lean) · [Shift determinants](LucasFibonacciIsolatedDivisors.lean)

## Scope

The hypothesis in Lean is `type_of% @ABC.abc`, the exact pinned rational abc statement. The earlier unconditional theorem applies on a set of density one along each positive-step progression and allows larger cutoffs. The conditional theorem applies at every sufficiently large index and controls the exponent. Both permit the prime to be a proper divisor. Whether infinitely many distinct primes equal an upper value remains open. Ribenboim and Walsh studied abc and the powerful part of terms in binary recurrences. The window assertion here also controls reuse of the same prime across the two diagonals.
