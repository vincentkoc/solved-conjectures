# Square classes in windows on the Hosoya diagonals

On either diagonal, for every integer d≥0 and every block of 4·2ʳ consecutive indices, **at most 4+r values are d times a square**. The same bound works for every block location and every d, including a multiplier chosen after seeing the block. The upper and lower values at counter n are `lucasFibonacciSum (n+2)` and `lowerNeighborAtAddTwo n`. Both use physical index n+2. [Square-class count](HosoyaDyadicSquareClasses.lean) · [Diagonal definitions](LucasFibonacciToralGaps.lean)

There is an unconditional consequence for products on both diagonals. In any interval of N possible starting indices, put r=⌊log₂ N⌋, H=⌊√r/16⌋, and Y=⌊r/16⌋. Once r≥25,600, **at most √N starts are exceptional**. At every other start n, each of the 2(H+1) sites from n to n+H has a prime p>Y of odd multiplicity that divides no other site on either diagonal within H steps of that site. In particular every nonempty selection of values from the window has nonsquare product. The exceptional fraction is at most 1/√N, uniformly in the interval's location. [Odd prime divisors and window bounds](HosoyaOddIsolation.lean)

## Why only one residue class can split

Fix a scale D=4·2ʲ. Cohn’s dyadic Lucas congruence supplies a prime q≡3 modulo 4 such that shifting any Fibonacci recurrence by D reverses its sign modulo q. The Fibonacci recurrence itself gives F_D≡0 and F_(D+1)≡−1. If q divided F_(D/2), the identity F_(2m+1)=F_(m+1)²+F_m² would make −1 a square modulo q. Thus q divides F_t exactly when D divides t. Indeed q divides F_gcd(D,t) whenever it divides F_t, and every proper divisor of D divides D/2. [Signed period](LucasFibonacciSquareValues.lean) · [Exact-rank proof](HosoyaDyadicSquareClasses.lean) · [Cohn, *Some quartic Diophantine equations*](https://msp.org/pjm/1968/26-2/pjm-v26-n2-p03-p.pdf)

Suppose one residue class modulo D contains values d·u² in both of its lifts modulo 2D. The two indices differ by an odd multiple of D, so these values are negatives modulo q. They must both vanish modulo q, whether or not q divides d. Two zeros on the same diagonal at indices i≤k force q to divide F_(k−i). All such zeros therefore belong to one class modulo D. At most one occupied class can acquire a second occupied lift at each doubling. Starting with four residue classes modulo 4 gives at most 4+r at modulus 4·2ʳ. Reduction modulo this last modulus is injective on every block of that length. [Splitting and block-count proofs](HosoyaDyadicSquareClasses.lean)

## From square classes to prime divisors

Let M be the product of the primorial through Y and all nonzero shift determinants up to H, with all four choices of diagonals. For a site with no qualifying prime, each prime of odd multiplicity is small or divides another nearby site. In the latter case it divides a shift determinant. Thus the squarefree part of the site value divides M. Counting one square class for each divisor of M bounds the number of exceptional sites on one side in a translated block of 4·2ʳ indices by (4+r)τ(M). A union over both sides and the H+1 offsets bounds exceptional window starts by 2(H+1)(4+r)τ(M). The estimates M≤2^(2Y+4(H+1)(H+10)) and τ(M)≤M give the explicit √N bound above. At a good start, the exclusive prime of any selected site contributes an odd exponent to its subproduct. ## Scope

The prime supplied here may divide a value to exponent three or higher. The earlier theorem assuming rational abc gives exponent one at every sufficiently late site and a larger window. The earlier unconditional divisor theorem gives much larger primes and windows on a set of density one, with no parity condition on the exponents. Whether infinitely many primes equal a whole upper value remains open. Şiar and Keskin study related square classes for generalized Fibonacci sequences with initial values 0,1.
