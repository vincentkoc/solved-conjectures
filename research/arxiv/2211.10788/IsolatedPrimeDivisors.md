# Isolated prime divisors on both Hosoya diagonals

Fix integers r≥0, d≥1 and let n count steps along the progression of physical indices r+dn+2. For a set of parameters n of natural density one, **both values have a large prime divisor that occurs nowhere else on either diagonal in a growing window**. For example, each divisor may be required to exceed n√log n and to occur nowhere else within ⌊√n⌋ steps. The two primes at the center are distinct. The window includes comparison sites off the progression. [Lean proof](LucasFibonacciIsolatedDivisors.lean) · [Ching et al., §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10788)

More precisely, fix any real functions e(n), f(n) tending to zero. The size cutoff is e(n)n log n and the radius is ⌊f(n)√(n log n)⌋, with negative cutoffs rounded down to zero in the natural numbers. Oscillating functions are allowed. For each fixed choice the exceptional proportion among the first N parameters tends to zero. The prime on each side divides its value and divides no value at any other site within that radius, including the opposite side at the center. The two sides are `lucasFibonacciSum (m+2)` and `lowerNeighborAtAddTwo m` at m=r+dn, with comparison sites on the same physical-index domain starting at 2. [Diagonal definitions](LucasFibonacciToralGaps.lean)

## Controlling repeated factors

Take two sides s,t and an index offset h≥0. Form the determinant of the pairs of consecutive values at starting positions 0 and h. Moving both positions forward one step changes the determinant's sign, by the Fibonacci recurrence. Any common divisor of s(n) and t(n+h) therefore divides the absolute value D(s,t,h) of the initial determinant. Distinct sites have nonzero determinant, and D(s,t,h)≤2^(h+10). The same-site comparison is excluded. [Nonvanishing proof](LucasFibonacciToralGaps.lean) · [Divisibility proof](LucasFibonacciIsolatedDivisors.lean)

Take the product B over offsets 0 through H and all four choices of sides, replacing zero factors by one. Then log B≤4(H+1)(H+10)log 2. Among primes dividing B, at most π(N) are at most N, and at most log B/log N exceed N. Thus for H²=o(N log N), all primes that can recur in the window form a set of size o(N). Primes below a separate size cutoff Y can be added whenever π(Y)=o(N).

A varying set of o(N) primes can contain all the prime divisors of only o(N) terms on either side of a positive-step progression. The proof uses the short-interval product estimate from the upper diagonal. Its inputs are exponential growth bounds and the fact that a common divisor at positions i and i+j divides Fⱼ. On the lower diagonal the addition formula and coprimality of consecutive values give the same divisibility. Almost every term on each side therefore has a divisor outside the combined set. Taking the union of the two exceptional sets gives the simultaneous conclusion. [Short-interval estimate and density bound](LucasFibonacciLargeFactors.lean)

The uniform theorem uses H(N) and Y(N) for all the first N parameters. Taking the running maxima of the two cutoffs preserves the required limits and gives a theorem at the individual parameter n. Chebyshev’s estimate supplies π(⌊e(n)n log n⌋)=o(n).
