# Prime values excluded by quadratic forms on the Hosoya diagonals

Let `Uₙ = Fₙ + Lₙ₊₁` for `n ≥ 0` and `Vₙ = Fₙ + Lₙ₋₁` for `n ≥ 1`. A prime of the form **4x² + 11y² never occurs on either diagonal**. More generally, a prime of the form **x² + 11y²** never occurs on the lower diagonal, and can occur on the upper diagonal only at an even index and with residue 1 modulo 4. The variables in both forms are integers. The classical theorem on primes represented by primitive positive definite binary quadratic forms gives infinitely many primes of the first form. The Lean result proves the exclusion for every represented prime. [Lean proof](LucasFibonacciPrincipalForm.lean) · [Briggs on infinitude](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/an-elementary-proof-of-a-theorem-about-the-representation-of-primes-by-quadratic-forms/52E50F7ED4AF4D35D1D2EE52AA141903)

These restrictions distinguish occurrence from divisibility. For instance, 103 = 4·1² + 11·3² divides U₇₅ and never equals an upper value. Likewise 53 = 3² + 11·2² divides V₁₁ = 212 and never equals a lower value. Both examples are kernel-checked. [Earlier restrictions on prime divisors](ExcludedPrimeDivisors.md)

**Prime factors.** Every odd-index upper value and every lower value has a prime factor outside the form x² + 11y². To see this, write `a = Fⱼ`, `b = Fⱼ₊₁`. Fibonacci doubling gives

- 3U₂ⱼ₊₁ = (3a + b)² + 11b².
- 3V₂ⱼ₊₃ = (3a + 2b)² + 11b².

If a prime p is represented by x² + dy², then pm is represented if and only if m is. For cancellation, choose the sign of y so that the two coordinates in the product with the conjugate are divisible by p. Dividing the coordinates by p gives a representation of m. Cancelling every represented prime factor in either displayed identity would represent 3 by x² + 11y², which is impossible. At the initial lower index V₁ = 3 directly. At even lower indices the recurrence gives Vₘ₊₂ ≡ 2·8ᵐ modulo 11, a nonsquare when m is even. Finally U₂ⱼ = (b + 3a)² − 11a² precludes residue 3 modulo 4. Together these give the stated prime-value restrictions.
