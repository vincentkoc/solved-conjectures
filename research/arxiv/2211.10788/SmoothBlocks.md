# Large prime factors on the Lucas–Fibonacci diagonal

Write `Uₙ = 2Fₙ + Fₙ₊₂`. Choose any set of `R` primes, any starting index `r ≥ 0`, any step `d ≥ 1`, and any `N ≥ 1`. Among the `N` terms `U(r+dt)` with `N ≤ t < 2N`, **at most `2R²` have all their prime divisors in the chosen set**. In particular, the union of prime factors occurring in each such block contains at least `√(N/2)` distinct primes. Both bounds hold even when the prime set depends on the block. [Lean proof](LucasFibonacciSmoothBlocks.lean) · [Pinned upper-diagonal definition](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/2211.10788/LucasFibonacciSumPrimes.lean)

For each term supported on the chosen set, select a prime `p` whose full power `pᵛ` in the factorization is largest. Then `U(r+dt) ≤ (pᵛ)ᴿ`. If two indices `a < b` select the same prime, the smaller of their two powers divides both terms and therefore divides `F_{d(b−a)}`. The elementary bounds `2ⁿ ≤ Uₙ²` and `Fⱼ ≤ 2ʲ` give `N ≤ 2R(b−a)`. Thus a selected prime occurs at most once in each of the `2R` equal subintervals of the block. [Modular return-rank criterion](ProgressionDivisors.md) · [Factorization and spacing proof](LucasFibonacciSmoothBlocks.lean)

On every fixed positive-step progression, the proportion of `t < N` for which every prime divisor of `U(r+dt)` is at most `√(r+dt)` tends to zero. Summing the block bound costs one factor of `log N`. Chebyshev’s bound for the number of candidate primes through `√(r+dN)` supplies two logarithms in the denominator after squaring. The result concerns a prime **factor** of the term. Infinitude of prime values on the diagonal remains open. Stewart’s Theorem 2 gives a stronger almost-all greatest-prime-factor estimate for general nondegenerate binary recurrences. The argument here proves the explicit finite block bound directly for the pinned sequence. [Stewart, Theorem 2](https://uwaterloo.ca/pure-mathematics/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/onprimef.pdf#page=3) · [Ching et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10788)
