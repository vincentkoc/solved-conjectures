# Exact index–value gcds in arithmetic progressions

Write Uₙ = Fₙ + Lₙ₊₁ = 3Fₙ + Fₙ₊₁. Given positive m and d and any residue a, an executable finite test decides whether gcd(n,Uₙ) = d for some positive n ≡ a (mod m). Whenever the answer is yes, there are arbitrarily large witnesses of the form gq for one fixed g and prime q in one reduced residue class. Every sufficiently large prime in that class works. For d = 1, a negative answer rules out prime Uₙ throughout the progression. A positive answer only certifies index–value coprimality. The pinned question asking for infinitely many distinct prime Uₙ remains open. ## The finite test

Take the smallest positive multiple A of lcm(m,d) that divides the Fibonacci number at index A. For each 0 ≤ t < A with t ≡ a (mod m), put g = gcd(t,A) and record gcd(g,Uₜ). The answer is yes precisely when the table contains d. Any other positive common multiple satisfying the same Fibonacci divisibility gives an equivalent test. Strong divisibility of Fibonacci numbers makes the gcd of any two such multiples another one, so the smallest divides them all. [Least return multiple](HosoyaLeastReturnModulus.lean) · [Finite equivalence](LucasFibonacciExactGcdSpectrum.lean)

To see that the search for A terminates, take any even M ≥ max(4,lcm(m,d)). Then M! is a suitable multiple. If pᵉ ∣ M!, then the Fibonacci return rank r at p satisfies r ≤ p+1 ≤ M, since M is composite. For p ≠ 5 the rank is coprime to p, so rpᵉ⁻¹ divides M! when e ≥ 1. Raising the Fibonacci generator at index r to the power pᵉ⁻¹ gives pᵉ ∣ F at index rpᵉ⁻¹. For p = 5 use r = 5 directly. This proves divisibility by every prime power in M!. ## Why the upper bound on the gcd is attained

For n ≡ t (mod A), divisibility of upper values by any divisor of g is preserved because g ∣ F at index A. Thus gcd(g,Uₙ) equals gcd(g,Uₜ). Write A = gL and t = gh, with h coprime to L. For a prime q ≡ h (mod L), take n = gq. The only extra possible common prime is q itself. Earlier Frobenius identities show that if q ≠ 5 and q ∣ U at index gq, then q divides the positive integer H(g) = U_g(7F_g−U_g). Every q avoiding 5 and the factors of H(g) therefore attains the recorded gcd, including all q > max(5,H(g)). Dirichlet's theorem supplies arbitrarily large such primes. Conversely, reducing any actual witness modulo A preserves its gcd truncated to A, which equals d because d ∣ A. The zero residue is covered by g = A and L = 1. ## Two exact tables

In each class modulo 12 the minimum gcd occurs arbitrarily far out and divides every actual gcd. It is 3 in class 3, 2 in classes 4 and 10, and 1 in the other nine classes. Thus this common-divisor obstruction excludes exactly three whole classes at modulus 12. Modulo 168, exact gcd 7 occurs in classes 84 and 140 only, and exact gcd 14 occurs in class 28 only. All three fibers are unbounded. [Class theorems](HosoyaExactGcdClasses.lean)

Independent integer arithmetic tested all 2,176 class and level combinations with m,d ≤ 16 against the actual gcds through n = 120,000. All 458 inhabited combinations agreed, as did all exclusions.
