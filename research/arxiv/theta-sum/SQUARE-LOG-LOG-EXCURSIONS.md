# Square-log-log excursions for positive and negative prime theta residuals

Let \(R(p)=S(p)/p-\tfrac12\log p\), where \(S\) is the original signed integer double sum. For each fixed reduced class \(r\bmod q\), each sign \(z=\pm1\) and each \(0<a<1/3\), infinitely many primes in the class satisfy
\[
zR(p)\ \ge\ \frac{a^2}{64\pi^2}(\log\log p)^2 .
\]
This includes each sign in the class \(1\bmod8\). The fluctuations concern the secondary term. Strict positivity of \(S(p)\) at every odd prime and the pinned eventual lower bound \(S(p)>np\) for every fixed natural \(n\) remain open. The Lean theorems assume an ordinary prime-number theorem for arithmetic progressions with a possible exceptional character. ## Finite lower counts

There are absolute \(\eta>0,T_0,X_0\) such that for every integer \(X\ge X_0\), every integer \(T\ge T_0\) with \(2^{31}T\le(\log X)^{1/3}\), every reduced class with \(q\le T^\eta\), and each sign, at least
\[
\pi(X)e^{-2^{18}T}
\]
primes \(p\le X\) in the class satisfy \(zR(p)\ge(\log T)^2/(64\pi^2)\). Taking \(T=\lceil(\log X)^a\rceil\) gives at least \(\pi(X)\exp(-2^{19}(\log X)^a)\) at height \((a\log\log X)^2/(64\pi^2)\), uniformly for \(q\le(\log X)^{\eta a}\). The endpoint threshold in the last statement may depend on \(a\). [Uniform count](ThetaUniformExcursionEndpoint.lean) · [Square-log-log count](ThetaSquareLogPrimeExcursions.lean)

For an absolute \(\delta>0\), all sufficiently large \(X\), every sufficiently large real \(t\le\log X\), each \(q\le t^\delta\) and every reduced class and sign, the count at height \((1+\log t)^2/(16384\pi^2)\) is at least \(\pi(X)e^{-t}\). The lower cutoff in \(t\) is independent of \(X\). Combined with the unconditional upper bound, this identifies the order of the finite quantile height throughout the range of the upper theorem. In particular, for either one-sided moment in the class, the \(k\)-th root after division by **all** \(\pi(X)\) lies between \((1+\log k)^2/(16384e\pi^2)\) and \(D(1+\log k)^2\) for \(q\le k^\delta\) and all sufficiently large integers \(k\) satisfying \(2^{27}k(1+\log\log X)^2\le\log X\). ## Why an exceptional zero is compatible with resonance

Start with the compact modulus \(m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,T)^4\) and a reduced class with the desired signed model mean. Let \(f\) be the conductor singled out by the ordinary prime estimate at this endpoint. Set \(l=mf\) if \(f\le m^{769}\), and \(l=m\) otherwise. Then \(m\mid l\) and \(l\le m^{770}\). For each denominator \(d\le Y=l^{768}\), all residue classes modulo \(ld\) above a fixed reduced class modulo \(l\) share the same secondary main term. In the second case \(f>ld\). In the first case \(f\mid l\). The conditional model mean on the starting class is the average of its means on the reduced refinements modulo \(l\). Select a refinement whose signed mean is at least that average, at exactly the chosen cutoff \(Y\). [Choice of conductor](ThetaExceptionalConductor.lean) · [Finite unit-group projection](ThetaRefinedSignedMean.lean) · [Model transfer on a selected class](ThetaFiberModelTransfer.lean)

On \(N^{56}\le p\le N^{64}\) give each prime weight \(\log p/(64\log N)\). The predicted weight \(w\) of the selected class is at least \(\kappa N^{64}/(l^3\log N)\). The original mean-square approximation on that class costs \(O(w)\) since \(Y^{-1/128}=l^{-6}\). If the signed model mean is large, the sum of \(|R(p)|\) over excursions of the desired sign is \(\gg w(\log T)^2\). Cauchy–Schwarz and the unconditional second moment on the entire band give a cardinality lower bound. This keeps the prime-count normalization even for a strongly depleted exceptional class. ## Ordinary analytic input and literature

Drappeau and Fiorilli's Lemma 2.2 bounds the sum over \(q\le Q\) of the maximal von Mangoldt discrepancy after removal of the common exceptional-character term. At \(y=x\), take \(Q=\exp((\log x)^{1/3})\). Dropping the other nonnegative summands, the lemma gives an absolute per-class discrepancy \(O(xe^{-c\sqrt{\log x}}+Q\sqrt{x}(\log x)^{O(1)})\). The second term and the implied constants can be absorbed by decreasing \(c\). The exceptional main term, when its conductor divides \(q\), is \((x-\chi(b)x^\beta/\beta)/\varphi(q)\). The gap \(1-\beta\gg f^{-2}\) follows from the classical bound at exponent two and modulus \(f\). When there is no exception, use \(f=1,\chi=0,\beta=1/2\) and decrease the absolute gap constant if necessary. [Drappeau and Fiorilli, Lemma 2.2](https://sary-aurelien-drappeau.pedaweb.univ-amu.fr/documents/moment1-sw.pdf#page=5) · [Thorner and Zaman, equations (1.2) and (1.3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10878#page=2)
