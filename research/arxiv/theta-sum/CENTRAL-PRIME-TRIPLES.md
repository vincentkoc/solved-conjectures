# Vertex-disjoint representations by three theta-positive primes

Write \(S(p)\) for the upstream integer double sum and \(L=1+\log n\). Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), \(c<1/2\), and \(3/4<\theta<1\). Put \(R=n^\theta\). For every sufficiently large odd \(n\), there are \(\gg_\theta R/L\) representations
\[
 n=p+q+r,\qquad |p-n/3|,|q-n/3|,|r-n/3|\le R
\]
such that no prime is used twice, within or between the chosen triples. Each summand satisfies both \(S(p)>c\,p\log p\) and \(\lvert S(p)-\tfrac12p\log p\rvert<\varepsilon p\log p\). The threshold may depend on \(c,\varepsilon,\theta\). The number of disjoint triples has the same order as the number of primes in the interval. This is an additive consequence of the signed theta estimate. The upstream pointwise conjecture asks for positivity at every prime greater than five. [Exact original-sum statement](ThetaPositivePrimeTriples.lean) · [Pinned DeepMind definitions and conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/2501.03234/ArithmeticSumS.lean) · [Berndt–Bhat–Meyer–Xie–Zaharescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03234)

**Formal scope.** Lean proves the loss bound below unconditionally in this range. The disjoint-family theorem takes two explicit ordinary prime-pattern estimates. Both follow on paper from Theorem 1.7 of Matomäki–Shao–Tao–Teräväinen as detailed below. In the second Lean theorem the additional complete Kloosterman estimate of Weil lowers the theta threshold to \(\theta>1/2\). Combining it with the ordinary estimates gives the stated disjoint-family conclusion for \(5/8<\theta<1\) from the published paper, and for \(3/5<\theta<1\) from Theorem 1.8 of the October 2026 preprint of Matomäki–Pandey–Pliego–Teräväinen–Wang. A separate argument below using Matomäki–Maynard–Shao and an upper-bound sieve gives both ordinary inputs for every fixed \(11/20<\theta<1\). This yields the same optimal-order disjoint family throughout that range using Weil. The paper applications and the complete Weil bound remain external to this replay. ## Loss in the ordered count

Let \(A\) be the primes in the displayed window, \(T_z(A)\) the ordered triples summing to \(z\), and \(E\) the primes violating the centered accuracy test. At most \(3(|E|+1)|A|\) triples are lost upon deleting \(E\) and excluding coincident coordinates. Specifying one forbidden coordinate and one more summand determines the third. Each of the three possible equalities between coordinates costs at most \(|A|\). Translating the window into an integer interval gives \((|E|+1)L^m=o(R)\) for every fixed nonnegative integer \(m\). Hence the loss is \(o(R^2/L^m)\), uniformly over **all** integer targets \(z\). In particular the surviving ordered triples at \(z=n\) have relative density tending to one whenever the ordinary count is \(\gg R^2/L^m\). ## Packing and exact ordinary inputs

The first ordinary estimate is \(|T_n(A)|L^3\ge bR^2\). For the second, let \(d(p)\) count those \(q\in A\) with \(p+q\le n\) and \(n-p-q\in A\). The input is \(\sum_{p\in A}d(p)^2 L^5\le C R^3\), with \(b,C>0\). Each incidence degree in the ordered triple system is at most \(3d(p)\). Take a maximal family \(D\) of disjoint surviving triples. Its at most \(3|D|\) vertices meet every surviving triple. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives
\[
 |T_{\rm good}|^2 \le 27 |D|\sum_{p\in A}d(p)^2.
\]
Eventually at least half of \(T_n(A)\) survives. Cancellation gives the explicit \(|D|\ge b^2 R/(108 C L)\). With only the first input at log power \(m\), the simpler greedy proof still gives \(\lfloor bR/(500L^m)\rfloor\) disjoint representations. ## Checking the paper inputs

Both cited affine-form estimates apply on convex subsets of a translated cube \((X,X+H]^d\), with bounded pairwise independent gradients and offsets \(O(X)\). Take \(X=n/3-R-O(1)\) and \(H=2R+O(1)\), choosing endpoints to contain the whole window. For fixed \(\theta\) strictly inside the paper's exponent range there is room for its fixed positive exponent margin at both ends. Throughout, the form values being counted are comparable to \(n\).

- For the lower bound take the forms \(x,y,n-x-y\) and restrict \(x,y\) to the central subinterval of radius \(R/4\). The third value then lies in the full window and the area is \(\gg R^2\). For an odd target the local factor at two is \(2\). At an odd prime \(\ell\) the normalized factor is \(1-1/(\ell-1)^2\) if \(\ell\mid n\) and \(1+1/(\ell-1)^3\) otherwise. Indeed, among the \((\ell-1)^2\) ordered nonzero pairs remove those with sum \(n\bmod\ell\). These local factors give a uniform positive lower product, since \(\prod_{j=2}^\infty(1-j^{-2})=1/2\). Prime powers of exponent at least two occur at \(O(\sqrt n\log n)\) possible values and fixing one form leaves \(O(R)\) points. Their total von Mangoldt weight is \(O(\sqrt n\,R\log^4 n)=o(R^2)\). Dividing the remaining weighted lower bound by \(O(\log^3 n)\) gives the ordinary count with some \(b>0\).
- For the upper bound use \(x,y,n-x-y,z,n-x-z\) and restrict all five values to the window. This convex region has volume \(O(R^3)\). The five gradients are pairwise independent over every prime field. Inclusion–exclusion through pair intersections bounds each local factor above by \((1-5/\ell+10/\ell^2)(1-1/\ell)^{-5}=1+O(\ell^{-2})\). Thus the product is uniformly bounded above. Each prime configuration contributes \(\gg\log^5 n\) to the weighted sum, giving \(\sum d(p)^2\ll R^3/\log^5 n\). No exclusion of overlapping form values is needed for this upper bound.
- Taking just the form \(x\) gives \(|A|\ll R/\log n\), which verifies the claimed packing order. The classical almost-equal three-primes existence theorem by itself supplies neither of the quantitative inputs. [Matomäki–Maynard–Shao, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02017)

## Reaching the almost-equal three-primes exponent

Here is a second derivation of the two **ordinary** inputs. The theta deduction in Lean is the same.

**Lower count.** Apply Matomäki–Maynard–Shao's proof of Theorem 2.1 with \(x=n/3\), \(W=\prod_{\ell\le0.1\log\log x}\ell\), and their verified hypotheses for fixed \(\theta>11/20\). For each admissible ordered triple of reduced residues with sum \(n\bmod W\), Proposition 3.1 gives a convolution \(\gg_\theta (R/W)^2\). Its constant is uniform in the residues. Indeed their density margin, restriction bound and chosen Fourier tolerance are uniform, while the target after translation lies between \(N/2\) and \(N\), with \(N=\lfloor4x^\theta/(3W)\rfloor\). Each summand of the normalized indicator contributes \((\log x)\varphi(W)/(\alpha^+W)\). Dividing out its cube yields \(\gg_\theta R^2 W/(\varphi(W)^3\log^3 n)\) actual ordered triples in that residue fibre. Their endpoints, including integer roundoff \(O(W)\), lie in the full radius \(n^\theta\) since \(3^{-\theta}<1\). By CRT the number of admissible residue triples is \((\varphi(W)^3/W)\) times the product of exactly the three-form local factors above for \(\ell\mid W\). It is at least \(\varphi(W)^3/W\) for large \(x\). The fibres are disjoint. Thus their sum supplies the required lower bound without losing a power of \(\log n\). [Matomäki–Maynard–Shao, Proposition 3.1 and its application](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.02017#page=5)

**Upper count.** Enclose the three independent coordinates in an integer cube of side \(h\asymp R\). For a prime \(\ell\ge7\), let \(g(\ell)\) be the fraction of residues where at least one of the five forms vanishes. Single hyperplanes and their pair intersections have proportions \(1/\ell\) and \(1/\ell^2\), respectively. Hence \(5/\ell-10/\ell^2\le g(\ell)\le5/\ell<1\). Extend \(g\) multiplicatively on squarefree integers. Modulo such a \(d\), CRT gives exactly \(d^3g(d)\) residue triples where \(d\) divides the product of the forms. For \(d\le h\) each residue triple occurs \((h/d)^3+O((h/d)^2)\) times in the cube. Thus the sieve remainder is \(O(h^2d)\), uniformly in \(n\).

Use the Selberg upper bound at \(z=h^{1/20}\), sieving primes \(7\le\ell\le y=z^\kappa\) for a sufficiently small absolute \(\kappa>0\). All five primes in a counted configuration are \(\asymp n\) and survive. Its error is \(O(h^2\sum_{d\le z^2}d^2)=O(h^2z^6)\), since the squarefree divisors involved have \(3^{\omega(d)}\le d\). To lower-bound the sieve denominator, give each divisor \(d\) of the sieving product weight \(\prod_{\ell\mid d}g(\ell)/(1-g(\ell))\). The total mass is \(\prod_{\ell\le y,\,\ell\ge7}(1-g(\ell))^{-1}\gg(\log y)^5\) by Mertens' estimate. Under the normalized weights, the expected value of \(\log d\) is \(\sum_{\ell\le y,\,\ell\ge7}g(\ell)\log\ell\ll\log y\). Choose \(\kappa\) so this is at most \(\tfrac12\log z\). At least half the mass then has \(d\le z\). The sieve bound is \(O(h^3/(\log h)^5+h^2z^6)\), proving the five-form upper estimate for every fixed \(0<\theta<1\). The one-form case gives the corresponding \(O(R/\log n)\) prime supply. [Selberg inequality with its exact denominator and remainder, pp. 1–2](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~elkies/M259.06/sieve.pdf#page=2)
