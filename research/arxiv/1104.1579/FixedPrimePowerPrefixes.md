# Common irreducible prefixes at powers of a fixed prime

Fix any prime p, including 2. For any prescribed length L, **every sufficiently large odd exponent** e gives a common seed pᵉ(X−1) for which both Jones polynomial recurrences have irreducible terms at every index 0 through L over ℚ. More explicitly, if pᵉ > 2·16ˢ, both signs work through index 4s²4ˢ. The earlier theorem required the coefficient itself to be prime and allowed its base to vary. For example, from 2048(X−1) both signs work through index 256. [Fixed-base theorem](CunninghamSharedPrimePowerPrefixes.lean) · [Prime-coefficient theorem](SharedLinearPrefixes.md)

Jones studies integer polynomials under f ↦ Xf ± 1 and factorization over ℚ. His infinite-chain constructions use different families for the two signs. The pinned DeepMind predicates instead require prime natural terms under t ↦ 2t ± 1 and a nonprime term at the specified endpoint. The theorem here gives polynomial prefixes. Exact termination, an infinite common prefix for a fixed seed in this family, and the upstream integer infinitude questions remain open. ## Three coefficients in a factorization

Let F = AB over ℤ, with constant of absolute value pᵉ, where e is odd. Suppose F(0) divides its linear coefficient and p does not divide its quadratic coefficient. Then A(0) or B(0) is a unit. To prove this, suppose both constants are nonunits. Both are divisible by p, so the quadratic coefficient forces the linear coefficients A₁ and B₁ to be units modulo p. Since A(0)B(0) divides A(0)B₁ + A₁B(0), it follows that A(0) divides A₁B(0). The only possible prime divisor of A(0) is p, so A(0) divides B(0). Reversing the roles gives equal absolute constants. Their product would have even p-adic valuation. For e = 2r > 0 the factors pʳ + X and pʳ − X satisfy both coefficient conditions and both constants are nonunits. This example pinpoints the parity restriction of the factor criterion. [Criterion and even-exponent example](CunninghamOddPowerFactorCriterion.lean)

Every positive Jones iterate from a(X−1) has top coefficients a, −a and ±1. Reverse an integral factorization and apply the criterion to force a factor with unit leading coefficient. The earlier root estimate bounds the absolute value of the integral polynomial Xᴷ(X−1) strictly between zero and one at every root. A nonconstant factor with unit leading coefficient would then have a nonzero integral resultant of absolute value below one. The original prime-coefficient result follows from the same test.
