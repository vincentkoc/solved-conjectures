# Cunningham chain length and spacing from a dyadic prime count

Let P₂ be the primes at which 2 is a primitive root. Assume that for some b>0, every sufficiently large real x satisfies

```text
#{q ∈ P₂ : x < q < 2x} ≥ b x/(log x)².
```

Then every exact Cunningham chain of either kind with seed p and length k satisfies **k = O(log p · log log p)**. The constant is uniform in p, k and the choice of sign. The notebook also proves this bound under the pinned Bateman–Horn conjecture by applying it to just two linear forms. [Length proof](CunninghamDyadicSpacing.lean) · [Bateman–Horn implication](CunninghamSafePrimeCounts.lean) · [Pinned Jones predicates](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/1104.1579/CunninghamChain.lean)

There is a seed-independent bound for two chains. Use the center z=p+1 for a first-kind prefix and z=p−1 for a second-kind prefix. If both chains have k prime terms and their centers differ by d>0, then **k = O(log d · log log d)** as d grows. In particular, for a first-kind prefix beginning at p and a second-kind prefix beginning at p+h, d=|h−2|. At h=2 their centers agree. The spacing bound applies to distinct centers and only uses the prime prefixes. For small d the exact statement in Lean uses the positive function L(1+log L) with L=1+log(d+1). ## Prime counts and forced divisors

Write M(k) for the product of all base-two primitive-root primes through k. For k≥6 the prime prefix is shorter than its seed. Fermat gives a nonprime term at index p−1 for an odd prime seed, and the seed 2 stops before the sixth prime for both signs. For q in M(k), multiplication by 2 runs through every nonzero residue modulo q. A center nonzero modulo q would therefore give a prime term divisible by q and larger than q. Thus every center is a multiple of M(k), as is the distance between any two centers. [Forced-product lemmas](CunninghamArtinBound.lean) · [Termination](CunninghamUnboundedPrefixes.lean)

The hypothesis in the interval (k/2,k) gives log M(k) ≥ c k/log k for all sufficiently large k. For any positive multiple d of M(k), this implies k ≤ A L log k. The elementary inequality log k ≤ 2√k first gives √k ≤ 2AL, and taking logarithms gives the stated bound. Positive density for P₂ would give the stronger O(log p) result reported by Ford. The dyadic count used here is the base-two instance of Theorem 1.1 in the October 2026 preprint *Primitive roots for every admissible integer base*. Its analytic proof is an external input. The Lean declarations keep the count explicit. ## The independent Bateman–Horn implication

Let A(t) count n≤t for which 4n+1 and 8n+3 are prime. Both polynomials are irreducible and admissible together. For the Schinzel condition, n=0 works at all primes except 3, where n=1 works. Bateman–Horn gives A(t) ∼ C t/(log t)² with C>0. Subtract the counts at x/8 and x/5. After multiplication by (log x)²/x, the difference tends to 3C/40. Each surviving parameter maps injectively to q=8n+3 in (x,2x) for x≥10. Here (q−1)/2 is prime and 2 is a quadratic nonresidue. For q>3 the multiplicative order of 2 can divide neither (q−1)/2 nor 2, so it equals q−1. The proof obtains the dyadic lower bound with b=C/30. This uses a fixed pair of forms throughout. Jones’s two assertions of infinitely many exact chains at every requested length remain open.
