# A logarithmic Cunningham chain bound under Artin’s conjecture

Assume the primes for which 2 is a primitive root have positive density relative to all primes. Then there is a single real constant `C > 0` such that every exact Cunningham chain of either kind, with seed `p` and length `k`, satisfies **`k ≤ C log p`**. Ford reported the first-kind bound in joint work with Konyagin and Luca. The proof here treats both signs and uses the base-two instance of Artin part (i) exactly as stated in the pinned source. The density assertion and Jones’s two infinitude conjectures remain open. [Ford, *Prime chains and Pratt trees*, Oberwolfach Report 14/2008, p. 687](https://publications.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/3058/OWR_2008_14.pdf) · [Artin part (i)](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Wikipedia/ArtinPrimitiveRootsConjecture.lean) · [Jones chain predicates](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/1104.1579/CunninghamChain.lean)

Let `P(k)` be the product of the base-two primitive-root primes up to `k`. The divisibility statement is unconditional and only needs the first `k` terms to be prime, with `k < p`. For the first kind, `P(k) ∣ p+1`. For the second kind, `P(k) ∣ p−1`. Indeed, take any prime `q` in this product. If the shifted seed is nonzero modulo `q`, multiplying it by successive powers of 2 visits every nonzero residue in the first `q−1` terms. One of those chain terms is therefore a multiple of `q`, yet is prime and larger than `q`. The factors are distinct, so their product divides the shifted seed. [Full-period divisibility tests](CunninghamLocalPeriods.lean)

If the relative density is `δ > 0`, the primitive-root primes up to `k` eventually number at least `(δ/2)π(k)`. Chebyshev’s lower bound gives `π(k) log k ≥ (k log 2)/2` for sufficiently large `k`. Each prime above `√k` contributes at least `(log k)/2` to `log P(k)`. Discarding the smaller primes costs at most `√k (log k)/2` in this estimate. Thus `log P(k) ≥ (δ log 2/16) k` eventually. Every exact chain of length at least six has `k < p` by the quarter-seed classification. This gives `P(k) ≤ p+1 ≤ p²`, and one constant absorbs all shorter lengths.
