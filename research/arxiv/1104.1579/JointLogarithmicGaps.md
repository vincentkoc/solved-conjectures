# Simultaneous logarithmic gaps in Cunningham orbits

For every $c\ge 0$, infinitely many primes $p$ have two properties. First, the first $\lfloor c\log p\rfloor$ positive steps of *each* orbit from $p$, under $x\mapsto 2x+1$ and $x\mapsto 2x-1$, are all composite. Second, no prime reaches $p$ by any positive number of iterations of either map. The sign stays fixed along each orbit. One can prescribe an arbitrarily large $c$ before choosing the seeds. The earlier density-one theorem covered windows of size $o(\log p)$. [Main declarations](CunninghamJointLogarithmicGaps.lean) · [Earlier orbit bound](OrbitIsolation.md)

Given $c\ge 0$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there are a positive modulus $W$ and a reduced residue $A$ such that, for all real $s>1$ sufficiently close to 1,

$$
(s-1)\sum_{p\in G}\frac{\log p}{p^s}\ \ge\ \frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varphi(W)}.
$$

Here $G$ consists of the primes in that class satisfying both properties. The full class has limiting normalized mass $1/\varphi(W)$. The progression can depend on both $c$ and $\varepsilon$. The ordinary relative density of these seeds for a fixed positive logarithmic radius remains undetermined. [Mass comparison](CunninghamProgressionReturnMass.lean) · [Progression mass](CunninghamPrimeDirichletMass.lean)

## Covering both signs

Choose two disjoint finite sets of prime periods $\ell$. For each sign, require the sum of $1/\ell$ to be at least $T$, while keeping the combined sum of $1/q_\ell$ at most 1 for primes $q_\ell$ at which 2 has order $\ell$. To do this, take $\ell$ from the class 1 modulo $(B+1)!$, whose prime reciprocals diverge. Each prime divisor of $2^\ell-1$ has the form $q=1+v\ell$. If $1\le v\le B$, then $v+1$ divides both $(B+1)!$ and $q$, contradicting the primality of $q$ since $1<v+1<q$. Thus $q>B\ell$. This lets us discard many offsets while controlling the effect of the seed modulus on the sieve. [Prime orders and divergence](CunninghamPrimePeriodSupply.lean) · [Selecting periods](CunninghamThinPrimePeriods.lean)

The Chinese remainder theorem gives one reduced seed class modulo the product $W$ of the chosen primes $q_\ell$. At every site $j=i+1$ with $\ell\mid i$, the term of the assigned sign is divisible by $q_\ell$. For $p>W$ it exceeds that divisor. Each sign therefore has at most $e^{-T}H+L$ possible offsets among $0\le i<H$, where $L$ is its product of periods. The seed modulus satisfies $\prod_{q\mid W}(1-1/q)^{-2}\le e^4$, with the product over primes. [Joint CRT class](CunninghamJointOrbitCover.lean) · [Counts and Euler factors](CunninghamJointCoverBounds.lean)

## Counting the surviving pairs

For the seed $A+Wt$ and its term at site $i+1$, the two forbidden residues coincide at odd primes outside $W$ whose order for 2 divides $i+1$. The earlier orbit estimate shows that the order-weighted sum of collision contributions converges. Split that sum at a fixed cutoff. On any set of offsets $I\subseteq [0,H)$, the sum of the collision factors is bounded by $C_\eta |I|+\eta H$, uniformly in the sieving primes. The two-point Selberg sieve in the progression then bounds the number of prime pairs, summed over $I$ and $t<X$, by $X(C'_\eta|I|+\eta H)/(\log X)^2$ for large $X$. Choosing the cutoff before the covering periods allows the factor $e^{-T}$ to absorb $C'_\eta$. [Collision tail](CunninghamSparseCollisionAverage.lean) · [Affine sieve](CunninghamProgressionPairSieve.lean) · [Uniform pair count](CunninghamSparsePairScale.lean)

For fixed $W,A$ and large $X$, every seed in the count satisfies $A+Wt\le X^2$. Take $H=\lfloor 2c\log X\rfloor$ and apply the pair estimate to each sign. For any prescribed $\delta>0$ this leaves at most $\delta X/\log X$ returning prime seeds among the first $X$ parameters. Transferring the count to the seed variable bounds their normalized prime mass. The unrestricted ancestor count $O(N/\log^2 N)$ contributes zero limiting normalized prime mass. [Parameter count](CunninghamLogarithmicReturnCounts.lean) · [Ancestor estimate](CunninghamOrbitIsolation.lean)

As a finite check, assign periods 3, 5, 7 to the plus sign and 11, 13, 17 to the minus sign. The seed class 143058078054531 modulo 680510498507777 leaves 48 of 105 offsets per period for the plus sign and 1920 of 2431 for the minus sign. Direct modular iteration checked the forced divisibilities across four full periods for each sign. Separately, the prime 63737 has no prime ancestor, and each of its first 26 terms in both orbits has a prime divisor below 3000.
