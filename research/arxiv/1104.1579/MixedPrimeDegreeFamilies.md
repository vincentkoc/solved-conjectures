# Jones’s polynomial in mixed-prime degree families

For all independent natural numbers `u,v`, take Jones’s parameter `t = 42u+5`, constant `m = 2(2ᵗ+1)/3`, and degree `j = 3·7^(2v+3)−1`. Then `Pⱼ(X) = Xʲ + … + X + m` is irreducible over ℚ. The smallest pair gives `t = 5`, `m = 22`, and `j = 1028`. Every degree in the family has both 3 and 7 dividing `j+1`. This proves a special case of Jones’s polynomial Conjecture 4.1. The pinned DeepMind statements ask for infinitely many *integer* seeds of each exact chain length under the two recurrences. Both infinitude questions remain open. ## Two constraints on the same factor

Suppose `m ≡ 2 (mod 4)`, `j > 0`, and `D = 2ᵃ` divides `j`. Let `q` be an odd prime dividing `m−1` exactly once. Write `j+1 = Qd` with `Q = qᵇ` and `q ∤ d`, and assume `q² ∤ m+j`. If `j ≤ DQ`, then `Pⱼ` is irreducible over ℚ. This extends the earlier tests in which a single prime power accounts for a whole degree or its successor. [General criterion and families](CunninghamJonesMixedDegrees.lean) · [Prime-power cases](JonesPrimePowerDegrees.md)

In any monic integral factorization, both factor degrees are multiples of `D`. Modulo 2, a factor can initially have degree congruent to 0 or ±1 modulo the full 2-part of `j`. For even `j`, the factors have even degree, eliminating the odd residues. At `q`, multiply by `X−1` and use the identity `(X−1)Pⱼ = X^(j+1)−1+(m−1)(X−1)`. The reduction is `(Xᵈ−1)^Q`. A factor shared by the two reductions would divide the first-order correction; the evaluation at 1 and the hypothesis on `m+j` exclude the exceptional factor `X−1`. One of the original factor degrees must therefore be divisible by `Q`. Since `D` and `Q` are coprime, that factor is constant or has degree at least `DQ ≥ j`. [Dyadic and predecessor constraints](CunninghamJonesDyadicDegrees.lean) · [Factor degrees over a field](CunninghamJonesDegreeResidues.lean) · [Common-factor lemma](CunninghamJonesLocalFactors.lean)

For the displayed family, `m ≡ 22 (mod 49)`, `4 ∣ j`, and `j < 4·7^(2v+3)`. More generally, for every admissible coefficient pair `m,q` above and every bound `R`, there are `s > max(1,R)` coprime to `q` and `L > 0` such that all degrees `s·q^(L(k+1)+1)−1` give irreducible polynomials. Choose a large power of two `D ≡ 1 (mod q²)`, write `D = 1+q²z`, and set `s = 1+q(q−1)z`. Then `sq = 1+(q−1)D` and `s < D`. Euler’s theorem gives the required exponents for `L = φ(D)`.
