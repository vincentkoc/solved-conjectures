Start with T₀ = 2(X−1) and put Tₙ₊₁ = XTₙ + ε. Over ℚ, every plus iterate is irreducible. The minus iterate is irreducible exactly when n ≠ 3. The minus chain therefore has exact length three, and irreducibility resumes at every index from four onward. Its exceptional term factors as (X²−X−1)(2X²+1). ## How the search covers every degree

For n ≥ 3 put N = n+2, R(G) = XᴺG(1/X), and F = −ε(X−1)Tₙ. The constant coefficient of F is 1. In descending degree its three leading coefficients are (−2, 4, −3) for plus and (2, −4, 1) for minus. Every other coefficient vanishes. An integral G with GR(G) = FR(F) has the same sum of squared coefficients as F, namely 30 or 22. Its two outer coefficients multiply to ±2. Reversing and changing sign if necessary makes the constant coefficient 1 and fixes the leading coefficient. [Sparse identity and correlations](CunninghamBalancedTwoCoefficients.lean) · [Endpoint normalization](CunninghamSmallestBalancedChains.lean)

Read G from both ends. At each step the next low coefficient lies between −5 and 5. The next coefficient of the reflected product then fixes the high coefficient. Branches exceeding the remaining sum-of-squares budget drop out. Once the three prescribed high coefficients match, they and the constant term consume the full budget and determine G. When the ends meet, F(1) = 0 fixes any single middle coefficient. The search checks the remaining budget and the two correlations at lags one and two. At depth 30 for plus and 20 for minus, the kernel verifies that every branch has matched or has been ruled out. The minus search excludes N = 5, precisely the displayed factorization. The soundness proof follows a hypothetical G through these checks for arbitrary N.
