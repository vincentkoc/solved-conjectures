# The missing base three in Jones’s fourth parameter

Let `Pⱼ(X) = 342 + X + ⋯ + Xʲ`. For **every prime q < 100,000 and every natural b**, P at degree qᵇ is irreducible over ℚ if and only if qᵇ ≠ 9. This completes the prime-power classification in that base range. The previous theorem omitted powers of three, and P₉ has the root −2. [Classification](CunninghamJonesTernaryDegrees.lean) · [Earlier prime-base theorem](JonesCommonOrderDegrees.md)

The proof also covers degree `3ᵏd` for k ≥ 3, with 3 ∤ d, whenever 3 has even multiplicative order modulo each prime dividing d. In particular it covers `3ᵏ·7ᵃ·17ᵇ` for all a,b ≥ 0 and k ≥ 3. The orders at 7 and 17 are 6 and 16. Degrees 729 and 9,639 both lie in the previously surviving class 9 modulo 90. [Mixed-degree theorem](CunninghamJonesTernaryDegrees.lean) · [Complementary-constant congruences](JonesComplementaryOrders.md)

## Two coefficient positions determine the possible multiplicities

This part works for any natural constant m divisible by 9 with 27 ∤ m. Set `j = 3ᵏd`, k ≥ 3, 3 ∤ d, and `h = 3ᵏ⁻¹`. For every monic integral factor A in a monic factorization of `m + X + ⋯ + Xʲ`, the root 1 in its reduction modulo 3 has multiplicity in the set **{0, h−1, 2h, 3h−1}**. Thus every such multiplicity is even. [Multiplicity theorem](CunninghamJonesTernaryMultiplicity.lean)

Translate X to 1+Y and write T for the resulting polynomial. Multiplying by Y gives `mY + (1+Y)((1+Y)ʲ−1)`. Its first surviving coefficient in T modulo 3 is at position 3h−1. Modulo 9 it is at h−1. Indeed `(1+Y)ʰ ≡ 1+Yʰ (mod 3)`, and cubing two polynomials equal modulo 3 makes them equal modulo 9. In `(1+Yʰ)³ᵈ` the first positive coefficient is 3d at position h. [Translated coefficient proof](CunninghamJonesTranslatedCoefficients.lean)

For a factorization T = AB, let e and f be the first positions surviving modulo 3 in the two factors. They sum to 3h−1. If both are positive, the constants of A and B are divisible by 3 exactly once, since their product is m+j, divisible by 9 but not 27. Below min(e,f) every coefficient of the product is divisible by 9. If e < f, the coefficient at e equals AₑB₀ modulo 9 and survives. The same argument works when f < e. Equality e = f would place the first surviving product coefficient at or beyond the midpoint, whereas h−1 is strictly smaller. Thus the shorter positive multiplicity equals h−1 and the other equals 2h. This coefficient argument is a special case of the product behavior behind Newton polygons. [Product lemma](CunninghamJonesMultiplicityProduct.lean) · [Newton polygons and polynomial factors](https://people.math.sc.edu/filaseta/papers/besselpaper.pdf)

## Applying the multiplicities at constant 342

Choose the ordering of a hypothetical nontrivial split so that 3 divides B(0). Modulo 3 the complementary factor A divides Xʲ−1. After removing the factor at 1 from A, each remaining irreducible component has even degree by the order hypothesis. Adding the even multiplicity at 1 makes deg A even. The complementary resultant congruence now forces A(0)ʲ ≡ 1 modulo 3. Since j is odd, A(0) ≡ 1 modulo 3. Both constants exceed one, so the only possibility is A(0) = 19 and B(0) = 18. But 18 has order 30 modulo 341 and the factor-constant congruence requires that order to divide j+1. This is impossible since 3 divides j.
