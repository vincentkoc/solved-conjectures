# Exact Cunningham chain counts under Bateman–Horn

For a fixed positive length `k`, let `N₊(x)` and `N₋(x)` count seeds at most `x` that generate exact first-kind and second-kind Cunningham chains, respectively. **Under the pinned Bateman–Horn conjecture, both counts are asymptotic to `Cₖ x/(log x)ᵏ` with the same constant `Cₖ > 0`.** The Euler product defining `Cₖ` converges to a positive limit unconditionally. Kanado’s introduction records the classical Bateman–Horn prediction for first-kind chains of length at least `k`. The theorem here counts both of Jones’s exact stopping predicates. [Kanado, introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.07650#page=1) · [Jones predicates](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/1104.1579/CunninghamChain.lean) · [Bateman–Horn statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Wikipedia/BatemanHornConjecture.lean)

For each sign `s = ±1` the first `k` chain terms are the linear forms `2ⁱX + s(2ⁱ−1)` for `0 ≤ i < k` at positive seeds. They are distinct by their slopes, each is primitive and irreducible, and the residue `−s` avoids all roots at every prime. At zero the first form already fails primality. This verifies the upstream polynomial hypotheses and relates the count using integer absolute values to the natural-number recurrence, including its subtraction convention for the second kind.

Negation carries the roots for one sign onto the roots for the other. The number `ω(q)` of forbidden residues at a prime `q` is **one at two** and **`min(k, ord_q(2))` at every odd prime**. For the first kind at an odd prime, the forbidden seeds are `2⁻ⁱ−1`. Once `q > 2ᵏ`, their count is `k`, since a shorter period would make `q` divide a positive integer `2ⁱ−1 < q`. The local factor is `(1−1/q)⁻ᵏ(1−ω(q)/q)`. Every factor is positive. For all sufficiently large primes, Bernoulli’s inequality bounds its distance from one by `2k²/q²`. This summable bound proves convergence to a positive limit for the ordered partial products in the canonical statement. [Lean proof](CunninghamBatemanHorn.lean)

For each sign, the exact count is the difference between the counts of prime prefixes of lengths `k` and `k+1`. Under Bateman–Horn the latter contributes a lower-order term, because their predicted scales differ by a factor of `log x`. Uniqueness of the limit for the identical partial products gives the shared constant. The two Jones infinitude conjectures remain open unconditionally.

The six exported declarations in `CunninghamChain` are `chainForms_omega_neg`, `chainForms_omega_two`, `chainForms_omega_odd`, `chainForms_partialProduct_neg`, `chainForms_positive_euler_limit` and `exact_counts_under_bateman_horn`. Only the last takes the type of the upstream Bateman–Horn conjecture as a hypothesis. All six use only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
