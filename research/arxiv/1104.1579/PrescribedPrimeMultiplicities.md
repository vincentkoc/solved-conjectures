# Prescribed prime multiplicities for infinite Jones chains

Choose any finite nonempty list of positive exponents `e₀,…,eₙ` and a cutoff `B`. Infinitely many coefficients `m` have exactly the factorization `q₀^e₀ ⋯ qₙ^eₙ`, on increasing primes above `B`, and give an infinite polynomial Cunningham chain of the first kind. For the same `m`, every odd positive `h` works simultaneously: all iterates from `mʰX+1` under `f ↦ Xf+1` are irreducible over ℚ. For a single prime base the result is stronger. **Every** prime-power coefficient gives an infinite chain. [Main declarations](CunninghamPrescribedLinearChains.lean) · [Jones, Corollary 1.6 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.1579) · [Squarefree coefficients](SquarefreeLinearChains.md)

## Why multiplicities stay together

The reciprocal of the iterate of degree `j` is `Pⱼ = Xʲ+⋯+X+m`. In a nontrivial monic integral factorization, the two constant terms `u,v > 1` satisfy `uv=m`. The complementary-factor congruence gives `u^(2j) ≡ 1 mod v`, so `u,v` are coprime. Each prime power in the coefficient must therefore go wholly to one side of the split. A prime-power coefficient has no such split. [Coprime-split criterion](CunninghamJonesSplitCriterion.lean) · [Complementary congruence](JonesComplementaryOrders.md)

As in the squarefree case, an odd constant `u` that differs from 1 modulo a power of two dividing `m−1` forces `j+1` to be even. If `u² ≡ −1` modulo an odd prime dividing the other constant, then `j` must also be even. If this rules out every coprime split of `m`, it rules out every coprime split of `mʰ` for odd `h`. Indeed, each such split consists of the `h`th powers of a coprime split of `m`. Raising to an odd power preserves both the nontrivial residue in the group of units modulo a power of two and the square of order two at the opposite prime. [Odd-power proof](CunninghamJonesUnitaryPowers.lean)

## Constructing the primes

Let `E` be the product of the requested exponents and put `G=4E+1`, `c=Gᴱ`. Choose a power of two `W=2^(k+1)>cⁿ` with `2ᵏ≥n`. The first `n` prime-power blocks have residue `c` modulo `W`, and the last block has residue `c^(2ᵏ−n)`. The full product has residue 1 by Euler’s theorem, while every nonempty subproduct omitting the last block has residue `cˡ` with `1 ≤ l ≤ n`, strictly between 1 and `W`. Since `eᵢ` divides `E`, these block residues come from explicit powers of `G` prescribed for the primes themselves modulo `4EW`. [Prime-power residues](CunninghamJonesPrescribedBlocks.lean)

In particular, `qᵢ ≡ 1 mod 4E`. The cyclic group modulo `qᵢ` contains an element of order `4eᵢ₊₁`. Prescribe that residue for `qᵢ₊₁`, so the square of the next **whole block** is −1 modulo `qᵢ`. Every later prime is 1 modulo `qᵢ`. The Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet’s theorem provide an arbitrarily large prime at each step. For any partition, take the side excluding `q₀`. Its first member has a predecessor on the opposite side, and reduction modulo that predecessor gives the required square of −1.
