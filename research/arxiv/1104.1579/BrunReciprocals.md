# Reciprocal sums for Cunningham chain seeds

For either sign, the sum of `1/p` over primes `p` for which `2p ± 1` is also prime converges. For Jones’s **exact-length** sets, the reciprocal sum diverges at length one and converges at every length at least two. The length-zero set is empty. These statements are unconditional. Wagstaff proved explicit bounds for the first-kind sum over Sophie Germain primes. The notebook formalizes a sieve proof for both signs and applies it to the pinned stopping predicates. [Wagstaff (2021)](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL24/Wagstaff/wag4.html) · [Jones](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.1579) · [Canonical predicates](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/1104.1579/CunninghamChain.lean)

At each odd prime `q`, divisibility of either of the two terms excludes exactly two seed residues. For any selected set of sieving primes, the Chinese remainder theorem gives the exact number of residues satisfying each intersection of divisibility conditions. On an initial interval, each residue count differs from its periodic mean by at most one. The even and odd Bonferroni bounds compare the interval count with the full-period density. Restricting to seeds larger than the sieving primes allows for the case where a prime term equals a sieving prime. [Lean proof](CunninghamBrunSieve.lean)

The product of the two-residue survival factors for odd primes through `z` is at most `4/log²(z+1)`. This follows by comparing the finite Euler product with the harmonic sum over the positive integers through `z`. A subset of primes with controlled product makes the next omitted inclusion–exclusion term small as well. For `j ≥ 12` and `8ʲ ≤ n`, the resulting bound on the number of pair seeds in `[2ⁿ, 2ⁿ⁺¹)` is `35 · 2ⁿ / 16ʲ`. Summing over `8ʲ ≤ n < 8ʲ⁺¹` gives a reciprocal contribution at most `280 · 2⁻ʲ`. The constants suffice for convergence. [Lean proof](CunninghamBrunSieve.lean)

Every exact chain of length at least two starts with a prime pair. At length one, the seeds are precisely the prime seeds whose successor is composite. Since the sum of all prime reciprocals diverges, removing the convergent pair subseries leaves a divergent sum. Convergence at higher lengths is consistent with both finite and infinite seed sets. Jones’s general infinitude statements remain open.
