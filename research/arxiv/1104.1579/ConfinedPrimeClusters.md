# Several prime returns on a confined Cunningham orbit

Fix $M>0$ and a desired number $m$ of later primes. For either Cunningham recurrence, a fixed finite set $V$ containing zero and at least $m$ positive indices occurs as prime sites for infinitely many prime seeds. **Every prime return of each of these seeds, however late, has index divisible by $M$.** The Lean proof takes `MaynardAffineClusters`, a fixed-family instance of Maynard's Theorem 3.4, as an explicit hypothesis. The analytic theorem itself is outside the replay. [Proof](CunninghamConfinedPrimeClusters.lean) · [Maynard, Theorem 3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2593#page=3)

The local statement is exact. There are positive $D,W$, common to both signs and all $m$, with $M\mid D$. Every site in $V$ is a multiple of $D$. At each of the resulting seeds $p>W$, the term $T_j(p)$ is coprime to $W$ exactly when $D\mid j$. Additional primes may occur among those multiples. The set $V$ can depend on $M,m$ and the sign. Jones asks for prime terms at *every* index below a given length and a nonprime term at the next index. Infinitude of such seeds for lengths at least two remains open. ## Which seed classes can contain the returning primes?

Let $w>0$ and fix a reduced seed residue $a$ modulo $w$. Take a positive representative $B$ of $a+1$ for the first kind and of $a-1$ for the second kind. There are arbitrarily large finite patterns of primes **all congruent to $a$**, each pattern recurring for infinitely many prime seeds congruent to $a$, exactly when $w/\gcd(w,B)$ is odd. For example, modulo 8 the qualifying prime seed class is 7 for the first kind and 1 for the second. Every reduced class qualifies for odd $w$. [Class criterion](CunninghamConfinedPrimeClusters.lean)

Necessity is unconditional. In centered coordinates a return after $j>0$ steps requires $2^j B\equiv B\pmod w$. Cancelling $B$ changes the modulus to $w/\gcd(w,B)$. An even quotient would give $2^j\equiv1\pmod2$. Conversely, for an odd quotient Euler's theorem gives a positive period $L$. Sampling the orbit at multiples of $L$ preserves the desired seed residue. This last step gives the periodic grid before any appeal to primes. [Arithmetic equivalence](CunninghamConfinedPrimeClusters.lean)

## Producing the primes

For the confined orbits, sample at multiples of the cover period $D$. Vary the center as $x=wt+B$. At a fixed grid index $i$, the orbit value is an affine form in $t$ with slope $2^i w$. Primes dividing $w$ miss every candidate by the local coprimality condition. At any other prime $q$, choose $t$ so that $x\equiv0\pmod q$. All the forms then take values $1$ or $-1$ modulo $q$. The slopes and intercepts give pairwise nonproportional forms. Maynard supplies at least $m+1$ prime values at arbitrarily large centers. Finitely many patterns are possible, so one recurs. Restarting at its first selected prime leaves the gcd criterion intact because its index is divisible by $D$. For $D=24$ and $W=798915$, the first-kind seed $938988793$ and second-kind seed $227427107$ both have prime terms at indices $0,24,48$. An independent integer computation checked all 24 local residues, the period identity $2^{24}\equiv1\pmod W$, and primality of the six values by recursive Lucas certificates from complete factorizations of $n-1$.
