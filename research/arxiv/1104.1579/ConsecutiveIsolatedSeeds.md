# Consecutive primes with isolated Cunningham orbits

Fix a positive modulus `M`. There is a **single positive gap** `g`, divisible by `M`, with the following property. For every finite depth `H`, infinitely many consecutive primes `p,p+g` each start an exact singleton chain of both kinds, and the first `H` positive steps on all four orbits are composite. The gap is chosen before the depth. The primes may change with the depth. In Lean the analytic hypothesis is `PrimeReturns.MaynardAffineClusters`, the fixed-family consequence of Maynard’s Theorem 3.4 already used in the notebook. Jones’s infinitude questions for general exact lengths remain open. [Consecutive-gap theorem](CunninghamRecurringSeedClusters.lean) · [Pinned Cunningham predicates](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/Arxiv/1104.1579/CunninghamChain.lean) · [Maynard, Theorem 3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2593#page=3)

For any requested number `m` of consecutive primes, the same argument gives a fixed finite set `U` of at least `m` offsets, all multiples of `M`. On arbitrarily large translates, the entire interval contains primes precisely at these offsets, and every one of these primes has both prescribed stretches of composite successors. The interval and the offsets work for every depth. Specializing to the two least offsets gives the gap theorem. At depth seven, 4967 and 4969 are a finite example. This example supplies no claim that gap two recurs infinitely often.

## The progression used for composite values

Choose any sufficiently large admissible tuple of positive offsets `A` in `[0,D]`. For every offset outside the tuple, force the translated seed to be composite. For every offset in the tuple, force its first `H` successors of both kinds to be composite. Each unwanted value is an affine form `a x+c`. At positive orbit depth, its slope is even and its constant term is odd. At an unselected seed, its slope is one and its offset lies outside `A`. In both cases `a j ≠ c` for every selected translate `x+j`. [Pinned recurrence computations](CunninghamSeedOrbitMask.lean)

For each unwanted form, choose a different prime `q` larger than the slope, intercept and every `a j`. Solving `a x+c = 0 mod q` then avoids the roots of all selected seed translates. The Chinese remainder theorem puts all these roots on one progression. Each unwanted value exceeds its assigned divisor there. At primes dividing the progression modulus every selected seed survives, and at all other primes varying the parameter realizes every residue. Thus the seed tuple remains admissible. [Affine progression lemma](CunninghamSeedAffineMask.lean)

Maynard now supplies the requested number of prime seeds at arbitrarily large parameters. Apply this at increasing composite depths, with the centers tending to infinity. Only finitely many subsets of the selected tuple can be prime, so one subset recurs at unbounded depths. For existence at each prescribed modulus use the offsets `(i+1) M K!` for `0 ≤ i < K`. All small primes admit seed residue one; larger primes see fewer than a full set of forbidden residues. [Fixed subset and exact singleton proof](CunninghamRecurringSeedClusters.lean)

Banks, Freiberg and Turnage-Butterbaugh used admissible tuples to obtain consecutive primes. The extra affine forms here impose composite successors on both Cunningham recurrences while leaving the same bounded set of possible seed offsets. The earlier notebook cluster theorem concerns primes at multiple depths along one orbit. The current tuple instead consists of consecutive primes in the ordinary ordering. The earlier weighted pair bound applies unconditionally to a growing range of seed gaps.
