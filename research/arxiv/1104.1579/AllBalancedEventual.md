# Eventual irreducibility for every balanced Jones coefficient

For an integer a ≥ 2 set T₀ = a(X−1) and Tₙ₊₁ = XTₙ + ε. There is a single N, depending on a, such that Tₙ is irreducible in ℚ[X] for every n ≥ N and both ε = ±1. The missing case in the earlier notebook proof was ε = −1 with a a perfect square. The new proof covers that case without giving a numerical cutoff. For example, at a = 4 the third minus iterate factors as (2X+1)(2X³−3X²+X−1). [Simultaneous theorem](CunninghamAllBalancedEventual.lean) · [Earlier effective result](EventualBalancedChains.md)

Jones studies these polynomial recurrences in §4. The eventual conclusion follows mathematically from the classical large-gap theorem of Schinzel as stated in Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3 of Sawin, Shusterman and Stoll, together with reciprocal-factor exclusion. Their Theorem 1.5 records an effective bound due to Filaseta, Ford and Konyagin. Here the square-coefficient case has an independent, specialized Lean proof. The pinned DeepMind questions ask for infinitely many *natural prime seeds* of each prescribed positive exact length under q ↦ 2q ± 1. Both remain open. ## Why a bad support pattern cannot recur

Write a = b² with b ≥ 2 and put Aₙ(T) = Tⁿ(b²(T−1)²−1). Then Fₙ = (T−1)Tₙ = 1+Aₙ has degree N = n+2 for n > 0. For a polynomial G of degree at most N, write R(G) = TᴺG(1/T). Reversing a factor of Fₙ produces an integral candidate G with GR(G) = FₙR(Fₙ). The middle coefficient of this product gives the sum of squared coefficients of G. It bounds the number of nonzero coefficients uniformly in n. Mark their positions, together with 0, n, n+1 and n+2. Record which ordered pairs of marked positions have equal differences. Only finitely many such patterns occur. [Integral support bound](CunninghamGapSupportPresentation.lean) · [Finiteness of exceptional degrees](CunninghamGapPatternFiniteness.lean)

Suppose the marked pattern recurs at a larger index m. For a marked position i and its counterpart j, lift the corresponding monomial Tⁱ to TⁱSᵐ⁺²⁺ⁱ⁻ʲ. Treat the reflected positions the same way. Equality of the marked differences guarantees that terms contributing to one coefficient in the product retain a common S-exponent. Thus the product identity lifts to ℚ[T][S], using the coefficients at the smaller index alone. The lift of Fₙ is Sⁿ⁺²(Sᵐ⁻ⁿ + Aₙ(T)). The second factor is Eisenstein at T−(1+1/b), a simple root of Aₙ. It divides the lifted candidate or its reflection. Specializing S = 1 shows Fₙ divides one of the two original candidates. [Two-degree lift](CunninghamTwoDegreeLift.lean) · [Eisenstein proof](CunninghamSquareGapEisenstein.lean) · [Endpoint comparison](CunninghamSquareGapComparison.lean)

The degree bound makes the quotient constant. The reflected-product equality then forces that constant to be ±1. Consequently every degree admitting a candidate different from ±Fₙ in both orientations has a pattern that cannot occur at a larger exceptional degree. The uniform support bound makes the exceptional set finite. The earlier factor-reversal and reciprocal-root arguments now yield eventual irreducibility.
