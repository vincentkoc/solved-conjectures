# Recurring prime-return times on Cunningham orbits

Start at a prime and iterate either `x ↦ 2x+1` or `x ↦ 2x−1`, keeping the sign fixed. A first return at step `d > 0` means that step `d` is prime and all strictly intermediate steps are composite. Fix any finite set `E` of positive multipliers. There is a bound `D` such that every integer interval `[n,n+D]` contains a spacing `d` with the following property. For each `s ∈ E` and each sign, infinitely many prime seeds have first return exactly at step `sd`. The seeds can vary with the sign and multiplier. For instance, take `E = {1,2,3}` to get bounded gaps between choices of `d` for which all three return times `d, 2d, 3d` recur for both signs. [Lean theorem](CunninghamPrimeReturnTimes.lean)

**Analytic input.** The formal proof assumes `MaynardAffineClusters`, a fixed-family consequence of Maynard’s published Theorem 3.4. For each requested count `m`, sufficiently many admissible, pairwise nonproportional affine forms with positive integer slopes and nonnegative intercepts take at least `m` prime values at arbitrarily large common arguments. In Theorem 3.4 take `y=x` and a fixed small positive ε. The coefficients of a fixed family eventually satisfy the size conditions. For a larger family apply the paper’s theorem to a subfamily of the stated size. The analytic sieve itself is outside the Lean proof. [Maynard, *Dense clusters of primes in subsets*, Theorem 3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2593#page=3)

**Prescribing the composite positions.** In centered coordinates the values are `2ⁱx−1` for the first kind and `2ⁱx+1` for the second. Fix a set of allowed positions `A ⊆ [0,L]`. Assign each forbidden position its own prime larger than both two and `2ᴸ`. It can divide at most one orbit value in this window, since the powers of two through `2ᴸ` are distinct modulo it. The Chinese remainder theorem supplies a progression of centers on which the assigned primes divide their respective values. Starting far enough along makes them proper divisors. The allowed forms on this progression remain admissible. At a prime dividing the progression modulus the unique forbidden position accounts for the zero. At any other prime one can choose the center to be zero modulo that prime. The allowed slopes are distinct and their pairwise determinants are nonzero. [Progression proof](CunninghamReturnProgressions.lean)

Maynard’s theorem now puts at least `m` primes in the allowed positions when `A` is large enough. Some fixed pattern of successes occurs at infinitely many centers, by finite pigeonhole. With `m=2`, choose adjacent successes in that pattern. Restarting the canonical recurrence at the first prime gives the second after exactly the difference of their positions. Banks, Freiberg and Turnage-Butterbaugh used prescribed composite positions to isolate consecutive ordinary primes in tuples of translates. Here consecutiveness refers to the terms of the recurrence. [Orbit proof](CunninghamPrimeReturnTimes.lean) · [Banks, Freiberg and Turnage-Butterbaugh](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.7003)

**Why the gaps are bounded.** Each sufficiently large finite set of positions contains two whose positive difference is a recurring return time. A maximal set avoiding such differences is finite. Every integer larger than its elements then belongs to one of finitely many translates of the return-time set, which bounds its gaps. The finite Ramsey theorem preserves the difference property under intersection. Multiplying positions by a fixed positive integer preserves their distinctness, so the same argument applies to inverse images under multiplication. Intersecting over both signs and all requested scales gives the common spacing result. [Difference-set proof](CunninghamReturnDifferences.lean)

**Scope.** The main declarations in `CunninghamChain.PrimeReturns` are `recurring_exact_patterns`, `common_spacings_meet_differences` and `bounded_gaps_of_common_spacings`. Jones’s canonical predicates require every term before the stopping index to be prime and the stopping term to be nonprime. The result here leaves open any particular first return, including `d=1`, and infinitude at a prescribed chain length of at least two. The earlier upper bound shows that returning seeds for each fixed spacing have relative density zero among primes.
