# Eventual irreducibility for the balanced Jones chains

Fix an integer a ≥ 2. Start with T₀ = a(X−1) and iterate Tₙ₊₁ = XTₙ + ε. For ε = +1, every sufficiently late Tₙ is irreducible over ℚ. For ε = −1 the proof gives the same conclusion whenever a is a nonsquare. In particular a can have several distinct prime factors. For a nonsquare a the theorem reduces simultaneous irreducibility at **all** indices to a finite prefix for both signs. Early exceptions do occur. At a = 2 and minus index 3 the factorization is (X²−X−1)(2X²+1). [Lean statements and example](CunninghamEventualBalancedIrreducibility.lean)

The result applies the large-gap method of Sawin, Shusterman and Stoll to Jones's polynomial recurrences. Their Theorem 1.6 and §4 cover an irreducible primitive endpoint paired with 1. Their more general Theorem 1.2 also applies to the square-coefficient minus case by the squarefreeness of the quadratic endpoint, which prevents the Capellian exceptional powers. Our Lean proof in that case covers the reciprocal-root exclusion only. Jones gives separate seeds producing infinite polynomial chains of each kind. An earlier notebook proof gives simultaneous irreducibility at **every** index for odd prime powers at least 615. The two pinned DeepMind conjectures concern infinitely many natural prime seeds at each exact stopping length under q ↦ 2q ± 1. Both remain open. ## The cutoff

Put N = n+2 and c(X) = −ε(a(1−X)² + εX²). Then 1 + Xᴺc(1/X) = −ε(X−1)Tₙ. A rational zero of the quadratic would satisfy a(1−x)² = −εx². Both squares cannot vanish together. With the minus sign, x/(1−x) would therefore be a rational square root of a. The constant and quadratic coefficients differ by a unit before normalization, so Gauss's lemma gives irreducibility of c over ℤ under the stated hypotheses. [Endpoint and exact polynomial identity](CunninghamBalancedGapQuadratic.lean)

Let W = 1 + 5a² + (a+ε)². The formal cutoff specializes to N ≥ 12·4ᵂ, since c has degree 2 and sum of squared coefficients W−1. This sufficient bound makes the finite reduction explicit but grows too quickly for direct enumeration. The published robust-pair bound is smaller. [Formal threshold and rigidity theorem](CunninghamLargeGapRigidity.lean)

For degree at most N write R(G) = XᴺG(1/X) and F = 1+R(c). If GR(G) = FR(F), their middle coefficients give equal total squared coefficient weight. There is a dyadic interval [m, 2m) past the degree of c where G and R(G) both have zero coefficients. Otherwise the two truncated weights would increase beyond their budget. Truncating the product below 2m now gives an exact factorization of c by the two short ends. One factor is a unit. These two ends already consume all the weight of G, so G or R(G) equals ±F. [Dyadic gap and truncated product](CunninghamSparseGapTruncation.lean) · [Endpoint weights](CunninghamEndpointWeights.lean) · [Rigidity](CunninghamLargeGapRigidity.lean)

In any integral factorization Tₙ = AB, reverse just A inside −ε(X−1)AB. Rigidity makes A or B equal to its own reversal up to sign. At positive indices both factors have unit constant coefficient. A nonconstant reciprocal factor would give a pair of roots z and 1/z. The balanced root equations then force zⁿ⁺² = −1, contradicting the cyclotomic exclusion at every positive index. Thus one factor is a unit.
