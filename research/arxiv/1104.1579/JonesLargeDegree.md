# Jones’s first polynomial parameter and a finite range for every parameter

At the first parameter of Jones’s Conjecture 4.1, `k = 1`, the polynomial `X^j + ⋯ + X + 6` is reducible over ℚ exactly when `j = 3`, for every prescribed degree `j ≥ 2`. At the exception it factors as `(X+2)(X²−X+3)`. The declaration `jones_first_parameter_irreducible_iff` gives the equivalent irreducibility statement. [Lean proof](CunninghamJonesFirstParameter.lean) · [Finite certificate](CunninghamJonesSixCertificate.lean) · [Jones, Conjecture 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.1579#page=7)

Let `P_j(X) = X^j + ⋯ + X + m` for positive degree `j`, and set `K(m) = 4^(4 + (m−1)^2 + m^2)`. For every integer `m ≥ 2` and every `j ≥ K(m)`, the polynomial `P_j` is irreducible over the rationals. The bound is explicit and large. [Lean proof](CunninghamJonesLargeDegree.lean)

For Jones’s Conjecture 4.1 take `m = 2((2^t + 1)/3)` at the prescribed odd parameter `t`, using integer division. For every `t ≥ 3` and every initial degree `L`, the claim “for all `j ≥ L`, `P_j` is irreducible exactly when `j ≠ t`” is equivalent to the same check on `L ≤ j < K(m)`. Jones uses `L = t−k`. This reduction requires no primality assumption on the odd part of the constant term. Verifying the remaining finite interval for arbitrary parameters is open. The pinned DeepMind declarations concern infinitely many prime seeds of exact first- and second-kind chain lengths. Both remain open. ## Published bounds

This is a Lean formalization of eventual irreducibility with an elementary cutoff. Published large-gap theorems give smaller cutoffs for the same family. Take f = 1 and g = (m−1)X − m in their notation. The linear polynomial g is primitive because its two coefficients are coprime. It is irreducible, and the pair is robust in the sense of Sawin, Shusterman and Stoll. If E = 1 + (m−1)² + m², their Theorem 1.6 bounds the nonreciprocal-part threshold by 2^(E+1). Filaseta, Li, Patane and Skabelund’s Corollary 2 applies for N > 4 and N > F_(E+1). The identity at the end of the proof below identifies the reciprocal contribution for this particular trinomial. ## Proof

Write `N = j+1`, `b = m−1` and `T = (X−1)P_j = X^N + bX − m`. For a polynomial `f` of degree at most `s`, write `f* = X^s f(1/X)`, with the reflection length `s` taken from the particular factor. An integral monic factorization `P_j = AB` gives `G = (X−1)A B*` with `TT* = GG*`, using length `N` for the two products. Their coefficient of degree `N` is the sum of the squares of the coefficients of either `T` or `G`. Thus `G` has at most `1 + b² + m²` nonzero coefficients. Adjoining `0, 1, N` to its support gives a set `S` with at most `4 + b² + m²` elements.

If `4^|S| < N`, color the integers `z = 0, …, 4^|S|` by recording, for each `e ∈ S`, which quarter of `[0,N)` contains `ze mod N`. Two integers `a < c` have the same color. Put `h = c−a` and `v(e) = ⌊ce/N⌋−⌊ae/N⌋`. Then `v(0) = v(1) = 0`, `v(N) = h > 0`, and `0 ≤ v(e) ≤ h` for `e ≤ N`. On `S`, the absolute error `|he − Nv(e)|` is strictly less than `N/4`. Consequently, each equality `e₁+e₂=e₃+e₄` in `S` also holds for the four images under `v`.

Lift each term `X^e` to `X^e Y^v(e)` and each reflected term to `X^(N−e) Y^(h−v(e))`. Equal exponents in the univariate products acquire equal exponents of `Y`, so the lifted products still agree. The lift of `T` is `X^N Y^h + bX − m`. Eisenstein at the linear prime `bX−m` in `ℚ[X]` shows it is irreducible in `ℚ[X][Y]`. It is primitive since `bX−m` does not divide `X^N`. This prime divides one of the two lifted factors on the right. Setting `Y = 1` gives `T ∣ G` or `T ∣ G*`. Cancelling the common factor gives a factor of `P_j` dividing both `T` and `T*`.

The identity `X T* − (b−mX)T = bm(X−1)^2` restricts every such common factor to a scalar times a power of `X−1`. Since `P_j(1) = m+j ≠ 0`, a factor of `P_j` dividing both is a unit. Gauss’s lemma gives irreducibility over the rationals.

## The first Jones parameter

When t = 3 the constant is 6. Every complex root of P_j has absolute value greater than 1. Indeed, a root of modulus at most 1 would give z^N + (m−1)z = m, which forces the real part of z to be 1 and then z = 1. But P_j(1) is positive. The roots of a nonconstant monic integral factor therefore have product of absolute value greater than 1. Its constant term has absolute value at least 2.

If P_j with constant 6 is reducible, the two factor constants must have absolute values 2 and 3. Swapping one factor with its reflection and normalizing its sign gives an integral G of degree N with G(0) = 2, leading coefficient −3, G(1) = 0 and GG* = TT* for T = X^N + 5X − 6. This is the conclusion of `jones_six_factor_witness`. It supplies explicit necessary conditions for exceptional degrees.

Write the coefficients of G from constant to leading term. The middle coefficient of GG* says their squares sum to 62. Its three neighboring coefficients give the sums of products at distances 1, 2 and 3, namely −30, 0 and 0 when N ≥ 5. The endpoints 2 and −3 leave a square-sum budget of 49. After n endpoints have been read from each side, the coefficient of degree n of GG* determines 2y−3x for the next two coefficients x and y. Testing the integers x from −7 through 7 covers every possible choice within the budget. The last coefficient, if one remains, is forced by G(1) = 0.

The finite certificate checks that no branch beginning with these endpoints can terminate with a list of length at least six satisfying the sum, energy and three product conditions. Every hypothetical witness either gives a terminal list or advances to a child. Induction on the certificate therefore excludes every N ≥ 5 at once. Degree 2 follows from the notebook’s power-of-two theorem. The factorization at degree 3 supplies the remaining direction.
