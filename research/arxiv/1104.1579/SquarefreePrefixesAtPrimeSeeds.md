# Squarefree Cunningham prefixes at prime seeds

Fix either recurrence and a positive length `k`. There is a prime `t` such that, for every cutoff `B`, infinitely many **prime seeds** `p` satisfy all three conditions.

- The product of the terms at indices `0,…,k−1` is squarefree. In particular, a prime cannot divide two prefix terms.
- Every prime factor of that product exceeds `B`.
- The term at index `k` is divisible by `t²` and is composite. The choice of `t` is independent of `B`.

The theorem uses DeepMind’s `firstKindTerm` and `secondKindTerm` directly. Its prime seed and composite successor meet the corresponding parts of the exact-length predicates. Primality at every intermediate index remains open for general `k ≥ 2`. The earlier almost-prime theorem bounds the number of prime factors per term and allows composite seeds. The present theorem supplies no uniform bound on the number of distinct prime factors. ## Proof

Choose a prime `t > max(2,2ᵏ)` and a cutoff `R` above `B`, `4k` and `t`. Modulo `t²`, force the terminal iterate to vanish. At each other prime through `R`, put the seed at the fixed point of the recurrence. The Chinese remainder theorem combines these conditions into a reduced class modulo `m`. The terminal congruence cannot hit an earlier site modulo `t`, because the powers of two through `2ᵏ` are distinct modulo `t`. The same argument prevents two prefix terms sharing any prime larger than `R`. [Construction](CunninghamSquarefreePrimeSeeds.lean) · [Orbit uniqueness](CunninghamReturnProgressions.lean)

For each prime `q > R`, divisibility of a positive-index iterate by `q²` forbids one reduced seed class modulo `q²`. Only squares up to a constant times the seed can divide the term. For `R < q ≤ L`, the Chinese remainder theorem and the prime Dirichlet series give each forbidden class normalized mass `1/(φ(m)q(q−1))`. Summing over the forms and primes costs at most half the mass of the original progression. For `q > L`, a one-dimensional Selberg sieve and the elementary count in a single residue class bound the number of prime seeds below `X` hit by one family of classes by `1632X/(L log X)` for sufficiently large `X`. Choose `L` so large that this tail costs less than a further quarter of the progression mass. A finite set of survivors would have zero normalized mass, a contradiction.
