# Four-point blocks at every admissible order

For every positive integer $v$, a Steiner pair design with four points per block exists exactly when $v\equiv1$ or $4\pmod{12}$. At $v=1$ the empty family satisfies the pair condition. The Lean theorem `Erdos722.Quartet.hanani_iff` states the equivalence on `Fin v` using the pinned `Finset.IsBlockDesign 2 4`. The result is Hanani's classical spectrum. Its contribution here is the checked construction across both entire residue classes. [Lean proof](Erdos722HananiFour.lean) · [Hanani (1961)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177705047)

## Construction

Write $v=3r+1$. First build a pairwise balanced design on $r$ points whose blocks have sizes in $\{4,5,8,9,12\}$. The replication numbers needed are exactly $r\equiv0,1\pmod4$. The recursion follows the construction described by Stinson on pp. 167–168 of *Combinatorial Designs*, also implemented in SageMath. [SageMath's four-point design](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/combinat/sage/combinat/designs/bibd.html#sage.combinat.designs.bibd.v_4_1_BIBD) · [Auxiliary design](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/combinat/sage/combinat/designs/bibd.html#sage.combinat.designs.bibd.PBD_4_5_8_9_12)

1. For $r\ge240$, put $t=12\lfloor(r-4)/48\rfloor+1$ and $u=r-4t$. Then $0\le u\le t$, and both smaller orders are admissible. For smaller $r$ a finite certificate supplies a suitable $t$ or an explicit base case. Over $\mathbb Z/t\mathbb Z$, evaluate $x\mapsto mx+b$ in four columns at $0,1,2,3$. Their differences are units, so any pair in different columns determines a unique line. A fifth column contains $u$ of the possible slopes. Filling each column recursively gives order $4t+u$. Finite fields and direct products supply the small transversal orders. [Orders](Erdos722QuartetOrders.lean) · [Ring choices](Erdos722QuartetDirections.lean) · [Transversals](Erdos722QuartetTransversals.lean) · [Recursion and exceptions](Erdos722QuartetMaster.lean)
2. For each permitted block size $s$, take a design on $3s+1$ points and delete one point. Its incident blocks partition the other vertices into triples. The surviving blocks cover pairs from different triples exactly once. The five seeds have orders $13,16,25,28,37$. Translated difference families supply $13,25,37$, with the order-$25$ construction in the additive group of $\mathbf F_5^2$. The order-$16$ seed uses the affine-plane case of the notebook's Denniston construction. Order $28$ uses the classical Hermitian unital of order three. [Seeds and difference certificates](Erdos722QuartetSeeds.lean) · [Point deletion](Erdos722QuartetDeletion.lean) · [Earlier independent 28-point certificate](https://github.com/The-Last-Math-Competition/The-Last-Math-Competition/blob/aada3eabf145c79a6cc47995fb946ab9e4814892/solutions/00000001028/jilint777_submission_20261004151436/lean4/Main.lean)
3. Replace every master point by a triple and adjoin a common point. A triple together with the common point is a block. A pair in distinct triples selects a unique master block and then a unique surviving seed block. The resulting design has $3r+1$ vertices. [Pair covers](Erdos722QuartetCovers.lean) · [Inflation](Erdos722HananiFour.lean)

## Exact upstream scope

`design_divisibility` counts the $r$-subsets containing a fixed $i$-subset inside the blocks of any design. It gives the upstream divisibility conditions whenever $i\le n$ and $i\le r$. For $r=2,k=4$, the two conditions are $6\mid\binom v2$ and $3\mid(v-1)$. At positive orders they force precisely the two residue classes above. `four_of_divisibility` uses those exact upstream hypotheses. `eventually_four` gives the literal specialization of the pinned filter statement. The general Erdős 722 theorem quantifies over every $1\le r<k$.
