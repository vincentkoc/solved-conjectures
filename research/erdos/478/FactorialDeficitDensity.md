# Factorial deficits at almost all primes

Let $A_p=\{k!\bmod p:1\le k<p\}$ and $\delta_p=p-|A_p|$. For every fixed nonnegative integer $D$,
$$
  \lim_{X\to\infty}
  \frac{\#\{p\le X:p\ \text{prime},\ \delta_p\ge D\}}{\pi(X)}=1.
$$
The Lean declaration is `Erdos478.GapDensity.natural_density_one`. Its only argument is $D$. The image in `MeanDeficit.deficit` uses exactly the interval and remainder in the pinned upstream problem. The upstream conjecture asks for $|A_p|/p\to1-1/e$ along all primes. The density theorem gives no order of growth for $\delta_p$ relative to $p$. [Ordinary-density proof](Erdos478NaturalDensity.lean.in) · [Definition of the deficit](Erdos478MeanDeficit.lean) · [Pinned Erdős 478](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/478.lean)

In particular, the primes for which $2!,\ldots,(p-1)!$ are all distinct have relative natural density zero. Distinctness gives at least $p-2$ represented classes, so these primes are disjoint from the density-one set for $D=3$. The declaration `collision_free_density_zero` checks the distinctness condition on this shorter interval directly. Whether a socialist prime greater than five exists remains open. [Formal corollary](Erdos478NaturalDensity.lean.in) · [Problem and background](https://www.erdosproblems.com/478)

## Why the exceptional proportion vanishes

For each prime gap length $\ell$, let $r_\ell(p)$ count the distinct roots of $x(x-1)\cdots(x-\ell+1)-1$ in $\mathbf F_p$. A root identifies an endpoint of a repeated factorial. For each fixed finite set $S$ of gap lengths, a nonzero resultant excludes the finitely many primes where different polynomials can share an endpoint. The earlier disjoint-gap estimate then gives $\sum_{\ell\in S}r_\ell(p)\le\delta_p$ beyond that cutoff. [Disjoint endpoints](DisjointGapFamilies.md) · [Formal deficit bound](Erdos478GapVariance.lean)

Write $\mathbb E_X$ for the ordinary mean over rational primes up to $X$. For two monic irreducible polynomials $f,g$ of coprime degrees, the ordered pairs of roots modulo $p$ correspond to ring maps from the order generated by a root of each polynomial into $\mathbf F_p$. Coprimality of the degrees makes $g$ irreducible over the root field of $f$, by divisibility of field degrees in a tower. The resulting order has full rank in a number field. Away from its index, the ring maps count precisely that field's primes of residue degree one over $p$. The prime ideal theorem gives $\mathbb E_X(r_f r_g)\to1$. Taking $g=x$ gives $\mathbb E_X r_f\to1$. [Order and its maps](Erdos478RootPairOrder.lean) · [Coprime root fields](Erdos478CoprimeRootFields.lean) · [Degree-one correspondence](Erdos478PrimeOrderMaps.lean) · [Joint root mean](Erdos478CoprimeRootMoments.lean)

Set $M=\sum_{\ell\in S}1/\ell$ and $Z_p=\sum_{\ell\in S}(r_\ell(p)-1)/\ell$. Distinct prime degrees have limiting covariance zero. On the diagonal, $0\le r_\ell(p)\le\ell$ gives a majorant of limiting mean at most $2/\ell$. Hence $\limsup_X\mathbb E_X Z_p^2\le 2M$. If $\delta_p<D$ and $M\ge2D$, then $Z_p\le-M/2$ outside the finite exceptional set. The limiting upper proportion is therefore at most $8/M$. The reciprocal sum over prime gap lengths diverges, so $S$ can be chosen with arbitrarily large $M$ before taking the prime average. [Variance bound](Erdos478GapVariance.lean) · [Vanishing exceptional mass](Erdos478DensityOne.lean)

## Analytic inputs and prior work

The ordinary mean uses Tau Ceti's checked prime ideal theorem and its density-one theorem for prime ideals of residue degree one. The adapter proves that the number of degree-one primes of norm at most $X$ is exactly the sum of the counts over rational primes $p\le X$. Dividing the common asymptotic $X/\log X$ for the chosen field and for $\mathbf Q$ supplies the rational-prime normalization. The pinned port and the notebook share Lean 4.33.1 and mathlib revision `0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474`. [Tau Ceti prime ideal theorem in the compatible port](https://github.com/KitaKen1/simultaneous-primitive-roots/blob/bb3b4fff6bb0210a0f94fc535bf9adbf46ecc55a/lean/vendor/TauCeti/NumberTheory/ArithmeticDirichletSeries/Prime/PrimeIdealTheorem.lean) · [Residue-degree density](https://github.com/KitaKen1/simultaneous-primitive-roots/blob/bb3b4fff6bb0210a0f94fc535bf9adbf46ecc55a/lean/vendor/TauCeti/NumberTheory/ArithmeticDirichletSeries/ResidueDegree/NaturalDensity.lean) · [Tau Ceti project](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti)

The notebook's own degree-one Mertens theorem also gives density one with weights $(\log p)/p$ normalized by $\log X$. Summation by parts gives density one with weights $1/p$ normalized by $\sum_{p\le X}1/p$. Both specializations build entirely against the pinned Formal Conjectures environment. [Mertens input](Erdos478IdealPrimeWeights.lean) · [Log-weighted density](Erdos478DensityOne.lean) · [Reciprocal-prime density](Erdos478PrimeProportions.lean)

Banks, Luca, Shparlinski and Stichtenoth proved a growing deficit on a subsequence. Klurman and Munsch obtained quantitative lower bounds for the average deficit using gap polynomials and prime ideals. In §3 they discussed the almost-all-primes collision question through a conjecture about splitting fields. The argument here uses mixed second moments for root fields of coprime degrees and controls every fixed deficit threshold. The earlier notebook interval bound remains stronger for its explicit counts in prescribed progressions.
