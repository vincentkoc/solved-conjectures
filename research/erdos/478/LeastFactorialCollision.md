# The least distance between equal factorial residues

For each prime $p$, consider the pairs $1\le j<j+\ell<p$ with $j!\equiv(j+\ell)!\pmod p$. For every integer $\ell$, the primes whose smallest such distance is $\ell$ have an ordinary natural density $a_\ell$. These masses sum to one. Every prime length occurs with positive mass, including when all smaller composite lengths are excluded. Lengths zero and one have mass zero. For $\ell\ge2$ composite, the theorem gives the density and its finite Galois formula. Positivity at every composite length is open here. [Ordinary first-distance law](Erdos478NaturalFirstCollisions.lean.in) · [Exact first-distance event](Erdos478LeastPrimeCollision.lean) · [Pinned Erdős 478](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/478.lean)

Let $d_K$ be the density of primes with no collision at any distance from $1$ through $K$, and let $m_K$ count primes $3<\ell\le K$ congruent to $3$ or $4\bmod 7$. The estimates are
$$
  \left(\prod_{k=2}^K k!\right)^{-1}\le d_K\le 2^{-m_K},
  \qquad d_{K-1}=d_K+a_K\quad(K\ge1).
$$
For a prime $\ell$, put $c_\ell=2$ if $\ell>3$ and $\ell\bmod7\in\{3,4\}$, and $c_\ell=\ell$ otherwise. Then $a_\ell\ge d_{\ell-1}/c_\ell$. For example, at least half of the primes avoiding all distances $1$ through $10$ have a distance-$11$ collision. The empty product covers $K=0,1$. The fraction at cutoff $X$ counts primes up to $X$. ## The common avoidance event

Write $f_k(x)=x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1)-1$. Above an explicit finite cutoff, its roots modulo $p$ are precisely the upper endpoints of distance-$k$ collisions when $k\ge2$. At length one, cancelling the invertible smaller factorial would force $j\equiv0\pmod p$. For a finite set of lengths, the checked joint root-count law identifies every count-event density with a fraction of the Galois group of a common splitting field. Klurman and Munsch used factorial-gap polynomials and degree-one prime ideals to bound the average number of missed residues. The earlier notebook established the joint count law and its fixed-point interpretation. [Endpoint correspondence](Erdos478FactorialEndpointCounts.lean) · [Integer-length observables](Erdos478IntegerGapObservables.lean) · [Earlier conditional collision law](ConditionalGapDensities.md) · [Klurman and Munsch](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.974)

In that common field take a prime ideal above $2$ and lift squaring on its residue field. An integral point fixed by the lift reduces to $0$ or $1$. Neither value is a root of any $f_k$ for $k\ge2$. Thus one automorphism simultaneously avoids fixed points at every tested length, even when $2$ ramifies. Its contribution is at least one divided by the field degree, and that degree is at most the product of the $k!$. [Binary Frobenius lift](Erdos478ResidueFrobeniusAtTwo.lean) · [Simultaneous derangement](Erdos478ShortCollisionAvoidance.lean) · [Degree estimate](Erdos478GapSplittingDegrees.lean)

For a new prime length $\ell$, the irreducible degree-$\ell$ polynomial has an $\ell$-cycle. The permutation group on all the smaller root sets has order dividing a product of $k!$ with $k<\ell$. The kernel fixing those root sets still acts transitively at $\ell$. In each of its cosets the mean fixed-point count is one and the count is at most $\ell$, giving the $1/\ell$ bound. In the two specified residue classes the existing double transitivity argument also works with composite earlier degrees. Its second moment gives the stronger half bound. ## Long collisions within the short-free primes

Fix $A<\log(32/27)/9$. For every sufficiently large $K$, and then every sufficiently large $X$ depending on $K$, consider *only* the primes $p\le X$ with no collision of length at most $K$. A fraction greater than $1-\exp(-AK/\log K)$ of those primes has at least $K/(9\log K)$ distinct collision lengths in $(K,2K]$ among the two eligible residue classes. Writing $U_p$ for these witnessed lengths and $A_p=\{n!\bmod p:1\le n<p\}$, the same primes satisfy $|A_p|+|U_p|\le p-1$. The denominator of this relative fraction has positive prime density for each $K$ and tends to zero in density as $K$ grows. [Relative fraction and image certificate](Erdos478ShortFreeWindows.lean) · [Ordinary dyadic specialization](Erdos478NaturalFirstCollisions.lean.in)

The estimate holds in proportional windows $H(K)/K\to L>1$. For a threshold $bK/\log K$ and $0<z\le1$, the admissible rates satisfy $A<b\log z-(L-1)\log((1+z)/2)/3$. Every new eligible length contributes a factor at most $(1+z)/2$ to the weighted generating function for its hit count. The mass of the earlier event stays as a multiplicative factor throughout the induction, so division by that mass preserves the rate. The double limit permits $X$ to depend on $K$. Erdős 478 asks for convergence of $|A_p|/p$ to $1-e^{-1}$ along the prime filter. That convergence remains open.
