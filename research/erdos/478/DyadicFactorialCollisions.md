# Near-linear factorial collision counts in dyadic prime-gap bands

For every real $C,\varepsilon>0$, there are arbitrarily large integers $j$ with the following property. Put $K=2^j$ and $D=\lceil CK/j^3\rceil$. For all sufficiently large $X$, more than a $1-\varepsilon$ proportion of primes $p\le X$ have **at least $D$ distinct upper endpoints** $a$ satisfying
$$
1\le a-\ell<a<p,\qquad (a-\ell)!\equiv a!\pmod p,\qquad
K\le\ell<2K,\quad \ell\text{ prime},\quad \ell\equiv11,17\pmod{21}.
$$
For these primes, $|A_p|+D\le p-1$, where $A_p=\{n!\bmod p:1\le n<p\}$. For example, $C=1$ and $\varepsilon=1/100$ give a proportion greater than 99%. The cutoff for $X$ depends on the band. DeepMind asks whether $|A_p|/p$ tends to $1-1/e$ along all primes. That limit remains open. The earlier band theorem applies at every sufficiently large cutoff and gives $\lceil K^\alpha\rceil$ endpoints with proportion greater than $1-\alpha-\varepsilon$. Here the cutoff runs through a subsequence, the gap interval has fixed multiplicative width, and the endpoint bound is of order $K/(\log K)^3$ with arbitrarily small exceptional proportion. The construction selects the bands by their number of usable prime lengths. ## Why the Galois group matters

Write $F_\ell(X)=X(X-1)\cdots(X-\ell+1)-1$. For $\ell=2h+1$, its reduction modulo two is $X(X(X+1))^h-1$. The polynomial $X^3+X^2+1$ divides it for $\ell\equiv3\pmod{14}$, and $X^3+X+1$ divides it for $\ell\equiv11\pmod{14}$. Both cubics are irreducible. If a number field $L$ splits $F_\ell$, monicity lets the factorization descend to its ring of integers. Every prime above two has residue degree divisible by three, so $3\mid[L:\mathbb Q]$ by the ramification–residue degree identity. [Cubic factors](Erdos478BinaryCubicGap.lean) · [Number-field divisibility](Erdos478CubicSplittingDegree.lean)

For prime $\ell\equiv11,17\pmod{21}$, the irreducible polynomial $F_\ell$ has prime degree and $3\nmid\ell(\ell-1)$. Its Galois group acts faithfully on the roots. Its order is divisible by both $3$ and $\ell$, so Burnside's prime-degree theorem makes the action doubly transitive. The finite-field proof follows Peter Müller's polynomial approach. It uses the first nonzero power sum of an invariant set of differences to force affine linearity in the non-doubly-transitive case. ## From two rational factors to endpoint variance

Let $R_\ell(p)$ count the roots of $F_\ell$ in $\mathbb F_p$. An integer $M$ separates the ordered root pairs by $(x,y)\mapsto x+My$. The diagonal and the off-diagonal are the two Galois orbits. Their minimal polynomials give exactly two irreducible rational factors of the separating polynomial. At primes of good reduction its number of roots equals $R_\ell(p)^2$. The prime ideal theorem thus gives limiting first and second moments $1$ and $2$ over the observation primes. Coprime degrees give mixed moment $1$ for two different prime lengths. This argument uses degree-one prime ideal counts. [Two orbit factors](Erdos478TwoOrbitFactors.lean) · [Pair polynomial](Erdos478PairWordSpectrum.lean) · [Second moment](Erdos478CubicGapSecondMoment.lean) · [Mixed moments](Erdos478CoprimeRootMoments.lean)

For a finite set $S$ of selected lengths let $m=|S|$ and $E_S(p)$ count distinct upper endpoints over all its lengths. Above a finite cutoff the endpoints are pairwise disjoint across lengths, and $E_S(p)=\sum_{\ell\in S}R_\ell(p)$. The prime mean of $(E_S(p)-m)^2$ tends to $m$. For every $t,\eta>0$, the proportion with $|E_S(p)-m|\ge t$ is eventually less than $m/t^2+\eta$. [Endpoint union](Erdos478DistinctBandEndpoints.lean) · [Exact variance and finite tail](Erdos478LocalizedEndpointVariance.lean)

Mathlib proves that $\sum_{\ell\equiv11\ (21),\ \ell\text{ prime}}\log(\ell)/\ell$ diverges. If every sufficiently high dyadic band had at most $C2^j/j^3$ selected primes, its contribution would be $O(C/j^2)$, a summable bound. Hence arbitrarily high bands have more than $2C2^j/j^3$ selected lengths. Applying the finite tail bound with $t=m/2$ proves the stated frequency. Klurman and Munsch used roots of factorial-gap polynomials to bound missing factorial residues averaged over primes. The dyadic theorem here concerns a localized count of distinct repeated indices.
