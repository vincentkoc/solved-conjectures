# Twice the leading constant for missing nonzero factorial residues

Let $A_p=\{j!\bmod p:1\le j<p\}$, $F_p=p-1-|A_p|$, and $T=\log\log p$. Infinitely many primes satisfy
$$
  F_p\log T\ \ge\ 2T .
$$
For every $\varepsilon>0$, arbitrarily large primes satisfy the stronger inequality
$$
  2T\ \le\ F_p\bigl(\log T-\log\log T+\log 32+\varepsilon\bigr).
$$
The Lean theorem uses the factorial image and the prime filter of Erdős 478 directly. Its conclusion concerns a subsequence. The conjectured limit $|A_p|/p\to 1-1/e$ along all primes remains open. ## Every root at every even length

For each $n\ge1$, some prime
$$
 p\le 2^{(2n+4)^{n(n+1)+34}}
$$
splits the product of the falling-factorial gap polynomials $f_{2i}(X)=X(X-1)\cdots(X-2i+1)-1$, $1\le i\le n$, into distinct linear factors. Each of the $n(n+1)$ roots gives a different upper endpoint of a factorial collision. In particular, $|A_p|+n(n+1)\le p-1$. The construction allows any finite nonempty set of distinct positive even lengths and can exclude the prime divisors of any given positive integer. [Finite prime bound](Erdos478CompleteEvenCeiling.lean) · [Arbitrary even lengths and exclusions](Erdos478EffectiveEvenComplete.lean) · [Endpoint counting](Erdos478DisjointGapFamilies.lean)

The roots of $f_{2i}$ occur in pairs $a,2i-1-a$. Choose one root per pair over the complex numbers. There are $N=n(n+1)/2$ coordinates, and the polynomial encoding all possible selections has degree $D=\prod_{i=1}^n(2i)^i$. A separating integer weight gives each possible tuple a distinct weighted sum. Use the **minimal polynomial of the sum for the chosen tuple** in the finite Schur sieve. This restricts the modular root to the Galois orbit of the chosen tuple and preserves the degree and complex root bounds. [Reflection pairs](Erdos478ChosenGapPairs.lean) · [Bounded prime for the selected tuple](Erdos478ChosenWordPrime.lean)

A modular root gives an evaluation on the order generated by the chosen sum. Lying over extends the evaluation's kernel to a maximal ideal of the algebraic integers. In the residue field, Frobenius fixes the sum. Separability of the full word polynomial forces it to fix every selected coordinate. Each reflected partner is an integer minus its representative, so every gap polynomial splits in the prime field. Avoiding the discriminant of the product keeps all endpoints distinct across lengths. [Specialization](Erdos478ChosenSpecialization.lean) · [Frobenius argument](Erdos478ChosenWordReduction.lean) · [Complete splitting](Erdos478PairedSplittingPrime.lean)

The sieved Schur bound is quadratic in $D$ in the exponent, up to a polynomial in $n$. Since $D\le(2n+4)^N$, the finite ceiling gives $T\le(n(n+1)+34)\log(2n+4)$. Its inversion yields the displayed deficit bound. The choice of $32$ makes the elementary inequality $(2n+4)^2\le32N+16$ valid at every $n$. [Degree and height bounds](Erdos478CompleteEvenCeiling.lean) · [Logarithmic inversion](Erdos478DoubledFactorialDeficit.lean)

## Comparison

Banks, Luca, Shparlinski and Stichtenoth proved the individual-prime lower bound with leading constant one by splitting a single gap polynomial. Klurman and Munsch proved an unconditional prime-mean estimate with an implicit constant in Theorem 2.1 and a stronger GRH estimate in Theorem 2.2. The notebook previously reached coefficient one and counted witnesses through every sufficiently large cutoff at the earlier threshold. The present theorem proves coefficient two together with complete splitting at all the even lengths through one common cutoff.
