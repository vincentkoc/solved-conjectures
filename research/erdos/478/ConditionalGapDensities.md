# Distinct factorial collision lengths and their conditional prime densities

Let $A_p=\{n!\bmod p:1\le n<p\}$ and let $U_p(S)$ consist of those lengths $\ell\in S$ for which two indices in $[1,p)$, differing by $\ell$, have the same factorial residue. An eligible length is a prime $\ell>3$ congruent to 3 or 4 modulo 7. In the dyadic band $K<\ell\le2K$, at least $K/(9\log K)$ **distinct lengths** occur, and $|A_p|+|U_p(S)|\le p-1$, on a prime fraction greater than $1-\exp(-AK/\log K)$ for every $A<\log(32/27)/9$. The quantifiers are all sufficiently large $K$, then all sufficiently large prime-counting cutoffs $X$ depending on $K$. The fraction uses all rational primes $p\le X$. The earlier endpoint bound counts upper indices, allowing several at one length. The statistic here counts each length once. ## Conditioning on earlier counts

Fix an eligible $\ell$ and any finite set $S$ of smaller prime lengths. For each $i\in S$, count the positive upper indices $j<p$ with $j-i\ge1$ and $(j-i)!\equiv j!\pmod p$. An event $E$ can be any predicate on this whole vector of exact counts. Both the ordinary density $d$ of $E$ and the ordinary density $e$ of $E$ together with a collision at length $\ell$ exist. They satisfy $e\ge d/2$, including when $d=0$. The smaller primes need satisfy only $i<\ell$. More precisely, for any real weight determined by the earlier counts, with mean $m$, multiplying by the new count gives mean $m$ and multiplying by its square gives mean $2m$. [Endpoint moments](Erdos478ConditionalCollisionLaw.lean) · [Ordinary conditional densities](Erdos478NaturalConditionalHits.lean.in)

For a finite set $S$ of $m$ eligible lengths and any increasing family $\mathcal E$ of subsets of $S$, the exact natural density of $U_p(S)\in\mathcal E$ is at least $|\mathcal E|/2^m$. A decreasing family has the reverse inequality. In particular, the density of at least $t$ hit lengths is bounded below by $2^{-m}\sum_{j=t}^m\binom mj$. Every length hits with density at least $2^{-m}$, and the density of at most $\lfloor m/3\rfloor$ hits is at most $(27/32)^{\lfloor m/3\rfloor}$. The estimates permit dependence between different lengths. ## Why the conditional bound holds

For a finite family of monic, positive-degree integral polynomials separable over $\mathbb Q$, take a common finite normal splitting field. Every statistic of their joint root counts over $\mathbb F_p$ has prime mean equal to the uniform mean of the corresponding fixed-point counts in its Galois group. This fixed-point consequence of classical Frobenius density is checked here from degree-one prime ideal counts. Burnside’s lemma identifies a mixed fixed-point moment with the orbit count of tuples. A separating word identifies the same number with a rational factor count. Interpolation on the finite range of each root count gives the law for every statistic, including exact events. The proof handles intersecting splitting fields and repeated polynomials. [Fixed-point moments](Erdos478GaloisFixedMoments.lean) · [Interpolated joint law](Erdos478GaloisCountLaw.lean) · [Arbitrary statistics](Erdos478GaloisObservables.lean) · [Earlier rational interpolation](ExactCollisionCounts.md)

For the new length, the subgroup fixing every earlier root acts transitively both on equal ordered pairs and on distinct ordered pairs of roots. Averaging on each of its cosets gives first moment one and second moment two. If $R$ is the new fixed-point count, $1_{R>0}\ge R-R^2/4$. Every earlier test is constant on each coset, so the same inequality retains at least half of its mass after requiring a hit. Inductively expose the lengths in increasing order. For an increasing score, the increase from adding the new length is nonnegative and depends only on earlier observations, so the half bound applies at each step.
