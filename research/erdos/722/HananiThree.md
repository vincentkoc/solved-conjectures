# Three-column Steiner quadruple systems

Given a Steiner quadruple system of order $v=6d+4$, the two-point-stem construction produces one of order $3v-4=18d+8$. Given one of order $v=6d+8$, the four-point-stem construction produces one of order $3v-8=18d+16$. Both theorems hold for every $d\ge0$. Each hypothesis and conclusion uses the upstream predicate `H.IsBlockDesign 3 4` on the corresponding finite vertex type. Hanani proved the classical quadruple-system spectrum. The Sage routines for these two recurrences use the input classes $10$ and $2$ modulo $12$, respectively. The construction below treats both input parities of $d$. ## Completing triples

Take three copies of the source system sharing a stem of size $h$, where $h=2$ or $4$. For $h=4$ choose a block as the stem, so the copies agree on triples lying in it. Each ordinary column has cyclic labels in $A=\mathbb Z/(6d+h)\mathbb Z$. Reserve the first $h$ sums for the stem and give each column a consecutive interval of $2d$ sums. For a triple in three different columns, its label sum selects a stem point or one of the intervals. In the latter case, reflect the sum about the midpoint of the interval and add the resulting displacement to the label in that column. Each directed displacement $\pm1,\pm3,\ldots,\pm(2d-1)$ occurs exactly once in the interval. The reverse displacement recovers the original sum. [Sum intervals](Erdos722HananiSlots.lean)

Two ordinary points in one column and a third in another use that same rule backwards when their displacement is short. The other displacements require matchings. Split $A$ into its two parity layers and reserve the odd shift $b=2d+1$. Identify the odd layer with the even one by subtracting $b$. For each even-layer coordinate $c$, reflect $x$ to $c-x$ within its layer. At a fixed point, switch to its partner in the other layer along the reserved shift. Two distinct points in the same layer have unique color $x+y$, and the edge switching layers at $x$ has unique color $2x$. This holds for even and odd layer sizes. Every remaining odd shift, oriented from the even layer to the odd one, supplies a matching. The identity handles a repeated input point. [Matching decomposition](Erdos722HananiMatchings.lean)

These rules commute with all permutations of a triple. Fixing two inputs gives an involution on the third, and repeating an input returns the other point. The closed four-sets therefore contain each three-set exactly once. Within one augmented column the completion agrees with the input system.
