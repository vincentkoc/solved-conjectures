# The complete rank-two specialization of Erdős 722

For every fixed $k\ge2$, all sufficiently large orders $n$ admit a Steiner system $S(2,k,n)$ exactly when
$$
 k-1\mid n-1,\qquad \binom{k}{2}\mid\binom{n}{2}.
$$
The threshold may depend on $k$. For $k>2$, `Erdos722.PairDesign.eventually_pair_design k hk` has the same type as the pinned statement specialized at $r=2$. The converse is included in `pair_design_spectrum`. Both use the host's finite family of subsets of `Fin n` and its `IsBlockDesign` predicate. The equivalence also permits $k=2$. Higher ranks remain outside this proof. This formalizes Wilson's classical pair-design theorem. The earlier notebook covered prime-power block sizes, prime-power orders in some cases, and a separate six-point spectrum. For example, $k=10$ and $n=630t+406$ satisfy both tests for every natural $t$. Here $n$ is divisible by both two and seven, and $(n-1)/9=70t+45$ represents the mixed residue $5\pmod{10}$. The theorem supplies ordinary ten-point blocks for every sufficiently large $t$. ## Signed weights and unfolding

For $v\ge k+2$, consider the free abelian group on the pairs of a $v$-set, modulo the sums of pairs in its $k$-subsets. Four $k$-subsets with a common $(k-2)$-point filler give a relation on four distinct vertices. It forces every pair class to have the form $x_i+x_j+c$, with $x_a=0$ at a chosen anchor. Exchanging one vertex in a $k$-subset gives $(k-1)x_i=0$, and summing over a block gives $\binom{k}{2}c=0$. The two divisibility tests at index $\lambda$ therefore place the desired pair vector in the integral image of the block incidence map. Applying this to finitely many residues of $\lambda$ and adding a constant to every block weight gives nonnegative multiplicities at every sufficiently large admissible index. [Pair relations](Erdos722IntegralPairRelations.lean) · [Large index](Erdos722LargePairIndex.lean) · [Graver and Jurkat, *The module structure of integral designs*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(73)90037-X)

Brouwer's exposition of Wilson's construction unfolds a design of large prime index $q$ to index one. Use one field coordinate per unordered pair of starting points. Number the $q$ block copies through that pair by $\mathbf F_q$. The two slopes at its endpoints agree in this one coordinate and differ in every other coordinate. Prescribing two lifted points selects the block copy in the common coordinate. Elsewhere it determines each entry of a parameter vector. The zero-sum condition recovers its remaining entry and the first lifted point then determines the translation. This proves unique coverage between fibers. Filling each fiber with a cyclotomic design gives an ordinary pair design on $v q^{\binom v2}$ points. Choosing $q\equiv1\pmod{Mk(k-1)}$ preserves $v$ modulo any prescribed $M>0$. ## Covering all large orders

Write $n=(k-1)u+1$. The remaining test becomes $k\mid ((k-1)u+1)u$. It is preserved by congruence modulo $k$, including when $k$ has several distinct prime factors. Given a width $s$ divisible by $k$, lift each admissible class modulo $s$ by applying the preceding construction at modulus $(k-1)s$. This supplies a design representative for every class of group counts needed below. [Admissible residues](Erdos722AdmissibleResidues.lean)

Take a large prime $q$ congruent to one modulo a chosen multiple of $k(k-1)$. A prime-order design at $q$ supplies both group counts $s=k(q-1)/(k-1)$ and $s+1$. For the first, fill $k$ columns of height $q-1$ using the same extra point. For the second, fill $k$ columns of height $q$ independently. Varying the prime gives arbitrarily large adjacent usable heights. A single width translates all the residue representatives into an interval of any prescribed finite length. Choosing a second width $s'$ divisible by $s+1$ makes $s+1$ and $s'+1$ coprime. Dilation by these two bases covers a tail.
