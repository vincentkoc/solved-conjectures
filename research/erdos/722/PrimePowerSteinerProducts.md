# Prime-power products of Steiner 3-designs

For every prime power $q$ and all natural numbers $a,b$, a Steiner system $S(3,q+1,a+1)$ and a Steiner system $S(3,q+1,b+1)$ give an $S(3,q+1,ab+1)$. The three design predicates in `Erdos722.PointedProduct.PrimePower.product` are the pinned `Finset.IsBlockDesign` on the indicated finite ordinals. This formalizes Wilson’s product theorem. Mohácsy and Ray-Chaudhuri gave an independent proof in Theorem 1.3 of *Candelabra systems and designs*. [Lean theorem](Erdos722PrimePowerProducts.lean) · [Mohácsy and Ray-Chaudhuri](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-3758(02)00226-4) · [Balachandran, Theorem 1](https://www.math.iitb.ac.in/~niranj/papers/newcandelabras.pdf)

For block size six, the whole six-point block and the 22-point Witt design give an ordinary design at **every order $1+5^u21^v$**, for all $u,v\ge0$. Taking $u=v=1$ gives order 106, and $u=0,v=2$ gives order 442. Every order with $v>0$ lies outside the subline sequence $1+5^d$, since its punctured order is divisible by seven. The 22-point seed derives twice from danielpuri1901’s checked 24-point witness. [Six-point-block family](Erdos722WittPointedProducts.lean.in) · [Checked derivation](Erdos722WittMinimumOrders.lean.in) · [Pinned Witt witness](https://github.com/danielpuri1901/erdos-lean-formalization/blob/6bd7a2cd9d9a50ba92c3d3b3ae1a2d64e241f4bd/Solution/Witt/Main.lean)

## Local construction

Puncture each design at one point. Take the Cartesian product of the remaining points and add a single point at infinity. For a triple, discard infinity and inspect each coordinate projection. One value selects a point. Two values select the block through both and infinity. Three values select their unique block. Write *line* for a block through infinity and *circle* for a block avoiding infinity. Each triple selects a unique pair of faces. On a line face the triple must use at least two finite values, and on a circle it must use three. After relabelling the field coordinates, the local blocks glue by this partition. [Pointed gluing theorem](Erdos722PointedDesignProduct.lean) · [Coordinate transport and transposition](Erdos722RectangleCovers.lean)

| Faces | Blocks and unique coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Point with any face | Two points support no triple. A line paired with a point uses one whole block including infinity. A circle paired with a point uses its finite vertices. [Singleton covers](Erdos722SingletonFaceCovers.lean) |
| Line with line | Label the quadratic extension by two coordinates over the small field. Every row and column, augmented by infinity, is a projective image of the small field line. Delete these blocks from the inversive plane. Every triple with a constant finite projection was in one of the deleted blocks. [Affine images](Erdos722InversiveSquareTiles.lean) · [Deletion lemma](Erdos722CoordinateStripeDeletion.lean) |
| Circle with line | Use graphs of the nonconstant words $P(x)/h(x)$, where $h$ is a fixed monic root-free quadratic and $P$ has degree at most two. At infinity take the quadratic coefficient of $P$. Three finite values determine $P$ by the root bound. If infinity is prescribed, the top coefficient is fixed and two finite values suffice. The code contains every constant, so a nonconstant word cannot agree with a constant at three coordinates. [Interpolation over every finite field](Erdos722ProjectiveQuadraticWords.lean) · [Covering graphs](Erdos722QuadraticCircleLine.lean) |
| Circle with circle | Take graphs of projective linear transformations. Each ordered frame maps to any prescribed ordered frame. A transformation fixing infinity, zero and one is scalar and acts identically. Thus exactly one graph contains each compatible triple. [Frame and graph proof](Erdos722ProjectiveCircleGraphs.lean) · [Graph extension criterion](Erdos722FieldGraphCovers.lean) |

The construction includes $q=2$, where block size equals rank three. DeepMind assumes $r<k$, so the host specialization applies for $q>2$. Zero punctured order gives order one, and exponent zero in both seeds gives the empty design on two points. For each fixed $q$ the product requires two input designs. The full host theorem asks for a design at every sufficiently large order passing the binomial divisibilities. Keevash proved that general existence theorem. [Pinned statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/722.lean) · [Keevash](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.3665)
