# Complementary Steiner extensions and the least orders at ranks four and five

For every positive $k$, a Steiner system $S(k-1,k,2k+1)$ exists if and only if $S(k,k+1,2k+2)$ exists. Here $S(r,k,v)$ denotes a family of $k$-subsets in which every $r$-subset lies in exactly one block. For each labelled input system, adjoining a fixed new point gives exactly one extension with that derived family. The equivalence uses the host’s `Finset.IsBlockDesign` on `Fin (2*k+1)` and `Fin (2*k+2)`. Both ordinary conclusions formalize Mendelsohn’s Theorem 1. [Ordinary existence and uniqueness](Erdos722MendelsohnCriterion.lean) · [Mendelsohn, 1970](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1970-117-0) · [Pinned Erdős 722](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/722.lean)

## The extension on signed weights

Let $|V|=2k+1$ and $o\notin V$. Assign an integer weight $w(B)$ to each $k$-subset. Assume each $r$-subset lies in blocks of total weight $\lambda$, where $r\le k$ is even. Replace each $B$ by both $B\cup\{o\}$ and $V\setminus B$, with weight $w(B)$ on each. Every $(r+1)$-subset now lies in blocks of total weight $\lambda$. Deleting $o$ from blocks through it gives the inverse on complement-symmetric weights. The maps form a Lean `Equiv` on functions defined on the actual block layer, for every integer $\lambda$, including zero and negative indices. This is the integer-weight version of Alltop’s even-strength construction. [Extension formula](Erdos722AlltopSignedExtension.lean) · [Weight equivalences](Erdos722AlltopWeightEquivalence.lean) · [Alltop, 1975](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(75)90006-0)

For the proof, inclusion–exclusion expresses the weight of blocks avoiding $A$ as an alternating sum of incidence rows on subsets of $A$. Multiply the original weights by $\binom{|V|-r}{k-r}$ and subtract $\lambda$ from each weight. All centred rows of size at most $r$ vanish. On an $(r+1)$-set the surviving sign is negative, so the centred incidence and avoidance rows cancel. At odd $k$ and strength $k-1$, the analogous identity on the extended space forces complementary blocks to have equal weights. Thus every signed extension is captured. For an ordinary input with positive even $k$, the divisibility test at $i=k-2$ would require $2\mid k+3$, which excludes that case. ## Arithmetic and the first orders

For $v\ge r$, the host’s divisibilities at block size $r+1$ are equivalent to $\gcd(v-r,(r+1)!)=1$. For necessity, a prime $p\le r+1$ dividing $v-r$ makes the numerator at $i=r-(p-1)$ congruent to one modulo $p$. For sufficiency, put $j=r-i$ and use $(j+1)\binom{v-i}{j+1}=(v-r)\binom{v-i}{j}$, cancelling the coprime factor $v-r$. At $v=2(r+1)$ the tests are equivalent to primality of $r+2$. Yoshizawa’s Lemma 1 gives the prime-divisor obstruction for an ordinary system. The notebook’s signed incidence theorem supplies existence of integer weights of index one whenever $v\ge r+1$ and the tests hold. [Exact binomial criterion](Erdos722UnitExcessArithmetic.lean) · [Signed incidence theorem](Erdos722SteinerWeights.lean) · [Yoshizawa, 1981](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1981-109-x)

Let $p$ be the least prime strictly larger than $r+1$. The first signed order $v>r+1$ at block size $r+1$ equals $r+p$. This also bounds the order of any ordinary system from below when $0<r<k<v$, even with $k$ free. For $k\ge r+2$, Tits’s bound gives $v\ge(r+1)(k-r+1)$, and Bertrand’s postulate suffices. To see Tits’s bound, take an uncovered $(r+1)$-set. The blocks through its $r$-faces have pairwise disjoint exterior parts, each of size $k-r$. Counting these parts and the original points gives the inequality. [Rank bound and least orders](Erdos722FirstPrimeRankOrders.lean) · [Tits bound](Erdos722TitsRankOrder.lean) · [Tits, 1964, publication 59](https://abelprize.no/sites/default/files/2021-09/The%20Abel%20Prize%202008-2012_Full_.pdf)

## The two Witt parameter triples through twelve points

For $4\le r<k<v\le12$, ordinary existence is equivalent to $(r,k,v)=(4,5,11)$ or $(5,6,12)$. The minimum orders across all nontrivial block sizes at strengths four and five are therefore 11 and 12. This counts parameter triples. The uniqueness result fixes the derived labelled design at a distinguished point. The underlying Witt systems and their extension are classical. [Finite classification](Erdos722FirstPrimeRankOrders.lean) · [Witt](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02948948) · [Havlicek and Lenz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0096)
