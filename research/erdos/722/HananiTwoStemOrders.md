# Hanani’s two-stem construction for six-point blocks

Every ordinary Steiner system $S(3,6,v+1)$ gives an $S(3,6,4v+2)$. Both systems use DeepMind’s `Finset.IsBlockDesign`, and the theorem quantifies over every natural $v$. This is Hanani’s classical construction. The formal proof builds the blocks and verifies unique coverage of every triple. [Unconditional theorem](Erdos722WittTwoStemOrders.lean.in) · [Hanani, *A class of three-designs*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165%2879%2990050-5) · [Balachandran, Theorem 3](https://www.math.iitb.ac.in/~niranj/papers/newcandelabras.pdf)

Starting from one six-point block, iteration gives order $(4^{d+2}+2)/3$ for every $d\ge0$. The first four orders are 6, 22, 86 and 342. Wilson’s product multiplies punctured orders. Combining it with the preceding construction yields an ordinary six-point-block design at every order
$$
1+5^u21^v85^w341^z,\qquad u,v,w,z\ge0.
$$
In particular there are infinitely many orders outside the earlier family $1+5^u21^v$. Every punctured order $85^{d+1}$ is divisible by 17, whereas $5^u21^v$ has no such factor. The theorem `witt_infinite_extension` proves this set is infinite in Lean. [Order formula and separation](Erdos722HananiSpectrum.lean) · [Witt specialization](Erdos722WittTwoStemOrders.lean.in) · [Earlier product family](PrimePowerSteinerProducts.md)

## How the blocks fit

Remove one point from the master design. Replace each remaining point by four points and add two common stem points. Every triple meets at least one of the four-point groups. If it meets one group, its unique block is that group together with the stems. Two distinct group labels determine the unique master block through them and the removed point. Three distinct labels determine their unique master block. The following two fillings supply the required triples in these master blocks. [Trace, face selection and gluing](Erdos722TwoStemGeometry.lean)

- **Master blocks through the removed point.** Fix two points in the 22-point Witt design. Its blocks containing the pair partition the other 20 points into five groups of four after the pair is erased. Delete those five blocks. Each triple meeting at least two groups still lies in its original unique block. This constructs the required candelabra system on five groups with a two-point stem. The seed comes from deriving twice at points in danielpuri1901’s 24-point Witt witness. [General pencil deletion](Erdos722TwoStemPencil.lean) · [Witt derivations](Erdos722WittMinimumOrders.lean.in) · [Pinned witness](https://github.com/danielpuri1901/erdos-lean-formalization/blob/6bd7a2cd9d9a50ba92c3d3b3ae1a2d64e241f4bd/Solution/Witt/Main.lean)
- **Master blocks avoiding the removed point.** Over the four-element field take the four values of $a+bx+cx^2$, together with the two coordinates $b$ and $c$. Any three coordinates determine the word. In particular, for $b$ and two finite evaluations, injectivity of squaring distinguishes the two evaluation points. The word graphs give six-point blocks, each meeting the six groups once. The proof works over every finite field of characteristic two. [Hyperoval interpolation](Erdos722HyperovalWords.lean) · [Word graphs and singleton filling](Erdos722TwoStemTransversals.lean)

For a finite field of characteristic two and size $m$, `even_field_inflation` uses a seed $S(3,m+2,m(m+1)+2)$ and a master $S(3,m+2,v+1)$ to produce $S(3,m+2,mv+2)$. The seed is an explicit hypothesis. For $m=2^e$ with $e>0$, `even_seed_iff` proves that it exists **exactly when $e=1$ or $e=2$**. The notebook’s classification at the exterior-point lower bound restricts $m$ to 2, 4 or 10, and 10 is not a power of two. The affine eight-point design supplies the first case and the Witt witness the second. [Exact seed classification](Erdos722WittTwoStemOrders.lean.in) · [Exterior-point equality cases](Erdos722ExtremalParameters.lean)

At $v=0$ the conclusion has two points and no triples. At $v=1$ the conclusion is the single six-point block. DeepMind’s Erdős 722 asks for every sufficiently large admissible order at each $r<k$ with $r\ge1$. The result here gives the specified orders at $(r,k)=(3,6)$.
