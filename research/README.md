# DeepMind proof notebook

These Lean files develop partial results for six problems in [Formal Conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/tree/f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb). Codex assisted with the proofs. Each file prints its theorem’s axioms. The notes link the original question, prior work and the proof.

| Problem | Kernel-checked result | What remains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Green 33](green/NOTES.md) | Every basis satisfies [`2q ≤ k² + 2`](green/REFLECTION-RIGIDITY.md), with exact reflection symmetry at equality. A pointwise cap of `m + 4` implies `(2m + 2)q + 6 ≤ m k² + (m + 8)k`. Along every family with `q → ∞` and `k/√q → √2`, the [maximum ordered count tends to infinity](green/DIVERGENCE.md). All [nontrivial character sums](green/FOURIER-STABILITY.md) are then `o(√q)`. Residues above any fixed cutoff carry [at least `(2 − o(1))q` of the energy](green/TAIL-ENERGY.md). | Existence of a family attaining this density remains open. |
| [Green 33 · order-three quotient](green/ORDER-THREE.md) | If `3 ∣ q`, equality in `2q ≤ k² + 2` forces `4k − 7` to be a square. This raises the size bound by one at every `q = 2(15n + 5)² + 1`, with exact minimum 11 at `q = 51`. | The obstruction concerns exact finite equality. |
| [Green 33 · explicit cyclic bases](green/BEL-CONSTRUCTION.md) | The Bevan–Erskine–Lewis construction gives a basis of size `4q` modulo `6q(q − 2)` for every `q = 6n + 7`, attaining the limiting constant `√(8/3)`. | Green asks for the smaller constant `√2`. |
| [Green 33 · lifting a strict sum cover](green/STRICT-COVER-LIFT.md) | Twelve progressions give cyclic two-bases of size `12(Q+27720)` modulo `56Q²`, with limiting ratio `√(18/7)`. | Reaching `√2` remains open. |
| [Green 33 · distinct summands](green/STRICT-REDUCTION.md) | Green’s conjecture is equivalent to the infimum of `k/√N` over all finite cyclic strict sum covers being `√2`. An ordinary seed of size `k` gives a strict lift with normalized overhead at most `(2⌊√k⌋+3)/√N` relative to the seed modulus `N`. | The value of the infimum remains open. |
| [Green 33 · prime-order obstruction](green/PRIME-MOMENTS.md) | At prime order `p = 2m² + 1`, an extremal basis of size `2m` with `m > 1` forces `4ⁱ ≡ −m (mod p)` for some `i < m`. Equality is impossible for `m ≡ 1 (mod 4)`, giving at least 19 elements modulo 163. | The obstruction concerns exact finite equality. |
| [Green 33 · higher moments](green/NEWTON-OBSTRUCTION.md) | At prime-order equality, the basis size belongs to the additive submonoid generated by the resonant moment degrees. Modulo `173203273`, every basis has at least `18613` elements, although `18612` passes the first moment test. | The exact minimum at this modulus is unknown here. |
| [Green 33 · stability under deletion](green/HEREDITARY-TAIL.md) | In any family at the proposed density, every subset of size at least `α√q`, for fixed `α > 1`, has diverging maximum ordered multiplicity. Its own energy above a fixed cutoff is at least `(2α² − 2 − o(1))q`, uniformly in the subset. | Existence of the ambient family remains open. |
| [Green 33 · small self-sumsets](green/PIECE-OBSTRUCTION.md) | If a basis family is covered by a fixed number `r ≥ 2` of pieces with total self-sumset size `o(q)`, its limiting normalized size is at least `√(2r/(r−1))`. The four-progression bound is sharp. | Bases without such covers remain eligible for Green’s constant. |
| [Cunningham chains](arxiv/NOTES.md) | Dickson’s prime-tuples conjecture implies infinitely many first-kind chains of every positive **exact** length. The proof forces the next term to be composite. | Dickson’s conjecture is an explicit hypothesis. The unconditional first-kind statement remains open. |
| [First-kind chains in residue classes](arxiv/1104.1579/FirstKindProgressions.md) | Under Dickson, a class contains infinitely many exact length-`k` seeds iff each of its first `k` terms is coprime to the modulus. A failed test leaves at most one seed unconditionally. | Infinitude in admissible classes remains conditional. |
| [Second-kind Cunningham chains](arxiv/1104.1579/CunninghamSecondKindDickson.md) | Under Dickson, infinitely many seeds have each positive exact length for the recurrence `p ↦ 2p − 1`. | Unconditional infinitude remains open. |
| [Unbounded Cunningham prefixes](arxiv/1104.1579/UnboundedPrefixes.md) | For each kind, Jones’s full exact-length infinitude statement is equivalent to the existence of one prime prefix of each length. The equivalence is unconditional. | Existence of arbitrarily long prime prefixes remains open. |
| [Fixed Cunningham lengths and Sophie Germain primes](arxiv/1104.1579/FixedLengthPrefixes.md) | At each fixed positive length and for either kind, infinitely many prime prefixes are equivalent to infinitely many exact chains. The first-kind length-two case is equivalent to Sophie Germain prime infinitude. | Infinitude at length two remains open. |
| [Cunningham chain length bound](arxiv/1104.1579/QuarterSeedBound.md) | For both kinds, an exact length `k` from seed `p` satisfies `4k < p` with exactly seven exceptions, all verified. | Infinitude at fixed length remains open. |
| [Cunningham chains under Artin’s conjecture](arxiv/1104.1579/ArtinLengthBound.md) | Positive relative density of base-two primitive-root primes implies `k ≤ C log p` for exact chains of either kind. The product of these primes up to `k < p` divides the shifted seed unconditionally. | The density assumption and both Jones infinitude conjectures remain open. |
| [Erdős 722](erdos/NOTES.md) | Binary quadruples give orders `2^d`, and [harmonic quadruples](erdos/HarmonicQuadruples.md) give `3^d+1`. Quadruple-system orders are [closed under products](erdos/QuadrupleProduct.md), giving every `10^d` with positive `d`. [Tripling](erdos/QuadrupleTripling.md) gives orders `7·3^d+1`, including arbitrarily large orders outside the multiplicative closure of the binary and ternary families. [Projective sublines](erdos/ProjectiveSublines.md) give Steiner 3-designs on `q^d+1` points with block size `q+1` for every prime power `q` and positive `d`. | The general theorem covers all sufficiently large admissible orders for every pair of block and subset sizes. |
| [Erdős 478](erdos/478/NOTES.md) | For every odd prime, the number `a` of distinct factorial residues satisfies [`4p ≤ 3a(a+1)+2`](erdos/478/InverseParity.md). For `p > 5` with 5 a square, at least four residues are missing. [Mixed gaps](erdos/478/MixedGaps.md) give five for arbitrarily large such primes, and the [number missing tends to infinity](erdos/478/UnboundedDeficit.md) along a subsequence. For every positive modulus and every finite set of gap lengths at least two, [their degrees add to a deficit bound](erdos/478/DisjointGapFamilies.md) at arbitrarily large primes congruent to one modulo that modulus. | The conjecture asks for a limiting proportion along all primes. |
| [Theta sum](arxiv/theta-sum/NOTES.md) | The [mod-eight](arxiv/theta-sum/GAUSS-FACTORIAL.md), [mod-sixteen](arxiv/theta-sum/MOD-SIXTEEN.md) and [prime-modulus](arxiv/theta-sum/TANGENT.md) formulas constrain zeros. The [Fourier bound](arxiv/theta-sum/FOURIER.md) proves the upper side of v3 Conjecture 3.3. [Long rows](arxiv/theta-sum/RATIONAL-ROWS.md) give signed bounds on prime progressions. The [window about one third](arxiv/theta-sum/THIRD-WINDOW.md) has opposite signs on the two prime classes modulo three. | Positivity and the lower growth inequality remain open. |
| [Lucas–Fibonacci sums](arxiv/2211.10788/NOTES.md) | In every positive-step arithmetic progression of indices, the composite terms have infinitely many distinct prime divisors between them. | The upstream question asks whether infinitely many terms are themselves prime. |
| [Lucas–Fibonacci excluded primes](arxiv/2211.10788/ExcludedPrimeDivisors.md) | Every prime congruent to 13, 17, 21, 29 or 41 modulo 44 divides no term. Each class contains infinitely many primes. | Infinitude of prime values remains open. |
| [Neighboring Lucas–Fibonacci prime pairs](arxiv/2211.10788/NeighboringPrimePairs.md) | After index 6, both neighbors are prime iff the index is 0, 6 or 18 modulo 24 and `11Fₙ² − 1` is semiprime. | Existence of another pair remains open. |
| [Conic for neighboring primes](arxiv/2211.10788/NeighboringPrimeConic.md) | For primes `p < q`, the two neighbors equal `(p,q)` at a common index iff `11(p+q)² = 49(pq+1)`. Every positive ordered integer point comes from an even index. | Existence of a third prime pair remains open. |
| [Pell criterion for Lucas–Fibonacci values](arxiv/2211.10788/PellCriterion.md) | An integer `y ≥ 0` occurs on the upper diagonal iff some natural `x` satisfies `x² − 5y² = ±44` and `x ≡ 7y (mod 22)`. | Infinitude of primes satisfying this criterion remains open. |
| [Both Lucas–Fibonacci diagonals](arxiv/2211.10788/DiagonalUnion.md) | A natural number occurs on either diagonal iff `x² − 5y² = ±44` for some natural `x`. The lower diagonal uses `x ≡ −7y (mod 22)`. The only common value is 5. | Infinitude of prime values on either diagonal remains open. |

## Replay

Use Python 3.9 or newer and a Formal Conjectures checkout at `f606af160664c5a10952e9a957b100a1cd45bbeb` with its pinned Lean 4.33.1 dependencies. Set `FC` to the path of that checkout. Run the following from this repository’s root. The individual proof notes also use `$notebook` to locate their Lean files from `$FC`.

```bash
export notebook="$PWD/research"
runner="$PWD/scripts/replay-research"
(cd "${FC:?set FC to the Formal Conjectures checkout}" && lake env python3 "$runner")
```

The runner discovers every `.lean` file under `research/`, resolves its imports with Lean, and compiles notebook dependencies first. Use a unique module filename for each proof. Each file must print its theorem’s axioms. The runner checks the reports against `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, then replays all compiled notebook modules with `leanchecker`. Compiled notebook files are temporary.

The [theta Fourier replay](arxiv/theta-sum/FOURIER.md#replay) first compiles its diagonal-module dependency.
